# ch06 — Biosociality and Shared Identity

> How medical diagnoses create new forms of social grouping and community.

When people are diagnosed with a specific condition or genetic risk, they often form new social identities known as 'biosocialities.' These groups provide a space for people to share experiences, advocate for research, and challenge medical authority. Genomic information, in particular, reshapes how families understand kinship and resemblance. While these groups can empower patients, they also make them targets for corporate marketing and genetic testing. Biosociality shows that a diagnosis is not just a biological fact but a social catalyst that changes how people relate to themselves and others.
