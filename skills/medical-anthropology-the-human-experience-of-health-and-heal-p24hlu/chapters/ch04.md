# ch04 — The Work of Care and Tinkering

> The labor involved in managing chronic conditions and adapting medical protocols.

Care is not a static set of instructions but a process of 'tinkering.' Patients and caregivers constantly adjust medical technologies and routines to fit the messiness of daily life. For a child with diabetes, a blood sugar measurement is not just a data point; it is 'work' that involves social consequences, such as missing a conversation or feeling excluded from a meal. Patients often interpret medical data based on how they feel rather than clinical targets. They might delay an insulin injection to eat with their family or avoid a measurement to escape the judgment of a 'bad' reading. Care is an active, creative effort to maintain a sense of normalcy despite chronic illness.
