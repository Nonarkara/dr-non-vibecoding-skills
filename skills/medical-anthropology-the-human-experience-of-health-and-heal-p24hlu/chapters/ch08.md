# ch08 — Global Biomedicine and Local Soil

> What happens when Western medical protocols are exported to different cultures.

Biomedicine is often treated as a universal truth, but its efficacy depends on the infrastructure and culture where it is applied. When biomedical protocols are 'dropped' into resource-poor settings without the necessary tools, medications, or administrative systems, they lose power. For example, Western ideas about 'natural birthing' may clash with local desires for high-tech interventions like IVF in Africa. Global health interventions often impose Northern social concepts that settle uneasily in other countries. Success in global health requires a reflexive dialogue that acknowledges 'local biologies' and the specific environmental and social contexts of the patient.
