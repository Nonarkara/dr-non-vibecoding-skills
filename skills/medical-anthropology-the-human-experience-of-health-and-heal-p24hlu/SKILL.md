## When to load this skill

This skill provides a framework for understanding health beyond biological pathology. It focuses on the social, cultural, and political dimensions of illness, treating patients as active agents rather than passive recipients of care. It is essential for designing health products and systems that respect local contexts, recognize silent resistance, and address the social determinants of wellbeing.

## Core principles

- Illness is the subjective experience of the patient, while disease is the clinical pathology defined by practitioners.
- Health is shaped by social, economic, and political structures, not just individual biology.
- Patients are active agents who tinker with treatments to fit medical protocols into their daily lives.
- Symptoms are often idioms of distress that communicate social or economic suffering through the body.
- Medical systems exert biopower by mandating moralized codes of behavior and defining what is normal.
- Compliance is a misnomer; what looks like non-compliance is often a logical management of life constraints.
- Technology is integrated into care through human adaptability and constant social negotiation.
- The body serves as a site of political and social resistance against injustice and bureaucracy.
