# ch04 — Deferring Revision to the Next Day

> Explains why revisiting the draft after a break yields clearer judgment and better edits, and offers a simple schedule for implementing this habit.

Revision is most effective when the writer has distance from the original draft. By waiting until the next day, you allow the brain to reset, reducing the emotional attachment that clouds judgment. After you finish a writing session and mark the natural pause, close the document and engage in a different activity—walk, exercise, or simply rest. The following morning, return with fresh eyes. At this point, you can read the draft aloud, highlight inconsistencies, and make structural changes without the interference of the original creative spark. This separation also helps you spot redundancies and weak phrasing that were invisible during the initial flow. Implement a routine: write in the evening, mark the stop point, and schedule a revision slot for the next morning. Consistency turns this practice into a reliable part of your writing workflow.
