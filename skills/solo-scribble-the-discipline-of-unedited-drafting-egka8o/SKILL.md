## When to load this skill

A practical guide for writers who want to capture raw ideas without self‑censorship. It teaches how to write alone in a continuous flow, recognize the natural pause, and defer any editing until the next day. Load this skill when you need a reliable routine for first drafts that preserves creative momentum.

## Core principles

- Write in a single, uninterrupted stream until the thought runs out of steam.
- Never edit while the ideas are still fresh; editing kills the energy you are trying to capture.
- Treat the keyboard as an extension of your mind; keep your hands on it until the narrative exhausts itself.
- Identify the natural pause in the flow and stop only when the story itself tells you to.
- Schedule revision for the following day, not in the moment, to gain perspective and avoid premature judgment.

# Patterns

## Uninterrupted Drafting

**When to use:** When you sit down to start a new piece and want to capture raw ideas.

**Recipe:**

1. Clear all distractions and set a timer for a solid block of time.
2. Begin typing immediately, ignoring any urge to edit.
3. If you feel the need to correct, place an edit marker and keep typing.

**Chapter:** ch01

---

## Pause Detection

**When to use:** During a draft when you are unsure whether to continue or stop.

**Recipe:**

1. Read the last few sentences aloud.
2. Notice if the narrative rhythm has slowed or a scene feels complete.
3. Mark the spot with "[pause]" and end the session.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## Keyboard Commitment

**When to use:** When you tend to lift your hands frequently and lose momentum.

**Recipe:**

1. Start with a five‑minute warm‑up typing exercise.
2. Set the rule: no hand off the keyboard until the current thought ends.
3. When a distraction appears, acknowledge it and return to typing.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## Next‑Day Revision

**When to use:** After completing a draft and needing to edit it effectively.

**Recipe:**

1. Close the document and engage in a non‑writing activity for several hours.
2. Return the next morning, read the draft aloud, and note problem areas.
3. Make structural and line edits, removing any edit markers placed earlier.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## Routine Integration

**When to use:** When you want to make the unedited drafting method a habit.

**Recipe:**

1. Pick a consistent daily time slot for writing.
2. Use a minimalist editor and perform a short writing ritual.
3. Log word count and schedule a separate revision block for the next day.

**Chapter:** ch05

## Cheatsheet

# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## process

- Turn off all notifications before you start writing.
- Keep your hands on the keyboard until the idea runs out of steam.
- Schedule your revision for the next day, not the same session.
- Log your word count after every session to track progress.

## writing

- If you feel the urge to edit, place an edit marker and keep typing.
- Stop only when the story tells you it has reached a natural pause.
- Begin each session with a five‑minute warm‑up typing exercise.
- Read your draft aloud before editing to hear rhythm and gaps.

## life

- Use a simple writing ritual to signal your brain it’s time to draft.
- Share your schedule with a writing partner for accountability.
