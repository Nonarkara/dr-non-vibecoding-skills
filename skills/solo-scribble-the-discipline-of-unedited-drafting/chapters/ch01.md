# ch01 — The Power of Uninterrupted Flow

> Explains why a continuous stream of consciousness captures ideas more authentically than fragmented writing. Shows how to set up a distraction‑free environment and commit to typing without stopping.

When you sit down to write, the most important decision is to keep the words moving. A continuous stream mirrors the way thoughts naturally unfold, allowing you to catch nuances that would disappear under the weight of early editing. Begin by clearing your workspace: silence notifications, close unrelated tabs, and set a timer for a solid block of time—usually 20 to 45 minutes. Position your keyboard so your hands rest comfortably, and adopt a posture that encourages breathing and focus. As you type, resist the urge to backtrack or correct spelling; the goal is to let the narrative flow as if you were speaking aloud. This method reduces the internal critic’s interference, letting the subconscious bring forward surprising connections and vivid details. If you feel a sudden urge to edit, note the spot with a simple marker (e.g., "[edit]") and keep typing. The uninterrupted flow creates a raw, energetic draft that serves as a fertile ground for later refinement.
