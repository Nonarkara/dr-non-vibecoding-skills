# ch03 — The Discipline of Keyboard Commitment

> Focuses on the habit of keeping your hands on the keyboard until the thought is exhausted, reinforcing the mental link between typing and thinking.

Discipline in writing is built on the simple rule: keep your hands on the keyboard until the idea runs out of fuel. This habit creates a feedback loop where thinking fuels typing and typing fuels thinking. To strengthen this discipline, start each session with a short warm‑up—type a few unrelated sentences to get your fingers moving. Then, set a clear intention: "I will not lift my hands until the current scene feels complete." When a distracting thought arises, acknowledge it, but return immediately to the draft. If you find yourself reaching for the mouse or scrolling away, remind yourself that the draft is a living organism; it thrives on continuous motion. Over time, the act of typing becomes a physical manifestation of mental flow, making it harder to interrupt the process prematurely.
