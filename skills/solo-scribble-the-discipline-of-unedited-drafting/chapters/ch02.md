# ch02 — Recognizing the Natural Pause

> Describes how to listen for the story’s own signals that indicate a pause is appropriate, rather than imposing an arbitrary stop.

Even the most fluid stream will eventually run out of steam. The natural pause is not a forced deadline but a moment when the narrative itself seems to reach a resting point—perhaps after a scene concludes, a character makes a decision, or a thematic thread is explored. To detect this, pay attention to the rhythm of your sentences and the emotional cadence of the scene. If you notice a lull in excitement or a repetition of ideas, that is a cue to stop typing. Mark the spot with a brief note like "[pause]" and step away. This respects the story’s internal logic and prevents you from forcing continuation that feels artificial. By honoring the natural pause, you preserve the integrity of the draft and set up a clear boundary for the next writing session.
