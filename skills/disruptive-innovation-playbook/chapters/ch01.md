# ch01 — Why Great Companies Fail

> Explains the paradox that listening to current customers and investing in next‑generation products can be fatal when a disruptive technology is emerging.

The Innovator’s Dilemma shows that firms succeeding by satisfying their best customers can be blindsided by a technology that serves a different set of customers. In the disk‑drive industry, incumbents kept improving capacity and price for mainframe users, yet missed the opportunity presented by smaller, cheaper drives aimed at personal computers. The core insight is that the very practices that guarantee success in sustaining innovation—customer focus, incremental investment, and cost‑structure optimization—become liabilities when a disruptive wave reshapes the market. Managers must recognize when the market they serve is no longer the growth engine and be willing to step away from the demands of their most profitable customers.
