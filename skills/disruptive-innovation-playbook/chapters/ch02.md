# ch02 — Disruptive vs. Sustaining Innovation

> Distinguishes between improvements that extend existing performance trajectories and those that redefine them.

Sustaining innovations improve products along an established performance curve, such as higher capacity or faster speed, and are managed with traditional forecasting and planning. Disruptive innovations, by contrast, introduce a new architecture or cost structure that initially under‑serves mainstream customers but opens a new market. The book’s case studies—from thin‑film heads to 5.25‑inch drives—show that disruptive technologies are often simpler, use off‑the‑shelf components, and target attributes like size, weight, or price rather than raw performance. Because they serve a different value proposition, they cannot be evaluated with the same metrics used for sustaining change.
