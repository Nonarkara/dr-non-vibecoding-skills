# ch02 — Mapping the Customer Profile

> How to identify and prioritize customer jobs, pains, and gains.

To build a useful Customer Profile, you must look through the customer's eyes. Start with 'Jobs to be Done.' These aren't just functional tasks like 'mowing the lawn.' They include social jobs (trying to look good or gain status) and emotional jobs (seeking security or peace of mind). Next, identify 'Pains.' These are the obstacles, risks, and bad outcomes the customer fears or experiences. Be specific: 'waiting too long' is vague; 'waiting more than 10 minutes' is a concrete pain. Finally, list 'Gains.' These are the benefits the customer expects, desires, or would be surprised by. Once you have a list, you must rank them. Not all jobs are important, and not all pains are extreme. A common mistake is trying to address every single item. Instead, focus on the 'high-value' jobs—those that are important, tangible, unsatisfied, and lucrative. If you don't know which pains are 'extreme' versus 'moderate,' you cannot design an effective solution.
