# ch07 — Understanding High-Value Jobs

> Criteria for selecting which customer needs to solve.

Not all customer jobs are worth solving. To find 'high-value' jobs, you must evaluate them against four criteria. First, is it 'Important'? Does the customer's success or failure depend on this job? Second, is it 'Tangible'? Can the pains and gains be felt immediately and clearly? Third, is it 'Unsatisfied'? Are current solutions failing to solve the problem? Fourth, is it 'Lucrative'? Are there enough people with this job, or are a few people willing to pay a high premium to solve it? High-value jobs often hide behind social or emotional needs. For example, a customer might buy a luxury watch not to tell time (functional), but to signal status (social) or feel successful (emotional). If you only focus on the functional job, you will miss the real value. Always ask 'why' several times to get to the root motivation.
