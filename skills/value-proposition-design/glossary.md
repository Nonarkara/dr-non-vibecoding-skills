# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer Job** | The tasks, problems, or needs a customer is trying to satisfy in their work or life. | ch02 |
| **Functional Job** | A specific task the customer wants to complete, like writing a report or eating healthy. | ch02 |
| **Social Job** | A job related to how a customer wants to be perceived by others, such as looking trendy or competent. | ch02 |
| **Emotional Job** | A job related to seeking a specific state of mind, such as feeling secure or at peace. | ch02 |
| **Pain** | Anything that annoys the customer before, during, or after a job, or prevents them from getting it done. | ch02 |
| **Gain** | The outcomes and benefits a customer wants to achieve, ranging from required to unexpected. | ch02 |
| **Pain Reliever** | A description of how a product or service eliminates or reduces a specific customer pain. | ch03 |
| **Gain Creator** | A description of how a product or service produces outcomes and benefits the customer values. | ch03 |
| **Problem-Solution Fit** | The stage where you have a plan that aligns your value map with customer needs on paper. | ch04 |
| **Product-Market Fit** | The stage where you have evidence that customers actually want and value your offer in the real world. | ch04 |
| **Business Model Fit** | The stage where your value proposition is part of a profitable and scalable business structure. | ch04 |
| **MVP** | Minimum Viable Product; the smallest set of features needed to test a value proposition with customers. | ch05 |
| **Napkin Sketch** | A very rough drawing used to quickly communicate and explore a core business idea. | ch05 |
| **Ad-lib** | A fill-in-the-blank sentence used to quickly define the essence of a value proposition. | ch05 |
| **Shrek Model** | An extreme or outrageous prototype designed to spark debate and test boundaries. | ch05 |
| **Technology Push** | Starting the design process with a new invention and looking for a problem it can solve. | ch06 |
| **Market Pull** | Starting the design process with a known customer problem and looking for a solution. | ch06 |
| **Economic Buyer** | The person in an organization who controls the budget and makes the actual purchase decision. | ch09 |
| **Saboteur** | A stakeholder who might try to block a purchase because it threatens their interests or status. | ch09 |
| **Workaround** | A makeshift solution a customer uses because no existing product solves their problem well. | ch08 |
