# ch05 — The Contrast Effect

> Explains how relative comparisons distort our sense of value. Provides a rule for absolute evaluation.

The contrast effect is a mental glitch where we judge something to be beautiful, expensive, or large only because we have something ugly, cheap, or small to compare it to. We struggle with absolute judgments. For example, a $3,000 leather seat upgrade seems like a bargain when compared to a $60,000 car price, but you would never spend $3,000 on a single chair for your home. Retailers use this by showing a 'suggested retail price' next to a 'discounted' price to make the latter seem like a steal. To think clearly, you must evaluate an offer in isolation. Ask yourself: 'If this were the only option and there was no discount, would I still pay this price?' Rule: Evaluate every choice on its own merits, not in comparison to a nearby alternative.
