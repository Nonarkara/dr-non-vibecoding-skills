# ch08 — The Overconfidence Effect

> Explains why we overestimate our knowledge and predictive power. Provides a rule for building margins of error.

The overconfidence effect is the systematic tendency to overestimate our own abilities and the accuracy of our knowledge. In surveys, the vast majority of people rate themselves as 'above average' drivers or lovers—a statistical impossibility. This bias is even more pronounced in experts, who offer forecasts with high certitude despite a track record no better than a coin flip. Overconfidence leads to project delays, budget overruns, and risky investments. It is an innate human trait, not easily fixed by incentives. To manage it, always assume a 'pessimistic scenario' for any plan. If you think a task will take two weeks, double it. Rule: Be skeptical of any prediction, especially your own, and build in a significant margin for error.
