# ch04 — The Authority Bias

> Examines why we over-rely on experts and status symbols. Suggests challenging authority to ensure logical consistency.

Authority bias describes our tendency to obey or believe figures of status, even when their expertise is irrelevant or their track record is poor. This was famously demonstrated in the Milgram experiment, where people administered shocks to others simply because a man in a lab coat told them to. In the professional world, this leads to 'HIPPO' (Highest Paid Person's Opinion) decision-making, where junior staff stay silent about errors made by leaders. Airlines reduced crashes by training co-pilots to challenge captains, effectively deprogramming this bias. Symbols of authority—suits, titles, degrees—often mask a lack of real knowledge. When facing an expert, ignore the credentials and focus on the logic of their argument. Rule: Challenge authority figures by asking for the data behind their assertions.
