# ch07 — Story Bias

> Analyzes how narratives distort facts to create a false sense of understanding. Suggests deconstructing stories into raw data.

Story bias is our drive to knit a jumble of unrelated facts into a neat, consistent narrative. We want the world to make sense, so we invent 'meaning' and 'identity' where there is often just randomness. News media excels at this, focusing on the biography of a single victim rather than the structural causes of a disaster. Stories attract us, but they also simplify and distort reality, filtering out anything that doesn't fit the plot. This leads to a false sense of understanding and encourages us to take risks based on 'fairytales.' To counter this, try to view events out of context. Ask: 'What are they trying to hide by telling this story?' Rule: Strip away the narrative and look at the disconnected facts to see the real picture.
