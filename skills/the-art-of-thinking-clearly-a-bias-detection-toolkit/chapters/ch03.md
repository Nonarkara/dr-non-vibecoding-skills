# ch03 — Confirmation Bias

> Describes the tendency to filter information to fit existing beliefs. Provides a method for 'murdering your darlings' to find the truth.

Confirmation bias is the tendency to interpret new information so that it supports our existing theories and worldviews. We subconsciously filter out 'disconfirming evidence'—facts that challenge what we already believe. For instance, a manager who believes a specific strategy is working will notice every small win while dismissing major failures as 'special cases' or 'exceptions.' This makes us blind to reality. Charles Darwin fought this by immediately writing down any observation that contradicted his theories, knowing his brain would try to 'forget' it. To counter this, you must actively hunt for evidence that proves you wrong. If you find yourself using the word 'exception' to describe a failure, you are likely falling for this bias. Rule: Write down your beliefs and then spend time trying to prove them false.
