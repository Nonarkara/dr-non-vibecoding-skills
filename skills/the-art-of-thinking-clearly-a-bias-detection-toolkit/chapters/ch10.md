# ch10 — Outcome Bias

> Explains the error of judging a decision by its result rather than its process. Suggests evaluating the logic of the choice.

Outcome bias is the tendency to evaluate a decision based on its result rather than the quality of the decision-making process at the time. If a gambler bets their life savings on a single spin of the roulette wheel and wins, outcome bias would lead us to call them a 'genius.' In reality, it was a terrible decision with a lucky result. This bias makes us reward lucky fools and punish talented people who suffered from bad luck. To assess a decision fairly, you must look only at the information available when the choice was made. A bad result doesn't always mean a bad decision, and a good result doesn't always mean a good decision. Rule: Evaluate the process and the logic, not the final score.
