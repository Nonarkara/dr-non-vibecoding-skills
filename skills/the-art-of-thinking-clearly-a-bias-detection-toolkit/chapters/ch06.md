# ch06 — Availability Bias

> Discusses why we overvalue information that is easy to recall. Offers a strategy for using data over anecdotes.

Availability bias leads us to create a picture of the world based on the examples that most easily come to mind. We overestimate the risk of spectacular events like plane crashes or shark attacks because they are vivid and reported frequently, while we underestimate quiet killers like diabetes or slow-moving economic shifts. We prefer a 'wrong map' to no map at all, often using the most recent or dramatic information to make decisions. This is why boards often discuss the latest news cycle rather than long-term strategic threats. To fight this, you must look at quantitative data rather than relying on memory or anecdotes. Seek out people with different experiences to broaden your 'map.' Rule: If it's easy to imagine, you are likely overestimating its frequency.
