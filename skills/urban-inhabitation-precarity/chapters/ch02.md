# ch02 — Precarity as a Power Modality

> How instability is used by capital to control urban life and erode social contracts.

In modern cities, instability is not an accident; it is a tool of power. Powerful economic actors have 'stolen' the flexibility that urban inhabitants once used to survive, turning it into a system of precarious labor and debt. This creates a condition where security of tenure and social contracts are weakened to promote 'resilience'—a term often used to force the poor to absorb the shocks of a failing system. For the urban inhabitant, precarity means living in a state of nonexistence where their presence is ignored by the state until it becomes a problem to be managed. When reasoning about urban change, identify how technologies of 'stability' (like debt or property titles) might actually disentangle the social solidarities that keep a community afloat.
