# ch09 — Informal Labor and Urban Insertion

> How marginalized groups use informal work to claim their right to the city.

Informal workers, such as waste pickers (cartoneros) and street vendors, use the city as a workplace. Their presence is a form of 'urban insertion'—a way for the poor to claim a place in the center of the city even if they live on the periphery. These workers must navigate physical and symbolic boundaries, such as the 'clean' neighborhoods of the middle class. Their work is often stigmatized, yet it is essential to the city's functioning (e.g., recycling waste). An agent should analyze informal labor not as a sign of 'underdevelopment,' but as a sophisticated way of accessing life in an unequal society. Look for how these groups organize to turn 'illegal' work into 'legitimate' presence.
