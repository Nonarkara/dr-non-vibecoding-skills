# ch04 — The Discipline of the Small Scale

> Why deep dives into small groups yield better insights than broad surveys.

To truly understand how a society functions, you should focus on small-scale communities, typically between 200 and 300 people. At this size, it is possible for a single researcher to know every individual and understand the intricate web of relationships that bind them. This 'ruralist' approach reveals that people in similar environments—such as farmers in different parts of the world—often have more in common with each other than they do with city dwellers in their own country. By focusing on a manageable group, you can document the relationship between technology, environment, and social structure in exhaustive detail. This prevents the loss of nuance that occurs in large-scale studies and allows for the identification of patterns that are invisible at a higher level of abstraction.
