# ch03 — The Researcher as a Living Instrument

> Using personal experience and participation as a research tool.

Fieldwork is not a detached activity; it is a performative one. The researcher often finds that they cannot understand a phenomenon, such as a ritual or a state of mind, without experiencing it themselves. This shared experience, sometimes called a 'ciné-trance,' involves the observer being changed by the act of observing. By participating in the life of the community, the researcher moves beyond verbal exchange to a deeper, bodily understanding of social reality. This approach acknowledges that the researcher's own presence affects the field. Instead of trying to be a 'fly on the wall,' you should recognize that your interactions are part of the data. This requires a high level of self-awareness and the willingness to let your own grip on reality be loosened by the experiences of those you are studying.
