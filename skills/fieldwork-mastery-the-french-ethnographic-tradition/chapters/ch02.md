# ch02 — The Primacy of the Fact

> Why meticulous data collection is superior to theoretical speculation.

The history of research shows that theories are subject to intellectual fashion, but a well-recorded fact is permanent. A researcher should prioritize the collection of 'raw' data—what people do, how they move, and the objects they use—over the urge to explain why they do it. This anti-theoretical stance ensures that the work remains useful for future generations who may have different analytical frameworks. Meticulous observation of technology, tools, and daily routines provides a solid foundation for any claim. When you focus on the 'singular'—the unique aspects of a person or a community—you avoid the trap of over-classification. Counting and classifying can often lead to treating different things as the same, which obscures the truth. The goal is to let the facts speak for themselves before trying to fit them into a pre-existing box.
