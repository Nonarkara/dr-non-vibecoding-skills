# ch05 — Mapping the Ceremonial Sequence

> Understanding social change through the logic of rites of passage.

Social life is a series of transitions, and these transitions are managed through 'rites of passage.' Every major change in a person's status—birth, marriage, or death—follows a three-part sequence: separation, liminality, and reincorporation. The 'liminal' phase is the most critical, as it is the 'in-between' state where the individual is no longer in their old role but not yet in their new one. By mapping these sequences, a researcher can see how a society dramatizes change and maintains order. This requires looking at rituals as movements through physical space, not just as abstract ideas. You must document the specific gestures, the timing, and the participants involved in each stage. This structural approach reveals the underlying logic of how a community reproduces itself over time.
