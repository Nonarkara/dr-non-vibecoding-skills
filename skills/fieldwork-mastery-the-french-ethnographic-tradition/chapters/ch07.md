# ch07 — The Danger of Indirect Enquiry

> Why relying on intermediaries can lead to false conclusions.

One of the greatest risks in fieldwork is the use of 'indirect' methods, such as relying solely on interpreters or local elites. While these intermediaries can provide quick access to information, they often filter the data through their own biases or tell the researcher what they think they want to hear. A researcher who does not have direct contact with the population risks becoming an 'armchair' theorist even while in the field. To avoid this, you must learn the local language and participate in daily activities. Relying on intermediaries can lead to a focus on generic human conditions rather than the specific, lived reality of a culture. True knowledge comes from questioning the people directly and observing their actions, rather than accepting the explanations of a middleman.
