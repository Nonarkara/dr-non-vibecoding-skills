# ch08 — Functional Historicism and Ecology

> Linking social behavior to biological and environmental history.

Human behavior is deeply rooted in the relationship between people, plants, and animals. A 'hyperrealist' view of the world focuses on what can be known through the senses and rejects the idea that social representations are entirely autonomous from nature. For example, the way a society manages its livestock or crops directly influences its ideas about authority, religion, and hierarchy. By studying the biological history of a region and the specific environment in which a population lives, you can uncover the origins of their social structures. This 'functional historicism' looks for the roots of culture in the practicalities of survival. It suggests that nature and culture are in a symbiotic relationship, where one often imitates the other.
