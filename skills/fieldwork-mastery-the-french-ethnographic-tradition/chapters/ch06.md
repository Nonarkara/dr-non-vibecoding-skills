# ch06 — The Ethics of Engagement

> Navigating the tension between neutrality and political commitment.

A researcher in the field must decide their level of involvement with the people they study. This ranges from strict intellectual neutrality to active political advocacy. Neutrality allows for a clear-eyed understanding of practices that might be shocking to an outsider, such as controversial rituals or rigid social hierarchies. However, many researchers find that long-term presence in a community leads to a natural desire to protect them from external threats, such as the negative effects of modernization or political oppression. The key is to be honest about your own position. Whether you are a passive observer or a committed activist, your primary duty is to achieve an accurate ethnographic understanding. Your commitment to the people should enhance your data collection, not distort it to fit a political agenda.
