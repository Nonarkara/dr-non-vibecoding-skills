# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## process

- Treat your own neighborhood as if it were the heart of an unexplored continent.
- Never trust an intermediary to tell you what the 'natives' are thinking.
- Distrust exhaustive questionnaires; focus on specific details instead.

## general

- A single ethnographic monograph outlasts a dozen theoretical books.
- The 'singular' is more important than the 'universal' in fieldwork.

## design

- If you want to understand a trance, you must be willing to enter one.
- Identify the core values (like purity) before you try to explain the symbols.
- Use drawing and photography to document rituals; images see what words miss.
- Ritual efficacy is found in the process of action, not just the belief.

## product

- Limit your study to 300 people if you want to see the whole social web.
- Avoid counting people; it treats unique individuals as interchangeable units.

## writing

- Look for the 'liminal' phase in every social change; that is where the truth is.
- Let the informant digress; their 'distractions' are mnemotechnical prompts.
- A novelist often has a better ethnographic gaze than a social scientist.

## life

- Understanding a practice does not mean you have to approve of it.
- The researcher is a misfit in their own society; use that distance to observe.
- Keep your eyes open all the time; wonder at everything.

## argument

- The way people treat their animals tells you how they treat their subordinates.
- Culture evolves on an autonomous plan, independent of geography or economics.

## code

- Facts are restricted to what can be known through the senses alone.
