# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ciné-trance** | A state where the observer is physically and mentally transformed by the act of recording or participating in a ritual. | ch03 |
| **Thinking in the singular** | A mode of thought that stresses the uniqueness of every being and rejects classification or counting. | ch02 |
| **Liminality** | The middle stage of a rite of passage where a person is in an 'in-between' state, stripped of their old social status. | ch05 |
| **Ethnographic essentialism** | The belief that the primary goal of anthropology is to record the specific, enduring facts of a culture. | ch02 |
| **Ruralism** | A research focus on small, agricultural communities as the fundamental unit of social study. | ch04 |
| **Hyperrealism** | A perspective that restricts 'facts' to what can be directly perceived through the senses, rejecting abstract social theories. | ch08 |
| **Functional historicism** | An approach that links social structures to the biological and environmental history of a population. | ch08 |
| **Ceremonial cycle** | The recurring sequence of festivals and rituals that organize a community's sense of time. | ch01 |
| **Holism** | A social value system where the needs and identity of the group take precedence over the individual. | ch09 |
| **Diffusionism** | The study of how cultural traits, words, or technologies spread across different geographic regions. | ch02 |
| **Participant observation** | The practice of living within a community and taking part in their daily life to gain internal insight. | ch03 |
| **Indirect enquiry** | A flawed research method that relies on intermediaries rather than direct contact with the subjects. | ch07 |
| **Social fact** | A way of acting or thinking that is external to the individual and exercises control over them. | ch01 |
| **Mnemotechnical prompt** | A trigger or association that helps an informant recall deep-seated cultural memories. | ch10 |
| **Hierarchical opposition** | A pair of contrasting values, like pure and impure, that organize a society's structure. | ch09 |
| **Ethnographic illusion** | The false sense of reality created by literary works that mimic but do not accurately record cultural facts. | ch10 |
| **Symbiosis** | The close and often imitative relationship between a human culture and its natural environment. | ch08 |
| **Separation** | The first stage of a rite of passage, where an individual is removed from their current social group. | ch05 |
| **Reincorporation** | The final stage of a rite of passage, where an individual enters their new social role. | ch05 |
| **Armchair theorizing** | The practice of creating social theories based on books and reports rather than direct fieldwork. | ch01 |
