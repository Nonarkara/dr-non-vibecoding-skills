# ch01 — The Five Mindsets

> Explains empathy, optimism, ambiguity, making, and learning from failure and shows how each mindset guides every HCD activity.

Human‑centered design rests on five attitudes that shape decisions. Empathy means stepping into the lives of the people you serve, listening without judgment, and keeping their voice at the center of every artifact. Optimism assumes a viable solution exists and treats constraints as opportunities for creative work. Embracing ambiguity accepts that the problem is unknown at the start, allowing the team to generate many ideas before narrowing. Making stresses rapid, tangible creation—paper prototypes, sketches, or low‑fi models—so that concepts can be tested early. Learning from failure treats every failed test as data, prompting a quick redesign rather than a sunk‑cost mindset. When an AI loads this chapter it adopts these attitudes, ensuring that every subsequent step respects the people, stays hopeful, tolerates uncertainty, builds fast, and iterates based on real feedback.
