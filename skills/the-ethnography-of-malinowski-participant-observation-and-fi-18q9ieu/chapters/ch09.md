# ch09 — The Household and the Cooking Taboo

> The domestic unit as the site of daily life and the 'hearth' of the culture.

The Trobriand household is a simple unit: one house, one family. The house is a place for sleeping and storage, while the 'front' is the social center. A key observation is the 'cooking taboo': in many villages, cooking is forbidden within the inner ring of storehouses. This creates a physical separation between the 'accumulated wealth' (the yams in the store) and the 'daily consumption' (the cooking fire). The hearth stones are the symbolic center of domesticity and the site of offerings to ancestors. When researching users, look at their 'domestic' environment—their personal workspace or home. How do they separate 'work' from 'life'? What are their 'hearth stones'—the tools or rituals they value most? The way a family eats—usually in small groups, even during a feast—reveals a preference for private consumption despite public display. Understanding the domestic unit allows you to see the 'micro-logic' that supports the 'macro-structures' of the society.
