# ch06 — The Discipline of the Damnfool Question

> Using naive inquiry to break through the 'obvious.' Why the expert must act like an imbecile.

Malinowski was remembered by the Trobrianders as the 'champion ass' at asking 'damnfool questions.' He would ask which end of a seed tuber goes in the ground, or why a baby is buried in a certain way. These questions seem absurd to locals because the answers are 'obvious.' However, it is precisely these 'obvious' things that contain the core logic of a culture. When you ask a naive question, you force the subject to articulate the rules they usually follow unconsciously. This technique is essential for uncovering 'tacit knowledge.' Do not be afraid to look foolish; if you have not been laughed at, you have not been listening. The laughter of the subject is a sign that you have hit a fundamental assumption. By recording the answers to these 'imbecile' questions, you build a map of the community's 'inward coherence.' You are not just looking for facts; you are looking for the 'colouring'—the sense that people have of themselves and their world.
