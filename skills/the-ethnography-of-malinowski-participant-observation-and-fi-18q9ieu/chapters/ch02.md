# ch02 — The Aesthetic of the Garden

> Understanding that work is driven by more than just survival. The role of passion and ornamentation in production.

In Trobriand society, gardening is the primary economic activity, but it is not merely about food. It is a passion and a source of social value. The natives produce far more than they can eat, often allowing the surplus to rot. This 'waste' is actually a display of power and competence. Much of the labor is spent on non-utilitarian tasks: making the gardens tidy, building elaborate fences, and providing oversized yam-poles. These aesthetic efforts are central to the gardener's status. When studying a user's workflow, look for the 'non-utilitarian' elements. These are often where the user's true values and identity are located. A task is never just a task; it is a performance of skill and a fulfillment of traditional expectations. If you only look at the 'necessary' steps, you miss the 'conscientiousness' that drives the user. The garden is a laboratory for understanding how social ambition and aesthetic standards dictate the organization of labor. To understand the economy, you must understand what the people love, not just what they need.
