# ch10 — The Reflexive Observer

> Managing the tension between scientific detachment and personal involvement.

Malinowski's private diaries revealed a man who often felt alienated, angry, and frustrated by the people he studied. This 'intense ambivalence' is a common experience in fieldwork. The researcher must oscillate between 'cold detachment' and 'warm involvement.' You are a 'fair and true interpreter,' but you are also a human being with your own biases and 'Slavic perversity.' The goal is not to be a perfect, neutral machine, but to be aware of your own impact on the data. The 'reflexive' approach acknowledges that the ethnographer is part of the experiment. You must record your own feelings and reactions as part of the field notes, as they can provide clues to the social pressures of the environment. The 'truth' of a culture is often found in the gap between the researcher's expectations and the reality of the field. By being honest about your own 'moral struggle,' you can produce a more nuanced and accurate account of the people you are studying.
