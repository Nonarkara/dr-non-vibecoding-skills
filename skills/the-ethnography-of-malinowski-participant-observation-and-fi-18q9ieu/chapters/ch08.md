# ch08 — The Seasonal Cycle and Time-Reckoning

> Timing research to match the natural rhythms of the community.

Trobriand life is governed by a strict seasonal cycle, tracked through a complex system of 'moons.' There are months for gardening, months for fishing, and months for 'milamala'—the feast of the spirits. Public life changes completely depending on the season. During the harvest, the village is filled with displays of yams and ceremonial distributions. During the monsoon, the focus shifts to indoor crafts and overseas expeditions. An ethnographer who only stays for a month will only see one 'moon' of activity and will miss the full picture. When studying a process, you must account for its 'seasonality.' What are the 'harvest' periods of high activity? What are the 'monsoon' periods of maintenance and preparation? Use a 'Chart of Time-reckoning' to map out these cycles. Insights that emerge during a 'scarcity' period may be completely different from those that emerge during a 'plenty' period. To see the 'inward coherence' of a system, you must watch it go through a full cycle.
