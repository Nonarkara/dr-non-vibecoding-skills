## Core principles

- Immersion is the only path to true understanding; you must live among the people in their natural setting.
- Distinguish between the 'ideal' (what people say they do) and the 'real' (what they actually do).
- The 'imponderabilia of actual life'—the routine, the mundane, and the emotional—are as important as formal laws.
- Ask 'damnfool questions' to uncover the basic assumptions that locals take for granted.
- Economic activity is rarely purely utilitarian; it is driven by passion, aesthetics, and social obligation.
- Social reality is located in the gap between formal structure and individual behavior.
- Record observations immediately and without editorializing to prevent memory from smoothing over contradictions.
- A society's logic is found in its 'inward coherence,' which only emerges after months of observation.

## The Three-Tiered Exchange

**When to use:** When commissioning a specialized product or service.

**Recipe:**

1. Give a 'solicitary gift' (yams/tobacco) to initiate the order.
2. Provide 'maintenance gifts' (food/supplies) while the work is in progress.
3. Deliver a 'clinching gift' (valuable/shell) upon completion of the work.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## The Partnered Barter

**When to use:** Exchanging resources between different ecological zones (e.g., coast and inland).

**Recipe:**

1. Establish a permanent partnership with an individual in the other zone.
2. The inland partner brings an 'opening gift' of vegetables to the beach.
3. The coastal partner returns the gift with an equivalent measure of fish.
4. Maintain the relationship through regular, predictable exchanges.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## The Ceremonial Distribution

**When to use:** Marking a major social transition like a death or harvest.

**Recipe:**

1. A leader pools resources from their kindred and relatives-in-law.
2. The food is displayed in large, public heaps on the central place.
3. The food is redistributed to individuals based on their social relationship to the event.
4. Recipients take the raw food home to redistribute it further within their own circles.

**Chapter:** ch08

---

## The Naive Inquiry

**When to use:** When you encounter a process that locals take for granted.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify a 'basic' fact that seems too obvious to ask about.
2. Ask the question in a way that admits your own ignorance.
3. Observe the reaction (laughter, confusion, or explanation).
4. Record the 'obvious' answer as a foundational rule of the system.

**Chapter:** ch06

---

## The Spatial Hierarchy Map

**When to use:** When entering a new organization or community.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the 'center' or most prestigious physical space.
2. Observe who is allowed to occupy that space and in what posture.
3. Map the distance between the 'center' and the 'periphery' (where the work happens).
4. Note any 'taboo' zones where certain activities (like cooking or informal talk) are forbidden.

**Chapter:** ch07

## Cheatsheet

# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## process

- If you have not been laughed at, you have not been listening.
- Record observations immediately; do not trust your memory to stay messy.
- Study the full seasonal cycle before claiming to understand the system.
- If the task feels too simple, you are missing the ritual.

## general

- Observe the imponderabilia of actual life, not just the formal rules.
- Be a 'fair and true interpreter' of the user's reality.

## design

- Distinguish between what people say they do and what they actually do.
- Physical elevation equals social rank; watch the heads.
- Aesthetics often trump utility in the eyes of the user.
- Cooking and wealth-storage should be physically separated.

## product

- Wealth is for movement, not for accumulation.
- Every industry has a traditional owner; respect the monopoly.
- Surplus is a social tool, not a waste.

## writing

- The 'obvious' is the most valuable data point.
- Use 'solicitary gifts' to trigger the information you need.
- The 'colouring' of a fact is as important as the fact itself.

## argument

- Follow the matrilineal line to find the real power.

## life

- The 'tent' must be in the village, not on the veranda.
- A gift always demands a return, but the timing is the art.
- The researcher's alienation is a data point, not a failure.
