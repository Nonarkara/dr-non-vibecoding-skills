## When to load this skill

This skill provides a framework for deep user research and ethnographic study based on the foundational methods of Bronislaw Malinowski. It focuses on long-term immersion, the distinction between what people say and what they do, and the systematic recording of the 'imponderabilia of actual life.' An agent should load this when it needs to design research protocols for natural settings, understand complex social hierarchies, or move beyond surface-level interviews to uncover the underlying logic of a community or user group.

## Core principles

- Immersion is the only path to true understanding; you must live among the people in their natural setting.
- Distinguish between the 'ideal' (what people say they do) and the 'real' (what they actually do).
- The 'imponderabilia of actual life'—the routine, the mundane, and the emotional—are as important as formal laws.
- Ask 'damnfool questions' to uncover the basic assumptions that locals take for granted.
- Economic activity is rarely purely utilitarian; it is driven by passion, aesthetics, and social obligation.
- Social reality is located in the gap between formal structure and individual behavior.
- Record observations immediately and without editorializing to prevent memory from smoothing over contradictions.
- A society's logic is found in its 'inward coherence,' which only emerges after months of observation.
