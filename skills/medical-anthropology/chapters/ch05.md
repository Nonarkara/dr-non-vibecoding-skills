# ch05 — Biopower and Silent Resistance

> Why patients appear to disobey medical authority and the logic behind it.

Medical systems often exercise 'biopower'—a form of control that mandates specific moralized behaviors, such as 'coming out' about an HIV status or strictly adhering to a diet. When patients find these mandates impossible or culturally inappropriate, they rarely confront the doctor directly. Instead, they engage in 'silent resistance.' This might look like slouching in a chair, giving one-word answers, or 'forgetting' a monitoring device. This resistance is often a logical response to a medical protocol that ignores the patient's social needs, such as the need for secrecy to avoid stigma or the need to prioritize work over clinic visits.
