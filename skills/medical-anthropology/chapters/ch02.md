# ch02 — Illness vs. Disease

> Distinguishing between biological pathology and the lived experience of being unwell.

A fundamental distinction in medical anthropology is between disease and illness. Disease refers to the objective, biological malfunction of the body as seen through the clinical lens. Illness is the subjective experience of the patient, including the social and emotional meanings they attach to their condition. A doctor might cure a disease without ever addressing the patient's illness. Effective care requires understanding the patient's 'explanatory model'—how they understand the cause, timing, and severity of their symptoms. When clinical advice ignores the patient's lived reality, it leads to a breakdown in the therapeutic relationship.
