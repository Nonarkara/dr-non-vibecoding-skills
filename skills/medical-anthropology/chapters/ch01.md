# ch01 — Children as Social Actors

> Moving beyond the view of children as passive subjects to recognizing their agency in health.

Anthropology has historically neglected children, viewing them as not yet human or merely subjects of socialization. However, children are active social actors who contribute to social processes. In health settings, they participate in their illnesses with insight, fear, and humor. While international conventions and medical institutions define 'the child' through rigid metrics of growth and development, individual children manage their own and others' sickness. They often protect their parents by hiding their own pain or knowledge of their prognosis. Recognizing children as knowledgeable actors requires a trialogue in clinical settings—between the child, the parent, and the clinician—rather than a simple dialogue that excludes the child's perspective.
