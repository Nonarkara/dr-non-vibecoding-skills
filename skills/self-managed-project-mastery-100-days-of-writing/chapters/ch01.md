# ch01 — Daily Writing as a Project Engine

> Explains why a simple daily writing habit fuels larger academic or professional projects and how to keep the habit from slipping.

The author treats each blog post as a micro‑project that contributes to a grander goal—finishing a PhD dissertation. By committing to write every day, even a few sentences, the mental overhead of starting a task disappears. The habit works like a treadmill: the body stays in motion, preventing the inertia that often stalls big projects. To maintain the habit, set a fixed time, keep the output modest (one paragraph or an aphorism), and treat the act as a ritual rather than a performance. When a day is missed, the author recommends a quick “catch‑up sprint” of 10 minutes rather than a guilt‑laden overhaul. This approach turns writing from a sporadic activity into a reliable engine that powers research, teaching, and personal growth.
