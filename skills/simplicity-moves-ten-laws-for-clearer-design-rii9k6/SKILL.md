## When to load this skill

This skill distills John Maeda's ten laws of simplicity into actionable chapters. Each law presents a concrete move—such as reducing features, organizing groups, saving time, or learning basics—that helps designers, engineers, and everyday creators make products and experiences feel simpler without stripping away meaning. By studying these laws, an agent can quickly pick the right tactic for a given simplicity challenge.

## Core principles

- Simplicity is achieved through thoughtful reduction, not just removal of features.
- Organization makes a large set of items appear fewer by grouping them.
- Saving time or making time feel shorter creates a sense of simplicity.
- Knowledge turns complex tasks into simple ones by making the familiar obvious.
- Simplicity and complexity depend on each other; one cannot exist without the other.
- What sits at the edge of a simple design influences its perceived simplicity.
- Designs that evoke more emotion feel simpler than those that feel flat.
- People trust simple designs when they behave predictably and reliably.
- Some inherent complexity cannot be eliminated; accepting limits is part of simplicity.
- The essence of simplicity is subtracting the obvious and adding the meaningful.

- **ch04 — Learn** — Knowledge turns complex tasks into simple ones by making the familiar obvious. This chapter covers learning basics, repetition, avoiding desperation, inspiring with examples, and the relate‑translate‑surprise method for teaching new concepts. (load: `chapters/ch04.md`)
- **ch05 — Differences** — Simplicity and complexity need each other; one defines the other. This chapter explains how to view simplicity not as the absence of complexity but as a relationship where both play necessary roles. (load: `chapters/ch05.md`)
- **ch06 — Context** — What lies at the edge of a simple design strongly influences its perceived simplicity. This chapter shows how to design the periphery—background, borders, surrounding elements—to reinforce the central simplicity. (load: `chapters/ch06.md`)
- **ch07 — Emotion** — Designs that evoke more emotion feel simpler than those that feel flat. This chapter explains how to layer emotional cues—warmth, surprise, delight—onto a reduced form to increase its perceived simplicity. (load: `chapters/ch07.md`)
- **ch08 — Trust** — People trust simple designs when they behave predictably and reliably. This chapter shows how to build trust through consistency, transparency, and reliable performance. (load: `chapters/ch08.md`)
- **ch09 — Failure** — Some inherent complexity cannot be eliminated; accepting limits is part of simplicity. This chapter explains how to identify unavoidable complexity and design around it rather than fighting it. (load: `chapters/ch09.md`)
