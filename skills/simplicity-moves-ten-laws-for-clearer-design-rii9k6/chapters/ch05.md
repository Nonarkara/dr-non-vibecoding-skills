# ch05 — Differences

> Simplicity and complexity need each other; one defines the other. This chapter explains how to view simplicity not as the absence of complexity but as a relationship where both play necessary roles.

A design that is utterly simple often feels bland because there is nothing to contrast against. Conversely, a design that is only complex feels chaotic. Simplicity gains its meaning when it is placed beside complexity—think of a quiet room highlighted by a single bright object, or a plain background that makes a colorful icon pop. When reducing features, consider what complexity you must retain to give the simple element its significance. The same principle applies in business: a simple pricing plan stands out only because competitors offer confusing bundles. Embrace complexity as a partner that makes simplicity noticeable and valuable, rather than treating it as an enemy to be eradicated at all costs.
