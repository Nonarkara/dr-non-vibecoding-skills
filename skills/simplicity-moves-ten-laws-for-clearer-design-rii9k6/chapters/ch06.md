# ch06 — Context

> What lies at the edge of a simple design strongly influences its perceived simplicity. This chapter shows how to design the periphery—background, borders, surrounding elements—to reinforce the central simplicity.

The human eye does not judge an object in isolation; it constantly compares it to what surrounds it. A simple shape on a busy background looks cluttered, while the same shape on a calm field feels clean. Therefore, when simplifying a core element, give equal attention to its context: remove distractions, align edges, use neutral colors, or add subtle framing that guides the eye toward the center. In digital interfaces, this means minimizing chrome, using ample whitespace, and ensuring that toolbars or menus do not compete for attention with the main content. In physical products, consider the packaging, the surface it rests on, and the lighting in the environment. By managing the periphery, you make the central simplicity more pronounced without altering the core itself.
