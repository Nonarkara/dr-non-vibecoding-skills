# ch09 — Failure

> Some inherent complexity cannot be eliminated; accepting limits is part of simplicity. This chapter explains how to identify unavoidable complexity and design around it rather than fighting it.

Not every system can be reduced to a single button; some tasks truly require multiple steps, decisions, or sources of information. Recognize these boundaries early to avoid wasted effort on impossible simplification. Instead of trying to erase the unavoidable complexity, design ways to make it manageable: break it into stages, provide clear signposts, or offer defaults that handle the common case. Accept that a certain amount of complexity will remain and focus on making that remainder as clear and predictable as possible. By treating failure as a design constraint rather than a flaw, you create simpler experiences that honor the real nature of the problem.
