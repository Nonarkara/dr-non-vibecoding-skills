# ch04 — Learn

> Knowledge turns complex tasks into simple ones by making the familiar obvious. This chapter covers learning basics, repetition, avoiding desperation, inspiring with examples, and the relate‑translate‑surprise method for teaching new concepts.

Start with the basics: assume the mindset of a first‑time learner and identify the essential steps they must master. Repeat yourself often—repetition builds familiarity and reduces the mental load of recalling steps. Avoid overwhelming beginners with advanced features; desperation shuts down learning. Inspire learners by showing concrete examples of what they can achieve once they know the basics. Use the relate‑translate‑surprise pattern: connect the new idea to something the learner already knows (relate), show how it maps onto a tangible object or service (translate), then add an unexpected, pleasant detail that rewards the effort (surprise). The desktop metaphor succeeded because it related to a physical desk, translated files into folders, and surprised users with the ability to manage far more documents than before. When metaphors fail (e.g., a trash icon unfamiliar to some users), surprise can backfire, so test the mapping with your audience. Finally, remember that the real reward of learning is the ability to act confidently without constant instruction.
