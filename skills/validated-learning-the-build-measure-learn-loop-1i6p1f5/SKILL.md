## When to load this skill

This skill guides teams through the core process of validated learning – the build-measure-learn loop – to minimize risk and maximize the chances of creating a successful product. It’s for situations where a team is stuck between perfecting a solution and launching something that might fail.

## Core principles

- Startups operate in extreme uncertainty, requiring a constant cycle of experimentation to reduce this uncertainty.
- Validated learning is the foundation of a startup; it’s about testing assumptions through rapid, actionable experiments.
- Focus on creating a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) to test your riskiest assumptions quickly and cheaply.
- Metrics should be tied directly to actionable insights, not vanity metrics that provide misleading information.
- A pivot is a strategic course correction based on validated learning, not simply changing direction without evidence.

## Rapid Prototyping

**When to use:** When you need to quickly test an idea.

**Recipe:**

1. Create a basic prototype.
2. Get feedback from potential users.
3. Iterate based on the feedback.

**Chapter:** ch04

## A/B Testing

**When to use:** When you want to compare two versions of something.

**Recipe:**

1. Create two variations of a feature or design.
2. Randomly assign users to each variation.
3. Measure the results and choose the better performing version.

**Chapter:** ch08

---

## The Pivot Cycle

**When to use:** When your initial assumptions are proving incorrect.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the failing assumption.
2. Brainstorm alternative approaches.
3. Build a quick experiment to test the new approach.
4. Decide whether to persevere or pivot.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## Focus on Key Assumptions

**When to use:** When launching an MVP.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify your riskiest assumptions.
2. Design experiments to test those assumptions.
3. Prioritize experiments based on the potential impact of a wrong answer.

**Chapter:** ch04

## Cheatsheet

# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## design

- Before you ship the next feature, name the assumption it tests and how you would know if it were wrong.
- Don't fall in love with your initial product idea; be willing to change course based on customer feedback.

## product

- Don't build features just because you *could*. Build them because you *should* – based on validated learning.

## general

- If a metric isn’t driving action, it’s a vanity metric. Focus on metrics that tell you what to do next.
- Measure everything, but don’t get bogged down in analysis paralysis. Focus on actionable insights.

## argument

- A pivot isn't failure; it's a strategic course correction based on new information.

## code

- Always design experiments to test your riskiest assumptions – the ones that, if wrong, would sink your startup.

## process

- Regularly review your assumptions and validate them with experiments – it's the only way to build a sustainable business.
