# ch02 — The Experimenter

> The role that makes ideas tangible through rapid, low-fidelity prototyping.

Experimenters believe that 'failing often to succeed sooner' is the only way to innovate. They don't wait for a perfect plan; they build crude models out of whatever is at hand—foam core, duct tape, or even a film canister and a clothespin. The goal of an Experimenter is to lower the bar for what counts as a prototype. A prototype doesn't have to be a physical object; it can be a thirty-second video, a storyboard, or a 'paper-thin' version of a product. By showing a half-baked idea early, the Experimenter gets immediate feedback and avoids the 'sunk cost' trap of spending months on a doomed concept. They use 'implementation through experimentation,' asking teams to try a new process as a temporary test rather than a permanent 'rollout.' This reduces resistance to change. The Experimenter's mantra is that everything can be prototyped, including services, proposals, and customer experiences.
