# ch01 — The Anthropologist

> The role focused on observing human behavior with a 'beginner's mind' to find unarticulated needs.

The Anthropologist is the most critical learning role. They do not rely on what people say in surveys; they watch what people actually do in their natural environments. This role requires 'Vuja De'—the ability to look at a familiar situation as if seeing it for the first time. Anthropologists practice the Zen principle of 'beginner's mind,' setting aside their expertise to observe without judgment. They look for 'thoughtless acts'—the small ways people hack or adapt products to fit their needs, like taping a note to a photocopier. These hacks are clues to where a product is failing. To be an Anthropologist, you must keep a 'bug list' of things that frustrate you and an 'idea wallet' of clever solutions you see in the world. They often study 'extreme' users—people who either love or hate a service—to find insights that the average user might not reveal. The goal is to humanize the data and find the 'why' behind the behavior.
