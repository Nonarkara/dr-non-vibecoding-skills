# ch04 — When to Pursue a PhD (or Not)

> Provides a decision framework for evaluating the true value of a doctorate versus alternative career paths.

The author reflects on common motivations for a PhD—respect, money, fame, and curiosity—and argues that only deep curiosity justifies the investment. A simple decision filter is proposed: list the concrete skills you need for your desired career, then map which of those the PhD will actually teach. If the answer is “few,” consider MOOCs, apprenticeships, or industry experience instead. The chapter also warns against using a PhD as a “time‑killing” safety net; the financial and emotional costs often outweigh the benefits unless the research aligns with a personal passion that cannot be satisfied elsewhere.
