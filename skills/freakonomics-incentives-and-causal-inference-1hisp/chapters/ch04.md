# ch04 — Detecting Teacher Fraud

> How high-stakes testing creates incentives for teachers to manipulate student data.

In Chicago, the introduction of high-stakes testing meant that schools with low scores could be closed and teachers fired. This created a powerful incentive for teachers to cheat. An algorithm was developed to find them by looking for suspicious patterns in student answer sheets. The algorithm looked for two things: blocks of identical answers among students in the same class, especially on the hardest questions, and students who showed a massive one-year spike in scores followed by a dramatic drop the next year. A good teacher's students show real learning that carries over to the next grade, but a cheating teacher's students only look smart for the duration of the test. When the suspicious teachers were forced to have their students retake the test under neutral supervision, their scores plummeted. This proves that when you measure performance with a single metric, you create an incentive for people to 'manage' the metric rather than improve the actual work.
