# ch08 — Conventional Wisdom vs. Hard Data

> Why the stories we tell ourselves about the world are often based on convenience rather than truth.

Conventional wisdom is often a shorthand for things that are comfortable to believe but factually incorrect. It is frequently created by 'experts' or journalists who need a simple, dramatic story. For example, in the 1990s, it was widely believed that crime was soaring and would continue to do so. When crime actually fell, experts credited better policing, gun control, and a strong economy. However, the data suggested a much more distant and controversial cause: the legalization of abortion twenty years earlier, which reduced the number of children born into high-risk environments. Conventional wisdom is difficult to challenge because it is often tied to morality or political agendas. To find the truth, you must be willing to ask uncomfortable questions and look for the data that the conventional story ignores. If a claim sounds too simple or fits a popular narrative too perfectly, it is likely a piece of conventional wisdom that needs to be audited.
