# ch02 — The Haifa Day-Care Paradox

> A case study on how introducing an economic incentive can accidentally destroy a moral one.

In a study of ten day-care centers in Israel, economists tried to solve the problem of parents picking up their children late. They introduced a $3 fine for tardiness. Surprisingly, late pickups doubled. The incentive backfired for two reasons. First, the fine was too small; for $3, parents could buy off their guilt for a very low price. Second, the economic incentive replaced a moral one. Before the fine, parents felt guilty for making teachers wait. Once the fine was introduced, the relationship became a market transaction. Parents felt they were paying for a service rather than violating a social norm. When the fine was eventually removed, the high rate of lateness remained. The parents now had no fine to pay and no guilt to feel. This teaches that when you design an incentive, you must ensure the price is high enough to deter the behavior and be careful not to crowd out the existing moral or social pressures that were already keeping the behavior in check.
