# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## design

- If a fine is too small, it becomes a fee that parents or users will happily pay.

## process

- Cheating is most likely when there is a bright-line incentive between winning and losing.
- A sudden spike in performance followed by a crash is a primary indicator of fraud.
- Don't confuse correlation with causality; just because things happen together doesn't mean one caused the other.
- The '8th win' in a 15-match tournament is the most valuable and the most likely to be rigged.
- To find the truth, look for the 'incidental data' left behind like an airplane's contrail.

## general

- Experts use fear and information gaps to make you act in their interest, not yours.
- Bad weather and high-stress holidays increase the rate of cheating and theft.
- Morality is how we want the world to work; economics is how it actually does.
- An expert's incentive is to do the least amount of work for the quickest commission.

## code

- Data doesn't lie, but the people who collect it often do.

## life

- Small communities are more honest because the social incentive of shame is more powerful.
- Most people are honest 87% of the time, even when no one is watching.

## argument

- Ask 'Who benefits from this story?' before accepting conventional wisdom as fact.

## product

- Money follows winning candidates; it rarely creates them from scratch.
