## The Bubble Match Analysis

**When to use:** When you suspect collusion in a competitive or ranking system.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the 'bubble' threshold where a win is worth significantly more than a loss.
2. Compare the win rates of those on the bubble against those who have already secured their rank.
3. Check the results of the next meeting between the same parties to see if the win-loss pattern reverses.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## The Identical String Search

**When to use:** When you need to detect if data has been manually manipulated or faked.

**Recipe:**

1. Look for long strings of identical answers or results within a single group.
2. Focus on the hardest parts of the task where identical success is least likely.
3. Compare the group's performance to their past and future results to find temporary spikes.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## The Price of Guilt Test

**When to use:** When you want to know if a fine will deter or encourage a behavior.

**Recipe:**

1. Measure the baseline frequency of the behavior under moral pressure.
2. Introduce a small financial penalty.
3. Observe if the behavior increases because the penalty acts as a 'price' that removes the guilt.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## The Expert Outcome Audit

**When to use:** When you want to see if an expert is giving you biased advice.

**Recipe:**

1. Find data on the expert's personal outcomes for the same task they perform for you.
2. Compare the time spent and the final price achieved for the expert vs. the client.
3. Identify the point where the expert's marginal gain from your success is no longer worth their effort.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## The Honor System Baseline

**When to use:** When you need to measure the inherent honesty of a specific population.

**Recipe:**

1. Provide a desirable good in an unmonitored environment.
2. Request a specific payment via an honor-system box.
3. Audit the 'theft' rate against variables like group size, weather, and hierarchy.

**Chapter:** ch06

## process

- Cheating is most likely when there is a bright-line incentive between winning and losing.
- A sudden spike in performance followed by a crash is a primary indicator of fraud.
- Don't confuse correlation with causality; just because things happen together doesn't mean one caused the other.
- The '8th win' in a 15-match tournament is the most valuable and the most likely to be rigged.
- To find the truth, look for the 'incidental data' left behind like an airplane's contrail.

- Morality is how we want the world to work; economics is how it actually does.
