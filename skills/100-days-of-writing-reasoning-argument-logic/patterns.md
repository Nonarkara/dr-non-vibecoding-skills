## The Unimaginable Counter-Example

**When to use:** When someone claims a social norm is a universal necessity.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the core assumption (e.g., 'Society needs fathers').
2. Search for anthropological or historical exceptions (e.g., the Na people).
3. Analyze how the exception functions without the 'necessary' element.
4. Use the existence of the exception to dismantle the claim of universality.

## The Murakami Syntax Constraint

**When to use:** When trying to clarify complex or disjointed thoughts.

**Recipe:**

1. Strip away all complex vocabulary and jargon.
2. Write only in simple, short sentences.
3. Focus on the rhythm created by the sequence of sentences.
4. Let the connections between ideas emerge naturally from the simplified structure.

**Chapter:** ch04

## Biopower Deconstruction

**When to use:** When feeling social anxiety due to a label or stigma.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the label being applied (e.g., 'leftover').
2. Determine which institution or authority benefits from this label.
3. Look for the 'productive logic'—is it for your benefit or the state's?
4. Recognize the label as an external tool of control to neutralize its emotional impact.

**Chapter:** ch02

## Pragmatic Grief Management

**When to use:** When facing overwhelming emotional despair or loss.

**Recipe:**

1. Fill the schedule with immediate, concrete tasks (rituals, logistics).
2. Focus on 'what works' in the immediate moment rather than long-term meaning.
3. Use physical activity (like biking or cleaning) to ground the mind.
4. Allow the routine to provide a safety net that prevents the mind from wandering into the abyss.

**Chapter:** ch07

## Reflexive Fieldwork

**When to use:** When observing a new environment or person.

**Recipe:**

1. Note your initial impressions and emotional reactions.
2. Ask: 'What from my past does this remind me of?'
3. Identify any 'uncanny' biases based on previous experiences or media.
4. Adjust your description to account for your own subjective history.

**Chapter:** ch05
