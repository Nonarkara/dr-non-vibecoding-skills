# ch03 — The Unimaginable Alternative

> Using anthropological counter-examples to break the illusion of 'universal' social structures.

We often assume the nuclear family is the only possible building block of society. However, the Na (or Mosuo) people near the China-Burma border provide a real-world counter-example. In their society, there are no husbands or fathers in the traditional sense. They are matrilineal; children live with their mother's family, and men play no role in upbringing. Sex is a voluntary, nocturnal act without economic or social bonds. They view attempts to monopolize a partner as shameful. This 'unimaginable community' proves that what we consider 'natural' or 'necessary' is often just one of many possible frameworks. When an argument claims that a certain social structure is the only way for a society to function, look for 'Zomia'—geographical or social spaces beyond state control—to find evidence to the contrary. Finding a single working exception destroys the argument of 'universal necessity.'
