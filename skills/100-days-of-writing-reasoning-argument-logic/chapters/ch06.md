# ch06 — Existential Loneliness as Freedom

> Redefining loneliness from a negative emotional state to a productive condition for agency.

Loneliness is often viewed as a problem to be solved, but existentialists like Sartre and Schopenhauer see it differently. Schopenhauer argued that a person can only be truly themselves and truly free when they are alone. Loneliness is an 'unavoidable condition of our humanity' that expands as we confront life's big questions. It is not the same as the suffering of loss; it is a way of grasping for your own subjective truth. Instead of fearing loneliness, use it as a space for individual agency. Loneliness forces you to make decisions without the influence of others. It is the only state in which you can have a true dialogue with yourself. When you stop seeing loneliness as a 'lack' and start seeing it as 'solitude,' it becomes a tool for independence and self-discovery.
