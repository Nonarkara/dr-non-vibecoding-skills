# ch04 — Writing through Constraints

> How to use limited vocabulary and simple syntax to generate a distinctive rhythm and clear ideas.

Haruki Murakami developed his writing style by attempting to write in English, a language where his vocabulary was limited. This forced him to use simple, short sentences and strip away 'extraneous fat.' This constraint-based approach is a powerful tool for reasoning. When your thoughts are complex and disjointed, do not try to write them down in a complex way. Instead, force yourself into a 'container of limited size.' Use short, straightforward sentences. This creates a distinctive rhythm and prevents you from hiding weak logic behind flowery prose. By simplifying the language, you make the connections between your ideas more visible. If an idea cannot be expressed simply, it may not be fully formed yet. Use the constraint of simplicity to find the unified whole in your 'focused rambling.'
