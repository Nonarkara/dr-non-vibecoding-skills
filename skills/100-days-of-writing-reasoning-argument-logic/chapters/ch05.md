# ch05 — The Uncanny Bias

> Recognizing how past impressions and physical appearances distort our current judgments.

We are often drawn to people or places because of an 'uncanny' resemblance to something from our past. This is a form of bias that can cloud judgment. For example, you might fall for someone simply because they look like an actress you liked as a child or a previous partner. This obsession with past impressions is not just about people; it applies to research and field sites as well. You might like a city because it reminds you of a TV show you watched, not because of its actual merits. To maintain intellectual honesty, you must practice reflexivity. Acknowledge that when you describe your environment, you are often describing yourself and your own history. Ask: 'Am I seeing this clearly, or am I seeing a ghost of something I used to know?' Identifying these 'uncanny' triggers allows you to separate nostalgia from objective reality.
