# ch07 — The Pragmatism of Busyness

> Using focused activity to navigate grief and prevent the mind from falling into non-productive despair.

Grief and despair often thrive when there is too much 'time in hand.' When faced with a crisis, such as the death of a loved one, the best defense is pragmatism. During the ten days of a traditional Thai funeral, the sheer volume of rituals, greeting guests, and organizing publications leaves no time for 'collateral sadness' to take root. This is not about avoiding emotions, but about staying within the 'realm of pragmatism.' Busyness provides a structure that keeps the mind from wandering into the abyss of non-productive thoughts. By setting a schedule—getting up, exercising, working—you maintain the underlying structure of your life. This 'healthy busyness' is a cure for the kind of loneliness that leads to hopelessness. It allows you to contribute to society while your mind processes trauma in the background.
