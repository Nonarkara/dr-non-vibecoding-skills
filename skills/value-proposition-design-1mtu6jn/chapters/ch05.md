# ch05 — Prototyping Possibilities

> Using low-fidelity models to explore ideas quickly and cheaply.

Prototyping is the practice of building quick, inexpensive models to learn about your idea. It is not about building the final product; it is about exploring alternatives. Start with 'Napkin Sketches'—rough drawings that capture a single core idea. Use 'Ad-libs' to create a ten-second pitch that forces you to identify the customer, the job, and the value. As you gain confidence, move to more refined Value Proposition Canvases. The goal is to stay in a 'liquid state' as long as possible. If you refine an idea too early, you become attached to it and find it hard to throw away. Use 'Shrek models'—extreme or outrageous versions of your idea—to spark debate and find the boundaries of what works. By failing early and cheaply through prototypes, you learn faster and avoid wasting resources on a polished version of a bad idea.
