# ch07 — The Seven‑Stage Action Cycle

> Breaks down human activity into goal, plan, specify, perform, perceive, interpret, and compare, showing where design can support each stage.

Norman’s action cycle has seven stages: (1) form a goal, (2) plan the action, (3) specify the sequence, (4) perform the action, (5) perceive the result, (6) interpret the perception, and (7) compare the outcome with the goal. Designers can aid the first three stages with clear signifiers, constraints, and mapping, reducing the mental effort required to plan. The middle stage—perform—benefits from ergonomic design that matches the user's physical capabilities. The last three stages—perceive, interpret, compare—rely on feedback and a coherent conceptual model. If any stage is poorly supported, users experience frustration, make errors, or abandon the task. By mapping each stage to design elements, the designer can systematically evaluate where a product fails and apply targeted improvements.
