# ch09 — Designing for First‑Time Users

> Provides a checklist for ensuring that a product can be used correctly by someone who has never seen it before.

The first‑time user test is the most stringent. The product must reveal its affordances, provide unambiguous signifiers, use natural mapping, give immediate feedback, and limit possible actions with constraints. A quick walkthrough should let a new user achieve the core goal without consulting a manual. Designers can simulate this by asking a colleague who has never handled the product to complete a task while thinking aloud. Observe where the Gulf of Execution or Evaluation appears and add signifiers or feedback accordingly. The checklist includes: visible affordances, clear signifiers, spatial mapping, feedback for every action, constraints that prevent harmful actions, and a simple conceptual model that can be inferred from the device itself.
