# ch06 — Conceptual Models and System Image

> Describes how users build mental models from the system image—what they see, hear, and read—and why aligning the two is crucial.

A conceptual model is the user's internal explanation of how a device works. The system image is everything the designer provides: physical shape, labels, documentation, and online help. When the system image matches the user's mental model, actions are predictable and errors are rare. Mismatches—such as a refrigerator with two controls that do not correspond to the two compartments—force users to experiment or consult manuals. Designers should create a coherent system image that conveys the essential structure of the device. This includes using signifiers that double as affordances, arranging controls to reflect functional relationships, and providing consistent feedback that reinforces the model. A good conceptual model lets users diagnose problems and adapt when the device behaves unexpectedly.
