# ch06 — Adapting Solutions to Local Context

> Guides designers on how to translate abstract solutions into concrete actions.

The solution part of each pattern is deliberately abstract, describing the essential relationships rather than prescribing exact details. Designers must interpret these relationships in light of climate, culture, site constraints, and personal preferences. For example, the instruction "provide positive outdoor space" can become a garden, a patio, or a shaded plaza, depending on the local environment. The key is to preserve the invariant—what makes the space work—while customizing the form. This flexibility allows the pattern language to be used worldwide and across centuries, as long as the underlying relationships are respected.
