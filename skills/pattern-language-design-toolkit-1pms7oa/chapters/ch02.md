# ch02 — The Hierarchy of Patterns

> Explains the relationship between larger, same‑size, and smaller patterns.

Patterns are ordered from the biggest scale—regions and towns—down to the smallest details of construction. A larger pattern (for example, IDENTIFIABLE NEIGHBORHOOD) is incomplete without the smaller patterns that fill it (such as ACCESSIBLE GREEN). Conversely, a small pattern cannot be fully realized unless it is embedded in a larger context that gives it purpose. This hierarchy creates a web of dependencies: each pattern both supports and is supported by others. When a designer chooses a pattern, they must also consider the patterns that surround it, ensuring that the chosen pattern fits within a larger framework and that the necessary smaller patterns are also implemented. The hierarchy guarantees coherence across scales, preventing isolated interventions that would feel out of place.
