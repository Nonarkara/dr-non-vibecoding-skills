# ch06 — Dogmas of Substance

> Analyzes how cultures use physical metaphors like bone and blood to encode social belonging.

Cultures often use 'dogmas of substance' to explain social identity. For example, some Asian societies believe that the bones of a child come from the father's semen while the flesh and blood come from the mother. These are not primitive scientific theories but symbolic maps of social structure. 'Bone' often represents the permanent incorporation into a patrilineage, while 'flesh' represents the temporary or affinal link to the mother's side. In North Burma, the Kachins believe children should resemble their mothers because they are fed by her, yet they maintain a strict patrilineal system. These beliefs about physical appearance and substance are used to discriminate between permanent group membership (incorporation) and the links created by marriage (alliance). By identifying which substances are associated with which parent, an analyst can determine how a society balances the competing claims of different kin groups.
