# ch02 — Topology and Social Elasticity

> Introduces the concept of topology as a way to understand structural constants in changing social environments.

Topology, often described as the geometry of elastic rubber sheeting, provides a powerful metaphor for social structure. If you draw a pattern on a piece of rubber and stretch it, the manifest shape changes, but the fundamental connectedness of the lines remains the same. In anthropology, this means that the same structural pattern can appear in societies that look very different on the surface. We should not be distracted by the 'shape' of a culture—its specific rituals or names for things—but should instead focus on the 'connectedness' of its variables. By treating social phenomena as topological variables, we can identify regularities that persist across different manifest types. This approach allows us to compare a patrilineal system in Burma with a matrilineal system in the Pacific by looking at the mutual characteristics of their relationships rather than their specific cultural content. The focus shifts from individual entities to the patterns of organizational ideas.
