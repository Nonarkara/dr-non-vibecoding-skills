# ch07 — Incorporation vs. Alliance

> Defines the fundamental opposition between group membership and the links between groups.

The most basic distinction in any kinship system is between relations of incorporation and relations of alliance. Incorporation refers to the ties that make an individual part of a 'we-group' (like a lineage). Alliance refers to the ties that link that 'we-group' to 'other-groups' (usually through marriage). These two types of relations are always in opposition. Incorporation is typically symbolized by 'common substance'—the idea that group members share the same blood or bone. Alliance, on the other hand, is often symbolized by 'mystical influence' or external power. This dichotomy is universal, appearing in both unilineal and non-unilineal systems. Understanding a society requires identifying how it draws the line between these two categories. If we treat every relationship as 'kinship,' we miss the crucial structural difference between the people we are 'made of' and the people we are 'linked to' through social contract.
