# ch01 — The Failure of Classification

> Critiques the traditional 'butterfly collecting' approach to anthropology and proposes a shift from organic to mechanical and mathematical models.

Traditional anthropology often falls into the trap of treating societies like biological species. This approach, favored by functionalists, focuses on classifying different 'types' of societies—much like a botanist classifies plants. However, this method is limited because it treats social institutions as fixed things rather than dynamic principles. A more effective approach is to think like an engineer or a mathematician. Instead of asking what a society 'is,' we should ask how it 'works.' By moving away from the organic analogy—where society is a delicately balanced mechanism like a watch—we can begin to see it as a set of logical operations. The goal is not to collect and label different customs but to identify the underlying principles that govern social interaction. This requires abandoning the comfort of descriptive categories and embracing a more abstract, analytical mindset that looks for the functional interconnections between parts of a system.
