# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Butterfly collecting** | The practice of classifying cultures into types based on surface traits rather than underlying principles. | ch01 |
| **Topology** | A branch of mathematics used here as a metaphor for social structures that maintain their connectedness despite changes in manifest shape. | ch02 |
| **Filiation** | The social rule that assigns an individual a relationship to a parent. | ch05 |
| **Affinity** | Social relationship created by marriage rather than descent. | ch03 |
| **Incorporation** | The process or state of being part of a corporate 'we-group,' usually defined by shared substance. | ch07 |
| **Alliance** | The structural link between two distinct groups, typically maintained through marriage. | ch07 |
| **Uncontrolled mystical influence** | Supernatural power that acts involuntarily on an individual due to their social position, often associated with 'fate.' | ch08 |
| **Controlled supernatural attack** | The intentional use of supernatural power, such as sorcery or cursing, to assert authority or punish. | ch08 |
| **Tama** | A Trobriand category often translated as 'father' but structurally meaning 'mother's husband' or affine. | ch03 |
| **Pupa** | A Lakher category referring to the mother's patrilineage, treated as an affinal group. | ch04 |
| **Mayu-dama** | A system of enduring marriage alliance between groups of wife-givers and wife-takers. | ch09 |
| **Urigubu** | A Trobriand harvest gift paid to a sister's husband or a father, acting as a marker of affinal obligation. | ch03 |
| **Complementary filiation** | A term used by other anthropologists to describe bonds with the non-descent parent, which Leach argues is just a variable in a ratio. | ch05 |
| **Sociological paternity** | The social recognition of a father figure, which may be entirely independent of biological fatherhood. | ch03 |
| **Common substance** | The cultural belief that group members share physical material like blood or bone. | ch06 |
| **Metaphysical prime mover** | A force like God or Life Force used in organic analogies to explain social cohesion. | ch01 |
| **Neighborhood system** | A topological concept describing the set of relationships surrounding a specific social point. | ch10 |
| **Elastic rubber sheeting** | A metaphor for the flexibility of social structures that retain their core pattern under pressure. | ch02 |
| **Genetic inheritance dogma** | A society's specific theory about how physical traits are passed down, used to justify social rules. | ch06 |
| **Political dominance** | The use of marriage and ritual influence to establish a hierarchy between groups. | ch09 |
