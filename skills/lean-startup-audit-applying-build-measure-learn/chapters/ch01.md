# ch01 — Foundations of the Lean Startup

> Introduces the core philosophy behind the Lean Startup, focusing on hypothesis‑driven product development and the importance of learning over shipping. Sets the stage for the Build‑Measure‑Learn cycle.

The Lean Startup treats every new product as an experiment. Before any code is written, the team writes a clear hypothesis about the problem they think they are solving and the value they expect to deliver. This hypothesis becomes the yardstick for all later decisions. The method rejects the traditional "build‑then‑sell" approach, arguing that shipping features without evidence wastes time and money. Instead, the startup aims to generate *validated learning*—knowledge that a hypothesis is true or false—through rapid, low‑cost experiments. The Build‑Measure‑Learn loop is the engine that turns ideas into data. By iterating quickly, teams can discover product‑market fit before scaling. The chapter also explains why traditional metrics like lines of code or feature count are poor indicators of success; only learning that moves the business forward matters.
