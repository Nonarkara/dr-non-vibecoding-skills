## Create Autonomous Unit for Disruptive Tech

**When to use:** When a new technology requires a different cost structure or market focus.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the disruptive technology and its target market.
2. Spin off a separate business unit with its own budget and decision‑making authority.
3. Design the unit’s processes and incentives around low‑margin, high‑growth objectives.
4. Protect the unit from incumbent resource‑allocation pressures.

## Discovery‑Based Planning Loop

**When to use:** When market size and demand are unknown.

**Recipe:**

1. Formulate hypotheses about customer needs and value propositions.
2. Allocate small, reversible investments to test each hypothesis.
3. Gather real‑world feedback and iterate the business model.
4. Scale only after validated learning reduces uncertainty.

**Chapter:** ch05

## Match Team Size to Market Size

**When to use:** Large firms need to address a small, fast‑growing market.

**Recipe:**

1. Define the market’s current revenue potential.
2. Assemble a lean team whose total cost aligns with that potential.
3. Empower the team to make rapid decisions without senior‑level approval.
4. Review progress quarterly and adjust team size as the market grows.

**Chapter:** ch04

## Monitor Performance‑Demand Gap

**When to use:** To anticipate when the basis of competition will shift.

**Recipe:**

1. Track product performance metrics (e.g., speed, capacity) over time.
2. Measure customer willingness to pay for each performance level.
3. Identify when performance improvements exceed market willingness.
4. Develop lower‑margin offerings that capture the emerging price‑sensitive segment.

**Chapter:** ch07

## Leverage First‑Mover Advantage in New Architecture

**When to use:** When a simple, off‑the‑shelf technology creates a new market.

**Recipe:**

1. Enter the market early with a minimal viable product.
2. Focus on the attributes valued by the new customers (size, price, power).
3. Iterate quickly based on early adopters’ feedback.
4. Scale production once the technology proves viable and demand grows.

**Chapter:** ch09

## Avoid Customer Capture Trap

**When to use:** Incumbents are pressured by existing customers to ignore low‑margin markets.

**Recipe:**

1. Map the influence of current customers on resource allocation.
2. Create a separate decision‑making channel for the new market.
3. Set performance metrics that reward progress in the emerging market, not just existing sales.
4. Periodically review and adjust incentives to keep the new channel independent.

**Chapter:** ch04

## Separate Cost Structures for Low‑Margin Business

**When to use:** When a disruptive product must be profitable at low margins.

**Recipe:**

1. Analyze the cost drivers of the incumbent’s high‑margin model.
2. Redesign processes to reduce fixed costs and increase variable cost efficiency.
3. Adopt lean manufacturing, modular design, and supply‑chain simplification.
4. Validate the new cost structure against target price points before scaling.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## Use Low‑End Entry Point to Open New Markets

**When to use:** When a disruptive technology cannot meet high‑end customer specs.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify a segment that values different attributes (e.g., size, convenience).
2. Design a product that meets those attributes at a lower price.
3. Launch in the low‑end market and use feedback to improve performance.
4. Gradually move upmarket as the technology matures.

**Chapter:** ch09
