# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Disruptive technology** | A new technology that initially serves a low‑margin market but eventually reshapes the industry. | ch02 |
| **Sustaining innovation** | Improvements that extend existing product performance for current customers. | ch02 |
| **Resource dependence** | The way an organization’s resources are tied to its current customers and markets. | ch03 |
| **Autonomous organization** | A separate unit with its own budget, processes, and incentives to pursue a disruptive opportunity. | ch03 |
| **Value network** | The set of relationships that determines how resources flow to and from a firm. | ch05 |
| **Discovery‑based planning** | A planning approach that assumes forecasts are wrong and focuses on learning through experiments. | ch05 |
| **Capability** | An organization’s processes and values that enable it to succeed in a given context. | ch06 |
| **Disability** | The flip side of a capability; a process or value that hinders performance in a new context. | ch06 |
| **Performance trajectory** | The expected path of product improvement over time. | ch07 |
| **S‑curve** | A graph showing how technology performance improves slowly, then rapidly, then levels off. | ch07 |
| **First‑mover advantage** | The benefit of entering a new market early, gaining brand, learning, and network effects. | ch09 |
| **Cost structure** | The mix of fixed and variable costs that determines profitability at different price points. | ch03 |
| **Market capture** | When incumbent customers dictate resource allocation, preventing investment in new markets. | ch04 |
| **Low‑end entry point** | Launching a product at the low‑margin, low‑price end to access a new market. | ch09 |
| **Cannibalization fear** | The concern that a new product will eat sales from existing products, leading to delayed adoption. | ch09 |
| **Technology mudslide hypothesis** | The (incorrect) idea that rapid technological change alone explains incumbent failure. | ch01 |
| **Emerging market** | A small, fast‑growing market that does not yet have reliable size forecasts. | ch04 |
| **Customer capture** | When powerful customers steer a firm’s investment decisions, limiting flexibility. | ch04 |
| **Performance overshoot** | When a product’s capabilities exceed what mainstream customers need, shifting competition to price. | ch07 |
| **Entrant firm** | A company new to an industry that often leads disruptive innovations. | ch09 |
