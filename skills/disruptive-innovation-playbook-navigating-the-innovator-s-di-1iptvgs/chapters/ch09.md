# ch09 — Historical Lessons from Disk Drives

> Synthesizes patterns observed in the disk‑drive industry that explain why entrants win disruptive battles.

Across multiple generations—8‑inch, 5.25‑inch, 3.5‑inch, 2.5‑inch, and 1.8‑inch drives—entrant firms consistently led disruptive innovations. Their advantage stemmed from small size, flexible cost structures, and willingness to serve new applications (e.g., personal computers, laptops). Incumbents lagged because they were captive to existing customers, had entrenched processes, and feared cannibalization. The chapter distills these observations into repeatable patterns for any industry.
