# ch05 — Discovery‑Based Planning

> Presents a planning approach that assumes forecasts are wrong and focuses on learning.

Traditional planning demands quantified market sizes and projected returns before committing resources, a requirement that fails when the market does not yet exist. Discovery‑based planning flips this assumption: managers treat forecasts as likely incorrect and design experiments to learn what customers truly need. By allocating small, reversible investments and iterating based on real feedback, firms can uncover viable business models for disruptive technologies without being paralyzed by uncertainty.
