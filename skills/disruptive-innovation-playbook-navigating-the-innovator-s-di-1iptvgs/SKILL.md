## Core principles

- Great firms fail when they listen only to their most profitable customers and ignore emerging low‑margin markets.
- Disruptive technologies create new markets that cannot be forecast with traditional market‑size analysis.
- Large incumbents cannot allocate resources to small, fast‑growing markets because their cost structures and processes are tuned for high‑margin, high‑volume products.
- An organization’s processes and values are its capabilities; they become disabilities when the problem requires a different way of working.
- Technology supply often outpaces market demand, causing performance overshoot and a shift in the basis of competition.
- Successful response to disruption requires an autonomous unit with a cost structure matched to the new market.
- Discovery‑based planning assumes forecasts are wrong and focuses on learning rather than committing to uncertain projections.
- First‑mover advantage in disruptive markets belongs to entrants, not incumbents, because entrants are not constrained by legacy processes.
- Value networks shape what resources are allocated; changing the network can free a firm to pursue disruptive opportunities.
