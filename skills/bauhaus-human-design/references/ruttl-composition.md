## Dr Non's composition practice

The following method extends this repo's Dao × Palette lessons, not Ruttl's text.
Use [the worksheet](../assets/composition-brief.md) inside the existing design
contract. It is a decision aid, not a new mandatory documentation bureaucracy.

1. **Give attention a job.** State what the person must notice, understand and do.
   Choose the subject that earns the largest area. A map can need most of a viewport;
   a one-word filter cannot. A tiny control still needs a usable hit area.
2. **Account for space.** Name each region's purpose: content, orientation,
   comparison, action or an intentional pause. Use proximity and shared edges
   before introducing boxes. Describe how asymmetry balances actual visual mass.
3. **Build two arrangements, not two costumes.** Keep real content and actions
   fixed. Compare sequencing, adjacency and reading path. Select by the reader's
   task, not by the number of unusual techniques.
4. **Hold the language stable.** Same action, same label and behavior across
   routes. Put invention in the subject, relationships, rhythm and authored
   Palette proportions, inside the project contract. Do not invent obscure navigation.
5. **Change density for a reason.** A question, source note and long explanation
   have different reading speeds. Let type scale, measure and adjacency communicate
   those differences without making every region another card.
6. **Make a promise a control can keep.** Name the action and object. Show result,
   state, failure and recovery. Avoid forced signups, manipulative urgency and
   cosmetic CTAs on reading/operating surfaces. The successful outcome may be
   understanding or finding a safe route, not a click.
7. **Try to disprove authorship.** Replace the product name mentally. If every
   relationship remains interchangeable, identify the missing subject-specific
   decision. An accessible table or familiar back link is not slop merely because
   it is conventional.

## Proof without invented certainty

For an actual UI change, retain screenshots at the house widths 1440, 1024 and
390, with an independent reviewer. Also test prose and ordinary controls at
320 CSS pixels and enlarged text. A genuinely two-dimensional map/table may
retain its own scrolling region; do not exempt the entire interface. Follow
[WCAG reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).

Exercise keyboard focus, long Thai/Chinese labels, deep links, browser back,
no matches, loading, failure and cancellation. Do not claim a fluent-language
review or an older first-time-user test that has not occurred.

For performance, record device/network conditions, representative routes and
measurements. Use [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) for current
metric definitions. Keep field and lab evidence distinct; a fast local render
does not establish real-user performance. Choose byte/interaction budgets for
the project before adding video, fonts or animation, and record any unknowns.

Compare one task-relevant change at a time. With small samples, report observed
hesitations and completion, not an invented A/B winner or causal uplift. Use
statistical experiments only with a suitable design, sufficient traffic and
appropriate privacy safeguards. Ruttl is optional; annotated screenshots and a
task log can carry the same feedback conversation.
