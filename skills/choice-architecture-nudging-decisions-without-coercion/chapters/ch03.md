# ch03 — Loss Aversion and Status‑Quo Bias

> Shows how fear of loss and a preference for the current state create inertia, and how defaults can harness this tendency.

People feel the pain of losing something roughly twice as strongly as the pleasure of gaining the same thing. This loss aversion fuels a status‑quo bias: users stick with existing options unless a change offers a clear gain that outweighs perceived loss. By setting the desired outcome as the default—e.g., automatic enrollment in a retirement plan—designers exploit this bias to increase participation without restricting freedom. The default must be clearly communicated as a choice, not a hidden trap, to stay within ethical bounds.
