# ch10 — Ethical Boundaries: Nudging vs. Manipulation

> Defines the line between helpful nudges and coercive manipulation, emphasizing transparency and choice preservation.

A nudge respects freedom: all options remain available, and the default is presented openly. Manipulation hides alternatives, uses deceptive framing, or exploits biases without regard for welfare. Designers should ask: Is the intent to improve the user’s well‑being? Are the options clearly visible? Is the default justified by evidence of benefit? If the answer to any question is no, the design crosses into manipulation and must be revised.
