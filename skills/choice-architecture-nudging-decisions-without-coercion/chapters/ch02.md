# ch02 — Heuristics that Shape Judgments

> Describes anchoring, availability, and representativeness, showing how they bias estimates and how to use them responsibly as nudges.

Three common heuristics drive biased judgments. Anchoring occurs when people start from a known number and adjust insufficiently, leading to systematic over‑ or under‑estimates. Availability makes vivid or recent examples feel more likely than they are, inflating perceived risks. Representativeness causes people to judge probability by similarity to a stereotype, often ignoring base rates. While these shortcuts can be exploited—e.g., setting donation ranges to anchor higher contributions—ethical design uses them to guide users toward better outcomes, such as presenting realistic risk examples to correct over‑estimation of rare dangers.
