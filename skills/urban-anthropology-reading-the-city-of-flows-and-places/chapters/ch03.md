# ch03 — The Informational City

> Examines how digital networks and the 'space of flows' reshape urban form and elite control.

Modern cities are increasingly defined by electronic networks that allow for instantaneous communication and capital transfer. This creates a 'space of flows' that can seem placeless, yet it remains grounded in physical hardware and specific urban nodes. Elites use these networks to manage global interests, often creating a 'dual city' where high-tech professionals live alongside a service class that maintains the physical infrastructure. While digital tools can democratize information, they also allow for new forms of surveillance and control. Agents should evaluate a city's 'informational intensity'—its speed, clarity, and access—to determine its role in the global hierarchy. Even in a wireless age, the physical location of servers and the people who fix them remain vital.
