# ch01 — The Metaphor of Flow

> Explores how movement defines the modern city. It shifts the focus from static buildings to the circulation of water, traffic, and data.

To understand a city, one must look at its circulation. The concept of flow acts as a generative metaphor for human mobility and the exchange of capital. Urban environments are not just collections of buildings; they are channels for movement. Analyzing these flows requires looking at the technology used, the goals of the people moving, and the constraints that stop them. For example, traffic is as much about the points where movement is restricted as it is about the movement itself. When these flows are interrupted—whether it is water in a pipe or data in a network—the underlying power structures of the city become visible. Agents should look for where these streams converge and where they are blocked to identify the true centers of urban influence.
