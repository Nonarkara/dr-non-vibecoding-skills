# ch05 — Human Mobility and Migration

> Analyzes how the movement of people reshapes urban identity and neighborhood structures.

Migration is a fundamental urban process. People move between cities seeking opportunity, fleeing conflict, or following family ties. These movements are rarely one-way; they often involve complex loops of capital, news, and return visits. In cities, migrants often form enclaves that facilitate trade and provide social support, but these areas are also subject to state control and racialized policing. An agent should look at how migration channels are formed—often through 'paper' kin or informal networks—to understand how a neighborhood evolves. The city is a site where different 'ethnoscapes' meet, creating a constant tension between the desire for settlement and the reality of ongoing mobility.
