# ch07 — Transnational Networks

> How diasporas maintain links across borders, turning cities into global hubs.

Cities are nodes in global networks maintained by diasporic populations. These transnational links allow for the flow of ideas, money, and political influence without the constant mediation of a central state. For example, overseas populations may fund infrastructure in their home countries or use their urban positions to criticize foreign regimes. These networks turn cities into 'imagined places' that exist in the minds of people thousands of miles away. An agent should analyze how these connections bypass formal borders and how they influence local urban politics. The vibrant differences between ethnic enclaves in different global cities show that while the network is global, the local place still matters.
