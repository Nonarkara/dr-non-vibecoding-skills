# ch06 — The Concept of Community

> Deconstructs the use of 'community' as both a descriptive term and a tool for governance.

The word 'community' is almost always used favorably, carrying a sense of warmth and shared concern. However, in urban anthropology, it is a complex and often ambiguous term. It can refer to a physical neighborhood, a group with shared interests, or a specific identity. Governments often use the idea of community to manage diversity or to shift the burden of social services onto local groups. This can mask internal conflicts and inequalities within the group. An agent must be skeptical of the term, asking who is included and who is excluded when 'community' is invoked. It is often used to define 'outsiders' or to romanticize a lost 'golden age' of social stability that may never have existed.
