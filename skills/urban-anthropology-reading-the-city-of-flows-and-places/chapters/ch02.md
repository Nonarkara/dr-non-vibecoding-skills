# ch02 — Infrastructure and Social Power

> Analyzes how basic utilities like water and electricity are used to enforce social hierarchies.

Infrastructure is never neutral. The way water, sewage, and electricity are distributed reflects who the city values. In many urban settings, elites have seamless access to high-quality utilities, while the poor must rely on polluted sources or expensive, informal delivery systems. This 'urbanization of water' shows that nature and society cannot be separated in a city. When a utility is privatized or modernized, it often turns a basic human need into a commodity, creating lethal disparities in health and safety. An agent analyzing a city must map the physical wiring and piping to understand the social power relations. The points where infrastructure fails are often the points where social inequality is most intense.
