# ch04 — Commodity Circuits

> Tracks the global journey of goods to reveal the hidden politics of urban consumption.

Cities rarely produce what they need to survive; they are centers for the collection and redistribution of surplus. By tracking a single commodity, such as sugar or a T-shirt, one can reveal the vast networks of labor and power that sustain urban life. These circuits link rural production sites to urban markets through a series of intermediaries, brokers, and regulators. Cities act as organizing sites for 'growth coalitions'—elites who manage the finance and laws that keep these goods moving. Analyzing these circuits helps an agent see how local urban tastes are connected to global economic shifts. The disposal and recycling of these goods also create secondary markets that define the economic life of the urban poor.
