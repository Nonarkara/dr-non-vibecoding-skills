# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## Mapping the Invisible Flow

**When to use:** When a city is undergoing rapid physical change without an obvious local cause.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the digital and financial networks connected to the area.
2. Locate the 'growth coalition' managing the capital flows.
3. Determine how these abstract flows are being materialized in local buildings or infrastructure.

**Chapter:** ch01

## Infrastructure Power Audit

**When to use:** When evaluating the social impact of a new utility project or privatization.

**Recipe:**

1. Map the physical distribution of the service across different neighborhoods.
2. Identify who is excluded from the network or pays a higher relative cost.
3. Analyze the health and safety outcomes for those at the 'end' of the flow.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## The Community Litmus Test

**When to use:** When the term 'community' is used in policy or political rhetoric.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify who is being defined as an 'outsider' by the speaker.
2. Look for internal inequalities that the term might be masking.
3. Determine if the term is being used to shift government responsibility onto local residents.

**Chapter:** ch06

## Tracing the Commodity

**When to use:** To understand the global footprint of local urban consumption.

**Recipe:**

1. Select a common urban object (e.g., a T-shirt or a food item).
2. Follow its journey from raw material production to the urban store.
3. Identify the labor conditions and political treaties that allow the object to move.

**Chapter:** ch04

## The Flâneur Method

**When to use:** When trying to understand the social reality of a public space.

**Recipe:**

1. Observe the space at different times of day and night.
2. Note which groups (by age, gender, or class) occupy specific areas.
3. Identify the informal rules and barriers that dictate who belongs in the space.

**Chapter:** ch09

---

## Metabolic Resource Analysis

**When to use:** When assessing the sustainability of an urban development.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the primary natural resources flowing into the development.
2. Track where the resulting waste and pollution are directed.
3. Evaluate the social justice of this resource transformation.

**Chapter:** ch10

---

## Digital Divide Assessment

**When to use:** When a city claims to be a 'smart city' or high-tech hub.

**Recipe:**

1. Check for physical areas with limited or no internet access.
2. Identify the 'dual' populations—those who use the tech vs. those who maintain it.
3. Analyze how digital access mirrors existing class or racial boundaries.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## Diasporic Node Mapping

**When to use:** When analyzing the economic or cultural life of an ethnic enclave.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the primary 'home' locations connected to the neighborhood.
2. Track the flow of remittances and news between the nodes.
3. Determine how these global links influence local business and identity.

**Chapter:** ch07
