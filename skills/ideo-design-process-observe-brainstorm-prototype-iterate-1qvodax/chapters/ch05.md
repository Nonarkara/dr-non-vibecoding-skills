# ch05 — Iteration and Feedback Loops

> Test prototypes with users and the team, capture what works and what doesn’t, then refine in rapid cycles. This chapter covers evaluation methods, feedback capture, and deciding when to iterate or pivot.

Iteration is the engine that turns rough ideas into refined solutions. After building a prototype, IDEO teams run structured feedback sessions with representative users, internal experts, and sometimes clients. They observe how users interact with the prototype, note points of confusion, delight, or frustration, and ask open‑ended questions about what they like and dislike. Feedback is captured on sticky notes, photos, or video, then sorted into themes such as ‘works well’, ‘needs improvement’, and ‘confusing’. The team then decides which insights to act on in the next iteration. If a prototype fails to meet a core need, the team may pivot to a different concept; if it shows promise, they refine specific aspects. Each iteration aims to answer a new, more specific question, gradually increasing fidelity. For example, the shopping cart redesign went through multiple rounds: first testing the overall frame shape, then the child seat mechanism, then the checkout hook system. By the final round, the prototype was close to the final product, but the team still made last‑minute tweaks based on user feedback. The key is to keep cycles short—often a day or two—so learning stays fresh and momentum is maintained.
