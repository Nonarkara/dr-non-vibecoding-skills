# ch01 — Deep User Observation

> Learn how to watch people in their natural settings to uncover unspoken needs and behaviors. This chapter covers bug lists, being left‑handed, and why observation beats asking.

Observation is the first step in IDEO’s process. Instead of asking users what they want, designers go to the field and watch what people actually do. They keep a “bug list” of things that frustrate or confuse users, noting workarounds and pain points. The principle of “being left‑handed” reminds designers to set aside their own expertise and look at the world through the user’s eyes, especially when the user differs in age, ability, or context. For example, IDEO designers watched grocery shoppers struggle with carts, noticed parents juggling kids and baskets, and saw professional shoppers using carts as mobile bases. These observations led to insights about safety, child‑friendliness, and checkout flow. Observation also includes listening for the “why?” behind actions, but the focus remains on behavior, not self‑reported opinions. By immersing in the user’s environment, designers discover latent needs that users cannot articulate, such as the desire for a cart that can be steered sideways or a handle that reduces wrist strain. The output of this phase is a set of insight statements that become the foundation for ideation.
