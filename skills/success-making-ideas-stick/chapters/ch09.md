# ch09 — Idea Clinics and Before‑After

> Shows how to improve a weak message by applying the SUCCESs checklist, using before‑and‑after examples to illustrate forced prioritization and concretization.

Idea Clinics are practical exercises that take a weak message and make it stickier by applying the principles from the book. The process mirrors a before‑and‑after photo: you start with the original message, diagnose which SUCCESs traits are missing, then rewrite it to strengthen those traits. For example, a sun‑safety handout that began with a discussion of tanning as a status symbol buried the lead; the revised version opened with the core idea—“Skin damage from overexposure is cumulative and irreversible”—and added a concrete analogy to aging and a vivid image of sunburns as signals of hidden damage. The clinic highlights common weaknesses: missing concreteness (too many abstractions), lack of unexpectedness (no surprise), weak credibility (no authority or details), and flat emotion (no self‑interest or identity hook). By scoring the message on each trait and iteratively improving it, you can transform a forgettable notice into a memorable call to action. The key is forced prioritization: identify the single most important point and make sure it is unmistakable at the start, then build the rest of the message around that core.
