# ch02 — Unexpectedness

> Shows how to capture attention by breaking patterns and creating knowledge gaps that motivate people to seek closure, using the gap theory and surprise.

Unexpectedness works because it violates the audience’s guessing machines. When something does not fit expectations, attention spikes and people stay engaged to resolve the surprise. The gap theory of curiosity says that highlighting a missing piece of information creates a feeling of deprivation that motivates people to fill the gap. To generate unexpectedness, first identify the core message, then ask what is counterintuitive about it. For example, a campaign about heart disease might reveal that “being sedentary is as dangerous as smoking,” flipping a common assumption. Another technique is to break a pattern the audience has learned, such as a nutrition label that lists sugar first instead of last. The surprise must be tied to the core; a random stunt that does not reinforce the message will be remembered but not the point. After gaining attention, hold interest by opening gaps and promising to close them later, keeping the audience curious throughout the message.
