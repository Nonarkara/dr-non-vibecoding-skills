# ch01 — Finding the Core

> Explains how to distill an idea to its most essential element using Commander’s Intent and forced prioritization, with examples from Southwest Airlines and political campaigns.

The core of an idea is its single, most important point—what you would communicate if you could say only one thing. To find it, ask: *If we do nothing else during tomorrow’s mission, we must __________.* and *The single, most important thing that we must do tomorrow is __________.* This mirrors the military’s Commander’s Intent, a crisp statement that aligns action without requiring detailed orders. Southwest Airlines distilled its core to “We are THE low‑fare airline,” which guided every decision from adding a chicken Caesar salad to approving new routes. Bill Clinton’s campaign used “It’s the economy, stupid” as its core, keeping the team focused amid countless distractions. The process requires discarding many valuable insights to let the most important insight shine, much like a designer removing everything non‑essential to achieve elegance. Burying the lead—hiding the core behind interesting but irrelevant details—undermines impact; journalists avoid it by putting the most important fact first. The core must be both **core** (essential) and **compact** (short enough to remember), forming a proverb‑like guide that shapes behavior in uncertain environments.
