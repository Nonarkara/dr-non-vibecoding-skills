# ch05 — Emotion

> Shows how to evoke emotion that motivates action by appealing to self‑interest, identity, and emotions like resentment, without resorting to manipulative fear.

Emotion drives people to care and act, but not all emotions are equally effective. Fear can work, yet it often leads to denial or paralysis unless paired with a clear, actionable solution. More reliable are emotions tied to self‑interest—how the idea affects the audience personally—and identity—how it aligns with who they see themselves as. For example, anti‑smoking campaigns that emphasize teenage resentment toward Big Tobacco’s duplicity outperform those that merely warn of health risks. To craft an emotional appeal, ask: *Does this make people feel something that matters to them?* Then frame the message in terms of the audience’s goals, values, or group identity. A message about energy conservation might highlight “saving money on your bill” (self‑interest) or “being the kind of person who protects the planet” (identity). Emotion also works best when it is paired with a concrete image that lets the audience feel the emotion, such as a photo of a polluted river for an environmental cause. The goal is to move the audience from indifference to caring enough to act.
