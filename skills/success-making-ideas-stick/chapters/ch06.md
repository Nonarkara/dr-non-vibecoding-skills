# ch06 — Stories

> Explains how stories function as mental simulators that prepare people to act, and describes the three basic story plots that make ideas stick.

Stories are powerful because they let the audience mentally rehearse a situation, much like a flight simulator prepares a pilot. When people hear a story, they experience it internally, which builds readiness to respond in real life. Firefighters, for example, swap stories after each fire, expanding their mental catalog of effective responses. To make an idea stick, embed it in one of three story types: the Challenge Plot (underdog triumphs over great odds), the Connection Plot (people bridge a gap to help each other), or the Creativity Plot (someone makes a mental breakthrough that solves a problem). A sticky story often follows a simple structure: introduce a character with a goal, present an obstacle, show the character’s struggle, and reveal the resolution that illustrates the core message. The story should be credible and concrete, with details that make it feel real. Importantly, the story does not need to be elaborate; a brief anecdote can serve as a mental simulation. By telling a story that lets the audience see themselves acting on the idea, you increase the likelihood they will do so when the moment arises.
