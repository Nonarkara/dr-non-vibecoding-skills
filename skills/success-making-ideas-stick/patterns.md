# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## Find the Core via Commander's Intent

**When to use:** When you need to distill a complex idea into its essence for clear communication.

**Recipe:**

1. Ask: If we do nothing else, we must __________.
2. Ask: The single most important thing we must do is __________.
3. Write the answer as a short, plain‑spoken sentence.
4. Test it: Does this sentence guide decisions in uncertain situations?

## Create Unexpectedness with a Gap

**When to use:** When you need to capture and hold attention for a message.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the core message.
2. Determine what is counterintuitive or surprising about it.
3. Open with a question or statement that highlights the missing information.
4. Promise to close the gap later in the message.

**Chapter:** ch02

## Make an Idea Concrete

**When to use:** When the message relies on abstractions that the audience struggles to grasp.

**Recipe:**

1. Replace abstract nouns with specific, sensory details.
2. Ask what the audience can see, hear, touch, taste, or smell.
3. Add a vivid example or image that illustrates the point.
4. Check that a novice can picture the scene clearly.

**Chapter:** ch03

## Build Credibility with Anti‑Authority

**When to use:** When the audience distrusts traditional experts or authorities.

**Recipe:**

1. Find someone who has lived experience relevant to the idea.
2. Let them tell their story in their own words.
3. Highlight their lack of formal authority to increase trustworthiness.
4. Pair the testimony with concrete details that can be verified.

**Chapter:** ch04

## Appeal to Emotion via Identity

**When to use:** When you want the audience to care because the idea reflects who they are.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the audience’s self‑definition (e.g., ‘I am a responsible parent’).
2. Frame the idea as an expression of that identity.
3. Show how acting on the idea reinforces who they want to be.
4. Avoid fear‑only appeals; pair emotion with a clear action.

**Chapter:** ch05

## Tell a Challenge Plot Story

**When to use:** When you need to inspire perseverance and effort.

**Recipe:**

1. Introduce a protagonist facing a significant obstacle.
2. Show the protagonist’s struggle and effort over time.
3. Reveal the outcome where the protagonist succeeds despite the odds.
4. Tie the outcome directly to the core message.

## Use a Schema to Explain a New Concept

**When to use:** When you need to teach something unfamiliar quickly.

**Recipe:**

1. Find a familiar concept the audience already knows.
2. Describe the new concept as a modification of that familiar concept.
3. Specify only what is different or added.
4. Verify that the audience can grasp the idea with minimal effort.

## Apply the SUCCESs Checklist Iteratively

**When to use:** When revising a message to increase its stickiness.

**Recipe:**

1. Score the message on each of the six traits (Simple, Unexpected, etc.).
2. Identify the weakest trait and rewrite to strengthen it.
3. Repeat scoring until all traits are adequately present.
4. Test the final version with an unfamiliar audience for recall and action.

**Chapter:** ch10

## Avoid Burying the Lead

**When to use:** When opening any piece of communication to ensure immediate impact.

**Recipe:**

1. Write down the core message in one sentence.
2. Place that sentence at the very beginning.
3. Remove any preceding interesting but irrelevant details.
4. Check that a reader who stops after the first sentence still gets the point.

**Chapter:** ch09

## Test for the Curse of Knowledge

**When to use:** When you need to verify that your message is understandable to novices.

**Recipe:**

1. Find someone who lacks your expertise on the topic.
2. Explain the idea using your draft message.
3. Ask them to restate the core in their own words.
4. If they struggle, simplify and concretize until they grasp it easily.

**Chapter:** ch07
