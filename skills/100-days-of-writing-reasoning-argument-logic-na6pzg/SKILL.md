## Core principles

- Question traditions and norms that lack a clear productive logic.
- Use strict constraints in expression to force complex thoughts into a clear rhythm.
- Recognize that state-driven stigmas are often tools of biopower used to control populations.
- Acknowledge that all observation is reflexive; when you describe a field, you describe yourself.
- Distinguish between the anxiety of being unwanted and the freedom of being alone.
- Accept weakness as a shared human condition rather than a flaw to be hidden.
- Use pragmatic busyness to prevent the mind from wandering into non-productive despair.

## The Unimaginable Counter-Example

**When to use:** When someone claims a social norm is a universal necessity.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the core assumption (e.g., 'Society needs fathers').
2. Search for anthropological or historical exceptions (e.g., the Na people).
3. Analyze how the exception functions without the 'necessary' element.
4. Use the existence of the exception to dismantle the claim of universality.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## The Murakami Syntax Constraint

**When to use:** When trying to clarify complex or disjointed thoughts.

**Recipe:**

1. Strip away all complex vocabulary and jargon.
2. Write only in simple, short sentences.
3. Focus on the rhythm created by the sequence of sentences.
4. Let the connections between ideas emerge naturally from the simplified structure.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## Biopower Deconstruction

**When to use:** When feeling social anxiety due to a label or stigma.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the label being applied (e.g., 'leftover').
2. Determine which institution or authority benefits from this label.
3. Look for the 'productive logic'—is it for your benefit or the state's?
4. Recognize the label as an external tool of control to neutralize its emotional impact.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## Pragmatic Grief Management

**When to use:** When facing overwhelming emotional despair or loss.

**Recipe:**

1. Fill the schedule with immediate, concrete tasks (rituals, logistics).
2. Focus on 'what works' in the immediate moment rather than long-term meaning.
3. Use physical activity (like biking or cleaning) to ground the mind.
4. Allow the routine to provide a safety net that prevents the mind from wandering into the abyss.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## Reflexive Fieldwork

**When to use:** When observing a new environment or person.

**Recipe:**

1. Note your initial impressions and emotional reactions.
2. Ask: 'What from my past does this remind me of?'
3. Identify any 'uncanny' biases based on previous experiences or media.
4. Adjust your description to account for your own subjective history.

**Chapter:** ch05

## Cheatsheet

# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## process

- If a tradition lacks a productive logic, you are not obligated to follow it.
- If a task feels overwhelming, focus on the immediate pragmatic step in front of you.

## general

- Anxiety is often a tool used by the state to make you a 'stable' citizen.

## writing

- To find the truth in a complex thought, write it in the simplest possible English.
- Your description of the world is always a description of yourself.

## design

- Don't mistake the 'uncanny' feeling of familiarity for a genuine connection.

## life

- Loneliness is the only time you are truly free from social judgment.
- When despair hits, stay busy with pragmatic tasks until the mind stabilizes.
- Weakness is the one thing all humans share; don't be afraid to show it.
- The best way to avoid being taken for granted is to not reply to messages instantly.

## argument

- If you are going to conform, do it honestly; don't pretend to be a rebel while obeying.
- A society without fathers exists; therefore, the nuclear family is a choice, not a law of nature.
