# ch09 — Incentive Super-Response

> Discusses how rewards drive behavior in unexpected ways. Offers a method for predicting behavior by following incentives.

People respond to incentives with incredible speed and radical changes in behavior, often ignoring the 'intent' behind the reward. This is the incentive super-response tendency. For example, if you pay a lawyer by the hour, they are incentivized to make the case take as long as possible. If you offer a bounty for dead rats, people will start breeding rats to collect the reward. To understand why a person or organization is acting strangely, look at the reward structure. Most 'irrational' behavior is actually perfectly rational if you see what the person is being paid to do. Rule: Never ask a barber if you need a haircut; instead, ask what they are incentivized to tell you.
