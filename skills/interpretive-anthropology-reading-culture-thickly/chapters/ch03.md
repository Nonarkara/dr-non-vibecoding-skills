# ch03 — Emic vs. Etic: Two Lenses on Meaning

> Clarifies the distinction between insider (emic) and outsider (etic) viewpoints and why both are needed for balanced interpretation.

The emic perspective records the meanings that participants themselves articulate. It is the voice of the actor, expressed in native categories, idioms, and metaphors. The etic perspective, by contrast, is the analytical stance of the observer, using comparative concepts and theoretical categories. Geertz warns that relying solely on the etic can strip actions of their lived significance, while an exclusively emic account may lack the analytical distance needed to compare across cultures. A robust interpretation therefore weaves both lenses: the AI first gathers the emic explanation, then situates it within an etic framework that can relate it to other cultural cases without imposing a universal formula.
