# ch05 — The Actor’s Point of View

> Emphasizes the importance of foregrounding the actor’s own interpretation before drawing broader conclusions.

Geertz insists that any cultural analysis must begin with the actor’s point of view. This means presenting the participant’s own words, metaphors, and justifications before the interpreter adds any theoretical overlay. For AI, this translates into a rule: *before generalising, give the actor’s view first.* The system should store the raw emic statements as primary data, then annotate them with analytic tags. Only after this hierarchy is respected does the AI move to comparative or predictive modeling. This practice preserves the authenticity of the cultural signal and guards against the temptation to force data into pre‑existing categories.
