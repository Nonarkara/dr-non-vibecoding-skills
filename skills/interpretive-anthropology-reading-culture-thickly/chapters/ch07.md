# ch07 — Avoiding Reductionism

> Warns against collapsing a culture into a single pattern or law, and offers strategies to keep interpretations pluralistic.

Reductionism occurs when an interpreter extracts a single rule from a complex cultural scene and treats it as universally applicable. Geertz shows that such shortcuts erase the multiplicity of meanings that coexist in the web of significance. To avoid this, the AI should always retain multiple layers of explanation: the immediate emic meaning, the symbolic network, and the broader cultural narrative. When a pattern emerges, it must be presented as a *possible* regularity, not a definitive law, and always qualified by contextual boundaries. This pluralistic stance preserves the integrity of the cultural description.
