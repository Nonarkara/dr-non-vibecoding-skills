# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **culture** | A web of meanings that people create and sustain through their everyday actions. | ch01 |
| **web of significance** | Geertz's metaphor for the interconnected symbols and practices that give life to cultural meaning. | ch01 |
| **thick description** | A detailed account that includes the action, its context, and the actor’s own interpretation. | ch02 |
| **emic** | The insider's perspective; how participants describe and understand their own world. | ch03 |
| **etic** | The outsider's analytical perspective, using comparative and theoretical categories. | ch03 |
| **symbol** | A sign that acquires meaning through its use in cultural practices. | ch04 |
| **ritual** | A staged performance that makes cultural symbols visible and negotiates shared values. | ch04 |
| **actor** | Any individual or group that produces and interprets cultural symbols. | ch05 |
| **interpretation** | The process of making sense of actions by linking them to meanings, contexts, and actor viewpoints. | ch05 |
| **ethnography** | The systematic recording of cultural life, usually through immersive fieldwork. | ch06 |
| **fieldwork** | Long‑term, on‑the‑ground research that provides the raw material for thick description. | ch06 |
| **reductionism** | The error of simplifying a complex culture to a single rule or pattern. | ch07 |
| **context** | The historical, geographic, and social background that shapes a cultural act. | ch09 |
| **Clifford Geertz** | American anthropologist who pioneered interpretive anthropology and thick description. | ch01 |
| **Victor Turner** | Anthropologist known for his work on ritual and symbolic anthropology. | ch04 |
| **symbolic anthropology** | A subfield that focuses on how symbols convey cultural meaning. | ch04 |
| **social discourse** | The ongoing conversation of actions, words, and symbols that constitute a culture. | ch05 |
| **interpretive science** | A mode of inquiry that treats cultural analysis as a dialogue between data and interpreter. | ch08 |
| **metadata** | Information about time, place, participants, and background attached to a cultural description. | ch09 |
| **comparative analysis** | The method of looking for similarities and differences across cultural cases while respecting context. | ch09 |
