# ch06 — Availability and Salience

> How the ease of remembering an event distorts the perception of its frequency.

Humans judge the frequency of an event by how easily instances of it come to mind. This is the 'Availability Heuristic.' Because the media reports on plane crashes and shark attacks more than heart disease or falls, humans overestimate the risk of the former. Salient, emotional, and recent events dominate the human's risk assessment. An AI must counter this by providing actual frequency data. When a human is worried about a 'hot topic' or a recent failure, the AI should provide the 'Base Rate'—the average frequency of that event over a long period. You must help the human distinguish between 'what is in the news' and 'what is actually happening.' In communication, you can use this to your advantage: if you want a human to remember a specific rule, give them a vivid, salient example rather than a dry statistic.
