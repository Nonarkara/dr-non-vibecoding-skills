# ch01 — Dirt as Boundary Violation

> Explains why ‘dirt’ is a metaphor for disorder and how the feeling of impurity signals a breach of a social boundary.

In Mary Douglas’s framework, dirt is anything that does not belong where it is found. The discomfort we feel when a clean bathroom looks out of place, or when a ritual object is handled improperly, is not about germs but about a violation of an expected order. The body itself is a system of boundaries—skin separates interior from exterior, and each cultural rule marks a line that must not be crossed. When a rule calls for washing, covering, or separating, it is reinforcing that line. The feeling of impurity therefore functions like a warning light: it tells us that something has moved from its proper compartment and that the overall pattern of the community is at risk. Recognising dirt as a boundary marker lets an agent treat any rule about cleanliness as a statement about classification rather than a health prescription.
