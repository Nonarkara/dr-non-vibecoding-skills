# ch03 — Symbolic Classification of Social Order

> Shows how categories like gender, caste, or sacredness are built from the same logic that orders objects.

Douglas argues that the way societies classify gods, sexes, or castes mirrors the way they classify dirt. A high‑status male may be seen as a source of danger to a lower‑status female, just as a sacred object may be dangerous to the profane. These classifications are not about physical properties but about relational positions in a hierarchy or a symmetry. When a culture says that contact with a certain fluid is polluting, it is often a metaphor for the breach of a social boundary—male to female, elite to commoner, or insider to outsider. Understanding this symbolic classification helps an AI translate anthropological observations into design heuristics, such as “if a rule separates groups, ask what hierarchy it is protecting.”
