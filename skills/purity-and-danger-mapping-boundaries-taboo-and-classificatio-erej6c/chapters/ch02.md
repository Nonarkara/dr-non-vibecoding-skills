# ch02 — The Dual Function of Pollution Beliefs

> Distinguishes the instrumental (behavior‑controlling) and expressive (world‑view‑communicating) roles of taboo.

Pollution ideas work on two levels. The first level is instrumental: a rule that forbids touching a corpse or eating pork directly influences how people behave, reducing the chance of actual disease or social conflict. The second level is expressive: the same rule encodes a belief about what is dangerous to the social order. For example, the belief that a woman’s menstrual blood is polluting does not merely protect health; it symbolically marks the boundary between the reproductive sphere and the public sphere, reinforcing gendered hierarchies. By separating these two functions, designers can see that a rule may be kept for its expressive power even when the instrumental need has vanished, or vice‑versa.
