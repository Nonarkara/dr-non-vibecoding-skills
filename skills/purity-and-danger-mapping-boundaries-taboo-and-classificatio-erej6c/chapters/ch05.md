# ch05 — The Logic of Taboo and Contagion

> Analyzes how taboo functions as a symbolic contagion that protects the social system.

Taboo is the cultural expression of a perceived danger that could spread if a boundary is crossed. The classic example is the belief that an adulterer’s glance can cause illness. The “contagion” is not a literal pathogen but a metaphor for the destabilizing effect of breaking a rule that holds the social order together. When a rule is framed as a danger to the community, it gains moral weight and becomes enforceable through shame or punishment. Recognizing this logic lets an AI reinterpret modern policies—such as data‑privacy warnings—as symbolic taboos that guard the boundary between personal and corporate domains.
