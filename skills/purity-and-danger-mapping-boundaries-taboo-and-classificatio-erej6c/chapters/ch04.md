# ch04 — Ritual Purity as Social Cohesion

> Explains how purity rituals create a shared sense of order and identity.

Purity rituals are not random acts of washing; they are coordinated performances that make the invisible social order visible. By collectively bathing before a meal, a community publicly demonstrates that everyone respects the same boundary. The ritual turns an abstract classification into a concrete, repeatable action, reinforcing group identity. Even when the ritual seems excessive—such as multiple daily baths in a Brahmin community—it serves to constantly remind participants of their place in the hierarchy. Designers can harness this principle by creating onboarding flows or product rituals that repeatedly signal a user’s role within a system, thereby strengthening loyalty.
