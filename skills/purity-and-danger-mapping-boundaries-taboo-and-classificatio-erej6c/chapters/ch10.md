# ch10 — Applying the Model to Design and Research

> Shows how to use the boundary framework in user research, product design, and conflict resolution.

In product design, a rule that a user must verify identity before accessing a feature is a boundary protecting personal data. The symbolic meaning—trust, privacy, authority—can be reinforced through visual cues or onboarding rituals. In user research, noticing that participants avoid a certain UI element may indicate an implicit taboo; probing the perceived danger reveals the underlying boundary. In conflict resolution, identifying the boundary each side protects (e.g., cultural identity vs. economic security) helps negotiate compromises that respect both sides’ symbolic needs. By consistently applying the boundary question, designers turn abstract anthropological concepts into concrete, actionable insights.
