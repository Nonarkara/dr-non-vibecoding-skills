# ch07 — From Hygiene to Symbolic Order

> Shows how practical hygiene and symbolic purity intersect and diverge.

Sometimes a hygiene rule and a symbolic rule coincide, such as washing hands before meals, which both prevents disease and signals respect for social order. Other times they diverge: a ritual that bans touching a sacred statue may have no health benefit but still enforces a boundary. The key is to ask whether the rule’s primary purpose is to keep something in its proper place. If the answer is yes, the rule can be treated as a symbolic boundary; if not, it may be a pragmatic health measure. Designers can thus decide whether to keep, adapt, or discard a rule based on its boundary function.
