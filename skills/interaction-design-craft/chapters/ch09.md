# ch09 — From Research Prototype to Shipping Product: PARC to Apple

> The technologies invented at Xerox PARC—mouse, icons, overlapping windows, modeless editing—were not copied wholesale; Apple teams reinterpreted them through their own constraints and user goals, producing the Lisa and Macintosh.

When Steve Jobs negotiated access to PARC’s technology, he obtained the right to use the mouse and certain concepts, but Apple’s engineers did not simply replicate the Alto or Star. Bill Atkinson explicitly said they started a fresh research project asking, ‘What is the best user interface for this whole thing?’ They kept what worked (the mouse, windows) but re‑imagined others: overlapping windows were inspired by a mis‑seen glimpse at PARC but implemented via a novel QuickDraw trick that was far faster; the trash can went through several iterations before the final clean icon; the menu bar was a new synthesis of the idea of visible commands. The Apple team also brought in new influences—Atkinson’s background in neuro‑psychology and photography, Tesler’s usability focus—leading to a design language that felt both powerful and approachable. The result was not a clone but a new personal‑computer interaction model that set the standard for the next decade.
