# ch06 — Long‑Term Compounding

> Shows why time in the market beats timing the market, and how compounding turns modest gains into substantial wealth over decades.

Graham’s own results—about 17 % annualized over a period that included the Crash and the Depression—demonstrate the power of staying invested through cycles. Attempting to time the market often leads to missed opportunities and transaction costs that erode returns. Instead, a disciplined approach of buying undervalued issues with a margin of safety and holding them until the market reflects their value (or until fundamentals deteriorate) lets compounding work. The agent should encourage holding periods of years, not months, and reinvest dividends or proceeds into new bargains rather than chasing short‑term spikes.
