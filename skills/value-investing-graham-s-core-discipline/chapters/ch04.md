# ch04 — Investing versus Speculating

> Provides Graham’s precise definition of investing and shows how to test any proposed operation against it. Highlights the importance of thorough analysis, safety of principal, and adequate return.

According to Graham, an investment operation is one which, upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and an adequate return. Anything that fails this test is speculation. Thorough analysis means examining the company’s financial statements, understanding its business model, and estimating intrinsic value with a margin of safety. Safety of principal means the risk of loss is small; adequate return means a return commensurate with the risk taken, not a gamble on price momentum. The agent should run a checklist: Is the analysis thorough? Does the price offer a margin of safety? Is the expected return based on earnings or asset yield, not on hope of a higher resale price? If any answer is no, the operation is speculative.
