# ch07 — Emotion

> Designs that evoke more emotion feel simpler than those that feel flat. This chapter explains how to layer emotional cues—warmth, surprise, delight—onto a reduced form to increase its perceived simplicity.

Emotion acts as a cognitive shortcut: when users feel something positive, they are more tolerant of minor complexities and more likely to describe the experience as simple. Add emotional resonance through color, texture, sound, or micro‑interactions that trigger joy, curiosity, or comfort. A product that is merely functional can feel cold; the same product with a warm glow, a pleasant click, or a friendly tone feels approachable and thus simpler. However, avoid overloading—emotion should support, not obscure, the core function. Test whether the emotional element enhances understanding or merely distracts. When balanced, emotion makes the user feel that the design ‘gets them,’ which reduces the mental effort required to use it.
