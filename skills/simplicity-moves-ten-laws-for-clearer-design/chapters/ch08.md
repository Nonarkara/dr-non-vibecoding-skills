# ch08 — Trust

> People trust simple designs when they behave predictably and reliably. This chapter shows how to build trust through consistency, transparency, and reliable performance.

Trust grows when a system does what it says it will do, every time. Keep interactions consistent: the same button should always produce the same outcome, and navigation should not change unexpectedly. Be transparent about what is happening—show progress, explain delays, and admit limits rather than hiding failures. Reliability can be demonstrated through durable materials, solid construction, or dependable software updates. When users know they can rely on a design to work without surprises, they perceive it as simple even if it contains sophisticated internals. Conversely, erratic behavior erodes trust and makes the same design feel complicated and risky. Build trust by making the design’s behavior obvious, repeatable, and honest.
