# ch02 — Organize

> Organization makes a system of many appear fewer by grouping items into meaningful categories. This chapter introduces the slip method (sort, label, integrate, prioritize), the tab key for visual alignment, Gestalt principles, and the iPod’s evolution as concrete ways to impose order.

When faced with many items, begin by sorting them into piles based on similarity—write each item on a note and move them around until natural clusters emerge. Label each pile with a name or code; if you cannot agree on a name, use a letter or color. Integrate piles that are truly alike to reduce the total number of groups. Finally, prioritize: pull the vital few items into a top‑level set so they get the most attention, applying the 80/20 rule as a guide. Beyond slip, use the tab key to align information in rows and columns, turning a messy list into a clear table. Apply Gestalt thinking: the mind likes to see a whole even when it is made of parts, so arrange elements so they visually group together. Observe how the iPod controls moved from a scattered row to a seamless wheel, showing how organization can cycle from simple to complex and back to simple. The goal is not to eliminate items but to make the collection feel manageable through clear, repeatable groupings.
