# ch03 — Time

> Saving time or making time feel shorter creates a sense of simplicity. This chapter shows how to shrink time constraints, hide the passage of time, and embody time with feedback, using examples like the iPod shuffle, progress bars, and styling cues.

When a task feels slow, first look for steps you can remove entirely—shrink the time needed. The iPod shuffle exemplifies this by removing the display and letting the device pick a random song, eliminating the need to scroll. If you cannot cut steps, hide the passage of time: remove clocks, windows, or any indicator that makes users aware of waiting, as casinos do, or show a progress bar that moves smoothly so the wait feels shorter. Finally, embody time by giving clear feedback about how much remains—numerical countdowns, filling bars, or stylistic cues like streamlined shapes that suggest speed. Styling (e.g., fins on a toaster) does not make the object faster but creates the illusion of motion, which users interpret as saving time. Remember the trade‑off: making something truly faster often costs more, while making the wait feel shorter can be cheap and effective. Choose the approach that costs least to implement while delivering the desired sense of temporal simplicity.
