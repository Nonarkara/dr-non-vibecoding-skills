# ch08 — Public Space as a Moral Arena

> Viewing public space as a site of conflict between different social and moral rules.

Public spaces are not just places to walk; they are 'moral spaces' where different urban orders clash. An 'urban order' is a set of formal and informal rules that dictate who belongs in a space and what they can do there. Conflicts in public space—such as the removal of street vendors or the gating of a park—are privileged moments for understanding a city's true values. These disputes reveal the 'competing moralities' of the middle class, the state, and the poor. When an agent evaluates a conflict over public space, it should identify the underlying 'moral claims' being made by each side, such as the right to work versus the right to a 'clean' aesthetic.
