# ch10 — Inequality and Symbolic Boundaries

> The role of invisible social borders in maintaining urban segregation and inequality.

Urban inequality is produced through more than just income gaps; it is maintained through 'symbolic boundaries.' These are the invisible lines that tell certain people they do not belong in certain places. In cities like Buenos Aires, the 'city proper' is often contrasted with the 'suburbs' or 'slums,' which are seen as sites of decomposition. When poor people enter the city center to work, they are trespassing these symbolic borders. This creates a 'dual city' where the formal and informal exist side-by-side but are governed by different rules. To understand urban inequality, an agent must map these symbolic boundaries and see how they are reinforced by police, architecture, and social stigma.
