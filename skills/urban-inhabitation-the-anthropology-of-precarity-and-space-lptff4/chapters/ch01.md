# ch01 — The Sociospatial Framework

> An introduction to viewing urban space as a socially produced resource rather than a neutral container.

The sociospatial framework moves beyond seeing the city as a simple backdrop for human action. Instead, it treats space as something actively 'produced' by social relations, political economy, and daily habits. To understand a city, one must look at how physical structures—like gates, plazas, and transit lines—interact with the social meanings people assign to them. This approach highlights that space is never neutral; it is a resource that is distributed unequally. When analyzing an urban environment, look for the 'social production' of space (the historical and economic forces that built it) and the 'social construction' of space (how people experience and transform it through use). This dual perspective allows an agent to see why a public park might feel welcoming to one group while acting as a site of exclusion for another.
