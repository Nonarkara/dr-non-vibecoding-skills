# ch05 — Harvesting the City

> The process of turning urban waste and social gaps into resources for survival.

Harvesting is the practice of recuperating 'waste'—whether that waste is physical materials, discarded cultural memories, or the fuzzy gaps between formal institutions. Unlike simple scavenging, harvesting involves bundling these remainders into new circuits of exchange. For example, a group of youth might use their diverse religious or class backgrounds to move through different parts of a divided city, 'putting stuff together' that others cannot see. Harvesting relies on keeping these connections under the radar to avoid formal regulation. In an urban analysis, look for 'fallow' spaces or 'wasted' lives that are being repurposed into informal economies that the official city map does not recognize.
