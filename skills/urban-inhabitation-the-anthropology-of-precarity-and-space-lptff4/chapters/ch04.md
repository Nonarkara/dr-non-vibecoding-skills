# ch04 — The Temporality of Waiting and Accelerating

> How urban inhabitants use different speeds of action to manage risk and opportunity.

Time in the city is experienced through two speeds: waiting and accelerating. Waiting is not just passive; it is a mode of 'captivation' where individuals position themselves in a place where they anticipate a future opportunity will arrive. Accelerating is the move to seize an opportunity quickly and move on before a situation becomes stagnant or dangerous. Many urban youth refuse to stick with low-paying work for long, preferring to 'make their move' and circulate through the city. This circulation is supported by cheap, temporary housing and informal networks. When analyzing urban behavior, distinguish between 'stagnant waiting' (forced by the state) and 'strategic waiting' (positioning oneself for a coming shift).
