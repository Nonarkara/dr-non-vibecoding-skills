# ch05 — Temperament over Intellect

> Explains why emotional discipline is the investor’s chief challenge and how to detect temperament‑driven decisions such as FOMO, panic, or overconfidence.

Graham observed that many intelligent people lose money because they cannot control their emotions. The market’s fluctuations trigger fear and greed, leading to buying high in euphoria and selling low in panic. Temperament—the ability to stay calm, stick to a pre‑defined plan, and act contrary to the crowd—is more important than raw IQ. The agent should monitor internal signals: urgency to act without analysis, regret after a trade, or justification based on ‘this time is different.’ When such signals appear, the agent should pause, revisit the intrinsic value estimate, and enforce the margin‑of‑safety rule before proceeding.
