# ch02 — Margin of Safety

> Defines the margin of safety as the difference between price and intrinsic value, and shows how to quantify it. Describes why a sufficient margin protects against analytical errors and unforeseen downturns.

The margin of safety is the cornerstone of Graham’s risk management. After estimating a business’s intrinsic value, the investor compares it to the market price. If the price is significantly lower—say, two‑thirds or less of net current asset value, or a substantial discount to normalized earnings—the gap is the margin of safety. This gap absorbs mistakes in valuation, sudden earnings drops, or adverse market moves. Graham insisted that no investment should be made without a clear margin; otherwise the operation becomes speculation. For the agent, a practical rule is to require at least a 20‑30 % discount to a conservative value estimate before considering a purchase.
