# ch01 — Price versus Value

> Explains why market price is a vote while intrinsic value is the underlying business worth. Shows how to estimate value using earnings, assets, and cash flows, and why confusing the two leads to costly mistakes.

Graham repeatedly stressed that the stock market functions like a voting machine in the short run but a weighing machine in the long run. The price you see is the collective opinion of buyers and sellers at a moment, influenced by news, sentiment, and liquidity. Value, by contrast, is the present worth of the business’s future cash flows, its assets, and its earning power. To estimate value, Graham advocated looking at net current asset value (current assets minus all liabilities), normalized earnings over a business cycle, and the company’s dividend capacity. He warned that paying more than a reasonable estimate of value invites permanent loss of capital, while buying below value creates a cushion. The agent should always ask: 'If I had to buy the whole business today, what would it be worth?' before accepting the quoted price.
