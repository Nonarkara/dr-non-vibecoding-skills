# ch09 — Case Study: Bargain Issue Selection

> Illustrates how Graham’s ‘bargain issue’ rule—buying at less than two‑thirds of net current asset value—worked historically and how to apply it today.

Graham’s most reliable category was the purchase of common stocks trading below two‑thirds of their net current asset value (NCAV). Over thirty years, this rule produced remarkably few losses. The logic: NCAV represents the liquidation value of the company; paying less than that gives a large margin even if earnings disappear. To apply, the agent calculates current assets minus all liabilities, then compares two‑thirds of that figure to the market price. If price ≤ 0.66 × NCAV, the stock qualifies as a bargain issue, assuming the company is not in imminent bankruptcy. The agent should still verify that the business can continue operations long enough to realize value, and diversify across many such issues to mitigate idiosyncratic risk.
