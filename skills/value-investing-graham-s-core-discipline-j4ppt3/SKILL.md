## Core principles

- Price is what you pay; value is what you get, and the investor must always estimate the latter before accepting the former.
- A margin of safety—the gap between price and estimated value—protects against error and uncertainty.
- Mr. Market offers daily prices; the wise investor ignores his mood and acts only when the offer suits their valuation.
- Investing promises safety of principal and an adequate return after thorough analysis; speculation lacks this promise.
- Temperament, not intellect, determines investment success; emotional discipline outweighs IQ.
- Compounding over time beats market timing; staying invested through cycles yields the best results.
- Graham's methods were forged in a less‑efficient market; modern agents must adapt while keeping the underlying principles.
- Honest self‑appraisal and rigorous record‑keeping prevent self‑deception and improve future decisions.
- Diversification across many undervalued issues reduces idiosyncratic risk without diluting potential gain.
- Continuous learning from both successes and mistakes is the investor's most reliable edge.

## Net Current Asset Value Screen

**When to use:** When seeking a large margin of safety in equities.

**Recipe:**

1. Calculate current assets minus total liabilities to obtain NCAV.
2. Multiply NCAV by two‑thirds to get the bargain threshold.
3. Compare the stock’s market price to this threshold.
4. If price ≤ threshold, flag the stock as a candidate bargain issue.
5. Verify the company is not facing imminent bankruptcy before proceeding.

**Chapter:** ch09

---

## Margin‑of‑Safety Gate

**When to use:** Before committing capital to any security.

**Recipe:**

1. Estimate a conservative intrinsic value using earnings, assets, or cash flow.
2. Compute margin of safety as (value − price) / value.
3. Check whether the margin meets the pre‑set minimum (e.g., 20 %).
4. If yes, proceed to temperament check; if no, reject or look for a cheaper alternative.
5. Record the margin and the assumptions used.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## Mr. Market Ignore Rule

**When to use:** When daily price quotes trigger emotional reactions.

**Recipe:**

1. Note the current market price offered by Mr. Market.
2. Recall your own independent estimate of intrinsic value.
3. If the offer is far above your estimate, treat it as a selling opportunity only if you already hold the stock.
4. If the offer is far below your estimate, treat it as a buying opportunity only if you have margin of safety.
5. Otherwise, ignore the quote and wait for a more favorable offer.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## Investing vs Speculating Checklist

**When to use:** When evaluating a proposed trade or strategy.

**Recipe:**

1. Confirm that thorough analysis of financials and business model has been performed.
2. Verify that the price offers a margin of safety relative to intrinsic value.
3. Ensure the expected return is based on earnings, dividends, or asset yield, not solely on price appreciation hopes.
4. If all three are satisfied, label the operation an investment; otherwise, label it speculation.
5. Document the reasoning for future review.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## Temperament Pause

**When to use:** When feeling urgency, fear, or excitement about a trade.

**Recipe:**

1. Stop and name the emotion driving the urge (e.g., FOMO, panic, overconfidence).
2. Re‑read your investment checklist and intrinsic value estimate.
3. Ask whether the trade would still make sense if the market were closed for a month.
4. If the answer is no, refrain from trading and revisit the decision later.
5. If the answer is yes, proceed only after confirming margin of safety and analysis.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## Long‑Term Holding Plan

**When to use:** After purchasing a security with a margin of safety.

**Recipe:**

1. Set a target holding period of at least three‑five years unless fundamentals change.
2. Plan to reinvest dividends or proceeds into new bargain issues rather than spending them.
3. Schedule quarterly reviews of earnings, debt, and competitive position.
4. Sell only if price rises to intrinsic value, margin erodes, or fundamentals deteriorate significantly.
5. Keep a journal of reasons for holding or selling each position.

**Chapter:** ch06

---

## Era‑Adaptation Review

**When to use:** When applying Graham’s principles to modern markets.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify which of Graham’s specific tactics (e.g., NCAV bargains) are still prevalent.
2. Adjust valuation models to incorporate modern data sources and faster information flow.
3. Keep the core principles—price vs value, margin of safety, temperament—unchanged.
4. Test any adapted rule on historical data before live use.
5. Continuously monitor market structure and recalibrate thresholds as needed.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## Decision Journaling

**When to use:** After every investment action (buy, sell, or hold).

**Recipe:**

1. Write a short note describing the estimated intrinsic value and the price paid/received.
2. Record the margin of safety at the time of the decision.
3. Note any temperament checks performed and their outcome.
4. State the reason for the action (e.g., price reached value, fundamentals worsened).
5. Review the journal periodically to identify patterns of error or success.

## Cheatsheet

# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## code

- If price is more than two‑thirds of net current asset value, look for a cheaper alternative.
- Never buy a stock without a documented margin of safety of at least 20 %.
- Use normalized earnings over a full business cycle to avoid being misled by temporary spikes.
- If a company’s debt exceeds its current assets, treat it with extra caution regardless of price.

## general

- When Mr. Market offers a high price, ask: would I sell my business at this level?
- When Mr. Market offers a low price, ask: would I buy the whole business at this level?
- Label any operation that lacks thorough analysis, safety of principal, or adequate return as speculation.
- When markets are euphoric, remember that high prices often reflect optimism, not value.
- When markets are depressed, remember that low prices often create bargains, not permanent loss.
- Never confuse a rising stock price with proof of a good investment; verify the underlying value.
- When in doubt, increase the margin of safety requirement rather than lowering it.
- Treat each investment as a business ownership stake, not a ticker symbol to flip.
- If you cannot explain the investment in plain sentences, you do not understand it well enough.

## life

- If you feel an urgent urge to trade, pause and name the emotion before acting.

## process

- Prefer holding a diversified basket of bargain issues over concentrating in a few stocks.
- Reinvest dividends and proceeds into new undervalued opportunities rather than spending them.
- Review each position quarterly; sell only if price reaches intrinsic value or fundamentals break down.
- Keep a simple journal of every investment decision: value, price, margin, reason.
- Diversify across at least twenty different bargain issues to reduce idiosyncratic risk.
- Continuously revisit and refine your valuation models as you gain experience.
