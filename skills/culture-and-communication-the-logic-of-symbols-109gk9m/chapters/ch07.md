# ch07 — Rites of Transition

> The rituals used to move between social categories.

When a person moves from one social category to another—such as from 'child' to 'adult' or 'unmarried' to 'married'—they must cross a boundary. This crossing is almost always marked by a ritual. These 'rites of passage' take place in a state of social 'timelessness.' During the ritual, the person is neither in the old category nor the new one; they are in a liminal, or threshold, state. This is why weddings, funerals, and initiations are so heavily structured. The ritual acts as a bridge over the 'no-man's-land' between social statuses. By performing these acts, society acknowledges that the boundary has been crossed and the person's new identity is valid.
