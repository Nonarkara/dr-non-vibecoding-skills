# ch03 — Metaphor and Metonymy

> The two primary ways the human mind connects ideas.

Meaning is built through two logical moves: metonymy and metaphor. Metonymy is based on contiguity; it is the logic of 'part for the whole.' A uniform is a sign of a soldier because it is a physical part of the soldier's social context. Metaphor is based on similarity; it is the logic of 'this is like that.' When we say 'the king is a lion,' we are making a metaphoric leap between the context of animals and the context of politics. In culture, these two modes are constantly mixed. We take a sequence of events (a syntagmatic chain) and transform it into a set of associations (a paradigmatic set). For example, the sequence of a wedding is a chain of events, but the white dress is a metaphor for purity. To decode a culture is to see how these metaphors and metonyms are layered on top of each other.
