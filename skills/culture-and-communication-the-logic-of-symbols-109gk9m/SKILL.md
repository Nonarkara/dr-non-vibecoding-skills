## When to load this skill

It moves beyond literal language to explain how non-verbal codes—like clothing, architecture, and gift-giving—operate as a 'social grammar.' By understanding the distinctions between signals, signs, and symbols, an agent can design interfaces, brands, or social systems that align with the deep logic of human categorization and boundary-making.

## Core principles

- Culture is a coded system where meaning is derived from contrast between items, not the items themselves.
- Every social act is dyadic, involving a sender who encodes a message and a receiver who decodes it.
- Social life functions like a musical score; the underlying structure remains even if individual performances vary.
- Meaning is generated by cutting the continuous world into discrete, named categories.
- Boundaries between categories are inherently ambiguous and are often treated as sacred or taboo.
- Ritual is the mechanism used to move people or objects across social boundaries during 'timeless' intervals.
- Magic is the logical error of treating a symbolic association as a physical cause-and-effect trigger.
- Non-verbal communication follows a syntax that is simpler than speech but equally structured.
