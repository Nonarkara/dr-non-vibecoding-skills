# ch02 — Concrete Imagery and Word Choice

> Shows how concrete nouns and vivid verbs create mental pictures. Discusses the power of specific adjectives, phonesthetics, and the careful use of uncommon words.

Concrete language lets readers see the scene in their mind’s eye. Instead of saying “the event was significant,” describe the event: “the crowd erupted in cheers.” Specific nouns (a sea of voluptuous models) and active verbs (the hurricane ripped the roof off) give the brain sensory cues. Uncommon words are useful when they convey a nuance no common word can, and when their sound reinforces meaning—phonesthetics. For example, *haunting* sounds like a ghostly echo, and *titillating* vibrates the tongue like a spark. Use such words sparingly; a fresh, precise term beats a long string of adjectives.
