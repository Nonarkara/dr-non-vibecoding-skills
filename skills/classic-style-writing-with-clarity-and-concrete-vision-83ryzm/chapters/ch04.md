# ch04 — Metadiscourse and Signposting

> Critiques excessive signposting and metadiscourse. Shows how to guide the reader with subtle cues instead of overt roadmaps.

Metadiscourse—phrases like “this section will discuss…”—often serves the writer more than the reader. Over‑signposting forces the reader to parse extra words before reaching the content. Instead, embed cues naturally: start a paragraph with a question, or use a transition that mirrors conversation (“Now, consider…”). Keep signposts sparse, just enough to keep the reader oriented without breaking immersion. When you must signal a shift, use a single word or a brief clause rather than a full preview.
