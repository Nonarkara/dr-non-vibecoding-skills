# ch02 — Building a Repetition Routine

> Shows how to structure a daily practice around the mantra, including setting targets, choosing timing, and integrating the routine into existing habits.

# Building a Repetition Routine

To make the mantra effective, you need a routine that specifies how many times you will repeat it and when you will do it. Start by picking a realistic target—perhaps 50 repetitions per session. If that feels too easy, increase it; if it feels hard, lower it. The key is to choose a number you can hit consistently without feeling overwhelmed.

Next, decide on a trigger that will remind you to begin. Common triggers include finishing a cup of coffee, sitting down at your desk, or hearing a specific alarm. Pairing the mantra with an existing habit makes it easier to remember.

During the session, focus only on the act of repeating. If you lose count, simply start over or estimate; the goal is not perfect counting but sustained effort. You can speak the mantra aloud, whisper it, or write each repetition on a piece of paper. Writing adds a kinesthetic element that can help some learners stay engaged.

After you finish, take a brief pause to notice how you feel. Do you feel more alert? Slightly bored? Calm? Noting these sensations helps you tune the routine over time.

Finally, log your session. A simple spreadsheet with date, target repetitions, actual repetitions, and a short note on mood is enough. Over weeks you will see patterns—perhaps you repeat more in the morning, or your mood improves after a session. Use this data to adjust targets or timing.

By turning the mantra into a scheduled routine with a clear trigger, a manageable target, and a quick reflection, you turn a simple repeat into a reliable habit that supports longer‑term learning goals.
