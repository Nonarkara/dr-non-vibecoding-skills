# ch10 — Design Sprint Planning Using the IDEO Process

> Apply the observe‑brainstorm‑prototype‑iterate cycle in a time‑boxed sprint. This chapter outlines a five‑day plan that adapts IDEO’s steps for rapid concept testing.

A design sprint condenses the IDEO process into a fixed schedule, typically five days, to test a hypothesis quickly. Day 1 is dedicated to understanding: reviewing the problem statement, constraints, and any existing data, then planning observation activities. Day 2 focuses on observation: teams go into the field, conduct interviews or shadowing, and compile bug lists and insight statements. Day 3 is for brainstorming: using the four rules, participants generate a wide set of ideas, then vote to select the most promising concepts. Day 4 is for prototyping: the team builds low‑fidelity prototypes of the top ideas, each aimed at answering a specific question. Day 5 is for testing: prototypes are shown to target users, feedback is captured, and the team decides whether to iterate, pivot, or proceed to development. Throughout the sprint, the team works in a shared space, holds short stand‑up meetings to share progress, and keeps a visible record of decisions and learnings. By the end of the five days, the team has a validated direction or a clear set of next steps, having used observation, ideation, prototyping, and iteration in a compressed, focused manner.
