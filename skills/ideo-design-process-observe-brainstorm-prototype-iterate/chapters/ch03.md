# ch03 — Brainstorming for Quantity and Wild Ideas

> Run a brainstorming session that follows IDEO’s four rules: defer judgment, go for quantity, build on others, and encourage wild ideas. This chapter details the setup, facilitation, and output capture.

IDEO’s brainstorming sessions are designed to maximize idea flow while preventing premature criticism. The four rules are posted on the walls: defer judgment (no criticism of any idea), go for quantity (aim for as many ideas as possible), build on others’ ideas (use “yes, and…” thinking), and encourage wild ideas (the wilder, the better). Sessions begin with a warm‑up activity to loosen thinking, then the facilitator presents the problem statement and any relevant insights from observation. Participants shout out ideas, which are captured on sticky notes or a whiteboard, one idea per note. The facilitator keeps the pace high, gently reminding the group to defer judgment and to piggyback on previous suggestions. Wild ideas are welcomed because they can lead to practical breakthroughs when later refined. After a set time (often 20‑30 minutes), the team groups similar ideas, looks for themes, and votes on the most promising concepts using dot voting or similar methods. The output is a collection of raw ideas that feed into prototyping, with the understanding that many will be discarded or combined.
