# ch02 — Understanding Constraints

> Identify and frame the problem by clarifying market, client, technology, and perceived constraints. This chapter shows how constraints shape the design brief and become creative fuel.

Before ideation, IDEO teams spend time understanding the context of the challenge. They gather information about the market, the client’s goals, the relevant technology, and the constraints that stakeholders believe exist. These constraints might include budget limits, manufacturing capabilities, regulatory requirements, or time windows. Rather than seeing constraints as blockers, IDEO treats them as inputs that focus creativity. For instance, when redesigning a medical inflation device, the team learned that technicians needed to use both hands, which became a design constraint that led to a two‑handed grip and an upward‑tilted pressure gauge. By explicitly stating constraints, the team avoids solving the wrong problem and can later challenge assumptions if evidence suggests a constraint is flexible. The understanding phase produces a clear design brief that outlines the problem space, the target user, and the non‑negotiable limits. This brief guides brainstorming and prototyping, ensuring that ideas remain relevant and feasible.
