# ch04 — Rapid Prototyping: Build to Learn

> Create low‑fidelity prototypes that answer the next design question. This chapter explains why prototypes are questions, how to choose the right fidelity, and how to keep them cheap and fast.

A prototype is not a finished product; it is a tool for learning. IDEO teams ask, ‘What do we need to learn next?’ and then build the simplest artifact that can answer that question. Early prototypes might be sketches, foam models, storyboards, or role‑plays. The key is to invest just enough effort to test a hypothesis, then discard or evolve the prototype based on feedback. For example, when designing a new toothbrush for children, IDEO first built a fat, squishy grip out of clay to see if kids could hold it more easily than a standard thin handle. The prototype answered the question about grip comfort without investing in molding or tooling. By keeping prototypes cheap and fast, teams can explore many variations and fail quickly, which accelerates learning. The process also reduces attachment to any single idea, making it easier to pivot when evidence shows a concept does not work. Prototypes are shared with users, engineers, and stakeholders to gather reactions that inform the next iteration.
