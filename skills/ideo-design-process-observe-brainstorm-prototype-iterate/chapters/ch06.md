# ch06 — Cross‑Functional Collisions and Hot Teams

> Create environments where engineers, designers, and users can collide around prototypes. This chapter describes hot teams, shared spaces, and the role of physical prototypes in sparking conversation.

IDEO believes that breakthroughs happen when people from different disciplines bump into each other while working on a shared prototype. A hot team is a small, multidisciplinary group—typically including an industrial designer, an engineer, a human‑factors specialist, and a business representative—given a clear goal and a tight deadline, often one to two weeks. The team works in a shared space where prototypes are displayed on walls, tables, or shelves, inviting anyone to pick them up, try them, and comment. Physical prototypes act as conversation starters; an engineer might notice a manufacturing issue, a designer might see an ergonomic opportunity, and a user might reveal a misunderstanding of how to use the product. These collisions generate rapid feedback and new ideas that would not emerge in siloed meetings. To encourage collisions, IDEO places prototypes in high‑traffic areas, uses movable furniture, and holds regular stand‑up reviews where each member shares what they learned from interacting with the prototype. The hot team’s time pressure focuses effort, while the open layout ensures that insights flow freely across disciplines.
