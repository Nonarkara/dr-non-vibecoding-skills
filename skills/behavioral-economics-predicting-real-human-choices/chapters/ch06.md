# ch06 — Anomalies as Signals and Opportunities

> Persistent deviations from rational predictions in markets or experiments are anomalies. When stable, they reveal real behavioral patterns and can be exploited for profit or social good.

Anomalies are repeatable patterns where actual behavior contradicts the rational-actor model, such as the equity premium puzzle, the disposition effect (selling winners too early and holding losers too long), or excessive trading volume. Unlike one-off mistakes, anomalies persist across time, contexts, and populations, indicating a systematic bias. Behavioral economists treat anomalies as opportunities: if investors systematically hold losing stocks too long due to loss aversion, a strategy that sells losers and buys winners can earn excess returns. Similarly, if consumers fail to switch to cheaper energy plans due to status quo bias, an automatic enrollment program can increase adoption. An AI should monitor decision outcomes for stable deviations from model predictions, investigate the underlying psychology, and design interventions that either correct the bias or harness it.
