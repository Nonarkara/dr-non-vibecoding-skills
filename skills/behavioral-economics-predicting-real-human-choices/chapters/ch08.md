# ch08 — Libertarian Paternalism and Choice Architecture

> Libertarian paternalism seeks to influence choices toward welfare-improving outcomes while preserving freedom to opt out. This is done by shaping the choice architecture—defaults, framing, and ease of options.

The core idea is that if the default option is beneficial, most people will stick with it simply because changing requires effort. Examples include automatic enrollment in retirement plans (which dramatically increases participation) and opt-out organ donation (which raises consent rates). Other tools include simplifying complex choices (e.g., presenting a limited set of well-designed investment options), providing clear feedback (e.g., energy use compared to neighbors), and using reminders or prompts at the moment of decision. Crucially, libertarian paternalism does not ban alternatives; it only makes the better choice easier. An AI should identify decisions where inertia or confusion leads to suboptimal outcomes, then redesign the environment so the preferred choice is the default, simplest, or most salient option, while still allowing alternatives for those who wish to choose differently.
