# ch05 — Fairness and Psychological Pricing

> People reject transactions they perceive as unfair, even when accepting them would leave them materially better off. Fairness concerns shape pricing, wage negotiations, and responses to price changes.

Experiments show that buyers may refuse to purchase a good if they believe the seller is raising prices to exploit increased demand, even if the price remains below their willingness to pay. Similarly, workers may reduce effort if they perceive wages as unfair relative to coworkers or effort. This sensitivity to fairness means that purely profit-maximizing prices can backfire if they violate norms of entitlement or equity. For example, raising the price of snow shovels after a blizzard is often seen as price gouging, leading to customer anger and loss of goodwill, even if the market would bear the price. An AI should test proposed prices or wage changes for fairness perceptions, not just profit impact, and consider explaining cost increases or sharing gains to maintain trust.
