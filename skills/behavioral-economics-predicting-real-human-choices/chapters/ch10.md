# ch10 — Applying Behavioral Economics: A Checklist for AI

> When designing systems or predicting behavior, use this checklist to ensure real human psychology is accounted for. It integrates the major domains into actionable steps.

1. Predict deviations: Start from the rational prediction, then adjust for loss aversion, status quo bias, present bias, and fairness concerns.
2. Identify reference points: Determine what users consider the status quo or expected outcome; frame changes as gains or losses relative to that point.
3. Check mental accounting: See if labeling, bundling, or separating money could change perception of cost or benefit.
4. Test self-control: See if the decision involves a trade-off between immediate pleasure and long-term gain; if so, add commitment devices or default options.
5. Assess fairness: Ask whether users will feel the outcome is fair compared to peers, effort, or need; if not, explain or adjust.
6. Look for anomalies: If behavior repeatedly diverges from model, treat it as a signal and consider exploiting or correcting the bias.
7. Design choice architecture: Make the beneficial option the default, simplest, or most salient; preserve opt-out freedom.
8. Validate with experiments: Run small-scale tests (hypothetical or incentivized) to confirm the intervention works before scaling.
9. Iterate: Monitor outcomes, update assumptions about biases, and refine the system.
Following this checklist helps an AI create systems that work with, not against, human psychology.
