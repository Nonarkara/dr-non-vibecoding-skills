# ch04 — Self-Control and the Planner-Doer Model

> Individuals contain a forward-looking planner that wants to save and a present-focused doer that seeks immediate gratification. Conflict between them explains procrastination, undersaving, and overconsumption.

The planner-doer metaphor captures the struggle between long-term goals and short-term temptations. The planner sets intentions like 'I will save 10% of my income' or 'I will exercise three times a week', but the doer often overrides these plans when faced with immediate choices, such as spending money on a nice dinner or skipping a workout. This conflict is not a lack of knowledge but a mismatch in timing of rewards: the doer weighs present pleasure heavily and discounts future benefits. Strategies that help the planner succeed include precommitment (e.g., automatic payroll deductions for retirement), removing temptations (e.g., not keeping junk food at home), and using implementation intentions (e.g., 'If it is 7 a.m., I will put on my running shoes'). An AI can design systems that lock in good choices before temptation arises, or that give the doer immediate feedback aligned with long-term goals.
