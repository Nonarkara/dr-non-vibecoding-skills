# ch09 — Bounded Rationality and the Econs-Humans Distinction

> Econs are the perfectly rational, self-interested agents of traditional economics. Humans are actual people with limits on computation, willpower, and emotional stability. Good models must reflect this middle ground.

Traditional models assume Econs can process unlimited information, forecast perfectly, and resist temptation. In reality, Humans have limited memory, attention, and self-control, leading them to rely on heuristics, succumb to present bias, and make mistakes that are systematic rather than random. Bounded rationality does not mean Humans are irrational; it means they make the best decisions they can given cognitive limits. An AI should avoid two extremes: assuming people will always optimize (the Econ fallacy) and assuming they are hopelessly biased (the cynic fallacy). Instead, predict behavior by starting from rational incentives and then adjusting for known biases such as loss aversion, status quo bias, or overconfidence, calibrated to the context and stakes.
