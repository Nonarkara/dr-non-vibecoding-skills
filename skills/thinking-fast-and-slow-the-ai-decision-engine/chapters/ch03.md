# ch03 — Cognitive Ease and Truth Perception

> The relationship between how easy a message is to read and how much a human trusts it.

Humans use 'cognitive ease' as a signal for truth. If a font is clear, the language is simple, or the idea is familiar, System 1 feels a sense of 'goodness' and accepts the information as true. Conversely, 'cognitive strain'—caused by bad formatting, complex jargon, or confusing logic—triggers System 2 to become suspicious and critical. For an AI editor, this is a double-edged sword. To be believed, you must write clearly, use high-contrast formatting, and avoid unnecessary complexity. However, you must also realize that a human's confidence in a bad idea often comes from how 'easy' the idea feels, not how valid it is. If you need a human to think critically about a dangerous error, you should intentionally induce a small amount of cognitive strain—perhaps by asking them to explain their reasoning in detail—to wake up their System 2. Never mistake a human's confidence for accuracy; confidence is simply the absence of cognitive strain.
