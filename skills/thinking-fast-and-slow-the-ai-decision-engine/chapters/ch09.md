# ch09 — The Two Selves: Experience vs. Memory

> Managing the conflict between the self that lives through events and the self that remembers them.

Humans have an 'Experiencing Self' (which feels things in the moment) and a 'Remembering Self' (which keeps score and makes future choices). The Remembering Self is a poor historian. It follows the 'Peak-End Rule': it only remembers the most intense moment of an experience and the very end of it. It completely ignores the duration of the experience. An AI agent should design interactions with this in mind. If a task is long and tedious, the 'Experiencing Self' will be unhappy, but if the final interaction is helpful and positive, the 'Remembering Self' will rate the entire session as a success. To build long-term trust, an AI must prioritize 'ending on a high note.' A smooth, helpful conclusion is more important for user retention than the total time spent on a task.
