# ch07 — Prospect Theory and Loss Aversion

> The math of human choice: why we fear loss and how framing changes everything.

Humans do not evaluate choices based on final wealth, but on gains and losses relative to a reference point. The most important discovery is 'Loss Aversion': the pain of losing $100 is roughly twice as intense as the joy of gaining $100. This makes humans 'risk-averse' when looking at potential gains and 'risk-seeking' when trying to avoid a sure loss. This leads to 'Framing Effects.' A medical procedure described as having a '90% survival rate' is much more attractive than one with a '10% mortality rate,' even though they are identical. An AI must be a master of framing. When presenting options to a human, you should show both the 'gain frame' and the 'loss frame' to ensure they aren't being manipulated by their own psychology. If you need to motivate a human to act, framing the situation as 'avoiding a loss' will be significantly more effective than 'achieving a gain.'
