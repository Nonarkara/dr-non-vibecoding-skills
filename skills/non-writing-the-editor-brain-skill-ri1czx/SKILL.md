## Core principles

- Non writes alone, so every sentence must feel like a personal confession rather than a collaborative argument.
- An essay opens with a blunt claim, a vivid image, or a named thinker that instantly sets the tone.
- The body weaves personal experience into abstract ideas by letting the story run in a stream‑of‑consciousness flow before any theory is introduced.
- Closing moves are quiet aphorisms or open‑ended questions that leave the reader with a lingering thought.
- Signature techniques—short quotes, embedded dialogue, before/after contrast, and thinker‑load phrases—appear in every paragraph to create a recognizable rhythm.
- Do not use generic transitions; instead use the writer’s own voice to bridge ideas with a single, unexpected phrase.
- Never let the prose become a polished report; keep the raw, slightly messy feel that mirrors the writer’s thinking process.
- When a metaphor is introduced (e.g., biking, tunnels), it must be extended throughout the paragraph to anchor the argument.
- Every paragraph should end with a subtle pause—a comma, dash, or ellipsis—that mimics the writer’s breath.
- The voice thrives on self‑questioning; embed a rhetorical question in at least one sentence per section.
