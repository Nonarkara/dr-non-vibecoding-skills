# ch03 — Weaving Experience into Argument: Stream of Consciousness

> Shows how Non lets a personal story flow uninterrupted before inserting theory, creating a natural bridge between lived experience and abstract ideas.

The body of a Non essay typically follows a stream‑of‑consciousness style. He writes a continuous narrative, often beginning with a mundane event—like a bike ride or a coffee shop visit—and lets the thoughts spill without immediate analysis. This unfiltered flow captures the raw emotions and observations that later become the foundation for any theoretical claim. Only after the story reaches a natural pause does Non introduce a thinker or concept, allowing the anecdote to “prove” the abstract point. The transition is subtle: a short phrase such as "From there, the idea of Hume’s internal empiricism emerged" signals the shift without breaking the narrative momentum. This method keeps the essay grounded in lived reality while still delivering intellectual insight.
