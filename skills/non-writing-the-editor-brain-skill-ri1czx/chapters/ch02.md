# ch02 — Opening Moves: Blunt Claims, Images, and Thinker‑Load

> Describes three standard ways to start an essay: a blunt statement, a vivid image, or a named thinker that carries the argument.

Non begins most posts with one of three hooks. The blunt claim is a stark declaration that forces the reader to accept a premise immediately, e.g., "Writing is essential to people like me." The image hook paints a concrete scene—a ship at Shanghai’s shore, a yellow Brompton—so the reader visualizes the setting before any argument appears. The thinker‑load move drops a philosopher or author’s name, letting the name do the heavy lifting; the reference instantly signals the essay’s intellectual direction. After the hook, Non often adds a short personal reaction, linking the opening to his own experience. This three‑part structure—claim/image, personal tie, thinker reference—creates a recognizable rhythm that readers can anticipate while still feeling fresh each time.
