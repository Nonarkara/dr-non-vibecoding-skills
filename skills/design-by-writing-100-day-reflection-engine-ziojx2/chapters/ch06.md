# ch06 — Non‑Possessive Empathy

> Teaches designers to avoid treating users as objects to be owned, fostering respect and deeper insight.

Drawing from the author's critique of possessive love, the chapter reframes user empathy as a non‑possessive relationship. Instead of trying to "capture" a user’s behavior for profit, designers observe and listen, allowing the user’s context to shape the solution. This approach reduces bias where designers assume ownership of a problem and impose their own agenda. Practical steps include: (1) conduct interviews without a predefined solution, (2) record observations without labeling them as "needs" until patterns emerge, and (3) validate insights with multiple users to avoid projecting a single story onto a whole market. By treating users as partners rather than assets, teams generate designs that respect autonomy and are more likely to be adopted.
