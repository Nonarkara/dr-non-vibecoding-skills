# ch02 — Stream of Consciousness for Insight

> Explains how uninterrupted, free‑flow writing can surface deep user insights that structured note‑taking often misses.

The stream‑of‑consciousness method removes the editor’s voice until after the initial flow is captured. By writing continuously for a set period—say, ten minutes—you bypass the brain’s censor and let raw impressions emerge. In ethnographic research, this mirrors the practice of recording field notes directly after an encounter, before theory intervenes. The author describes using a single powerful story as a launchpad, then letting the narrative dictate the shape of the write‑up. For designers, the same technique can be applied to brainstorming sessions: start with a single vivid user moment, write everything that comes to mind without pausing, then later extract themes, pain points, and opportunities. This approach reduces the risk of missing subtle cues because the writer is forced to stay in the moment, and it creates a rich data set that can be sliced into personas, journey maps, or problem statements.
