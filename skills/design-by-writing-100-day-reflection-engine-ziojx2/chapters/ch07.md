# ch07 — Failure Modes: Over‑Focus and Closing‑In

> Identifies common cognitive traps—over‑focus on a single aspect and "closing‑in" on assumptions—and provides ways to counteract them.

The tunnel metaphor illustrates how focusing solely on the wall (the obvious problem) can cause designers to miss obstacles on the floor (hidden constraints). The "closing‑in" effect occurs when deep work shuts out peripheral awareness, leading to blind spots. To counteract these failure modes, the author suggests a two‑step check after each design session: (1) list three things you ignored while concentrating, and (2) deliberately explore each listed item for a minute. This simple exercise surfaces hidden risks, such as accessibility issues or edge‑case scenarios, before they become costly defects. Incorporating this check into sprint retrospectives creates a systematic guardrail against tunnel vision.
