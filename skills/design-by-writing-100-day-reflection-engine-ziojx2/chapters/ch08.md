# ch08 — Incrementalism Over Big‑Bang Redesign

> Advocates small, testable changes rather than sweeping redesigns, reducing risk and learning cost.

The author’s experience of writing a single powerful story before consulting notes mirrors the incremental design approach: start with a minimal, high‑impact change, observe its effect, then iterate. This method contrasts with large‑scale redesigns that require massive resources and hide failure until late. By treating each change as a hypothesis—"If we adjust this button label, will conversion improve?"—designers can run rapid A/B tests, gather data, and decide whether to expand, revert, or pivot. The chapter provides a three‑stage recipe: (1) identify the smallest possible change that could move a key metric, (2) deploy it to a subset of users, (3) evaluate results before committing further resources.
