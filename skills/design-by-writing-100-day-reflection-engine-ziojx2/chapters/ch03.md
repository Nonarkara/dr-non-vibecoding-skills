# ch03 — Reflection and the Iteration Loop

> Shows how a brief daily reflection creates a cheap feedback loop that surfaces hidden assumptions and drives continuous improvement.

Social‑psychology research cited in the text shows that a fifteen‑minute reflection after work improves performance. The author applies this to writing: after each writing session, step away, then return to read with fresh eyes. This mirrors the design iteration cycle—prototype, test, reflect, refine. The reflection step forces you to ask: What worked? What surprised me? What assumptions did I make? By documenting answers, you create a living backlog of design hypotheses. The habit also mitigates the "closing‑in" effect where deep focus blinds you to broader context; a short break re‑opens peripheral vision, allowing you to notice edge cases and failure modes. Designers can embed a five‑minute post‑session journal into their workflow to capture insights before they evaporate, turning each session into a micro‑sprint with its own retrospective.
