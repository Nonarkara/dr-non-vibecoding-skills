## Core principles

- Writing forces you to make invisible thoughts visible, turning vague intuition into concrete material you can iterate on.
- Begin every design effort with the most vivid, single user‑centric story you can recall without consulting notes.
- Reflection after each work session creates a cheap, high‑impact feedback loop that surfaces hidden assumptions.
- Physical activity (e.g., biking) can be used as a deliberate focus‑shifting ritual to surface new ideas and reduce mental fatigue.
- Avoid treating users as possessions; instead practice non‑possessive empathy that respects their autonomy and context.
- Incremental, low‑fidelity prototypes are safer than big‑bang redesigns because they expose failure early and cheaply.
- Tradition is a hypothesis, not a rule; test it against current user needs before letting it dictate design decisions.
