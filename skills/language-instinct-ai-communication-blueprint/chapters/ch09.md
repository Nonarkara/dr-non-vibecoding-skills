# ch09 — Confidence Communication: Point Estimates vs. Intervals

> Provides concrete guidelines for expressing uncertainty so that humans update beliefs correctly.

People tend to treat a single number as a fact, even when the speaker’s knowledge is uncertain. Research shows that stating a confidence interval (e.g., “70 % ± 10 %”) leads to more accurate belief updates than giving only the point estimate (70 %). The brain integrates the width of the interval as a cue about reliability. Moreover, framing the interval as a range of plausible outcomes reduces motivated reasoning. AI agents should always accompany quantitative claims with a calibrated interval, explain the source of the interval (sample size, variance), and, when possible, compare it to a relevant base rate.
