# ch04 — Biases that Warp Language Reception

> Lists the main cognitive biases that cause listeners to misinterpret or reject messages, and explains their psychological roots.

Human listeners do not evaluate statements purely on logical merit. Anchoring makes the first number heard dominate later judgments; availability causes vivid recent examples to outweigh statistical evidence; framing steers choices by emphasizing gains or losses; motivated reasoning leads people to accept conclusions that fit their identity; and the “what you see is all there is” (WYSIATI) tendency makes incomplete information feel sufficient. These biases are automatic, often operating below conscious awareness, and they can be triggered by subtle wording choices. An AI that wishes to be understood must anticipate these distortions and counteract them with explicit cues—such as stating the base rate before a surprising fact, or providing confidence intervals alongside point estimates.
