# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **classic style** | A prose approach that shows concrete facts and converses with a cooperative reader. | ch01 |
| **concrete imagery** | Specific, sensory details that let the reader picture the scene. | ch02 |
| **phonesthetics** | The sense that a word’s sound matches its meaning. | ch02 |
| **curse of knowledge** | The bias that makes experts forget what it’s like not to know what they know. | ch03 |
| **metadiscourse** | Words that talk about the structure of the text rather than its content. | ch04 |
| **signposting** | Explicitly telling the reader what will come next. | ch04 |
| **nominalization** | Turning a verb into a noun, often hiding the agent. | ch05 |
| **zombie noun** | A lifeless noun created by nominalization that obscures action. | ch05 |
| **active voice** | Sentence structure where the subject performs the action. | ch06 |
| **passive voice** | Sentence structure where the subject receives the action, often omitting the agent. | ch06 |
| **hedging** | Using vague qualifiers to soften statements. | ch07 |
| **cliché** | An overused expression that no longer creates a vivid image. | ch08 |
| **idiom** | A phrase whose meaning is not literal but culturally understood. | ch08 |
| **fresh metaphor** | A new, vivid comparison that enhances imagery. | ch08 |
| **conditional** | A verb form that expresses a hypothetical situation. | ch01 |
| **subjunctive** | A verb mood used for wishes, demands, or contrary‑to‑fact statements. | ch01 |
| **concrete noun** | A noun that refers to a tangible object or person. | ch02 |
| **abstract noun** | A noun that names an idea, quality, or state rather than a physical thing. | ch05 |
| **signpost sparingly** | Use minimal transitional phrases to keep flow natural. | ch04 |
| **reader’s gaze** | The mental focus the writer directs toward a part of the scene. | ch01 |
