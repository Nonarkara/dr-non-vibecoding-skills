# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## Show‑Then‑Explain

**When to use:** When introducing a new concept or fact.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify a concrete example that illustrates the idea.
2. Present the example first, letting the reader picture it.
3. Follow with a brief explanation that ties the example to the abstract concept.

**Chapter:** ch01

## Revive Zombie Nouns

**When to use:** When a sentence contains a nominalization that hides the agent.

**Recipe:**

1. Locate the noun ending in –tion, –ment, –ance, etc.
2. Find the original verb that produced the noun.
3. Rewrite the clause using the verb and a clear subject.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## Active‑Focus Choice

**When to use:** Deciding whether to use active or passive voice.

**Recipe:**

1. Ask: Who is the most important character for the reader to track?
2. If the doer matters, use active voice.
3. If the doer is irrelevant or distracting, switch to passive.

**Chapter:** ch06

---

## Hedge‑Only‑When‑Needed

**When to use:** When a claim is uncertain or limited.

**Recipe:**

1. State the claim plainly.
2. Add a qualifier only if data or context truly limit the claim.
3. Explain the limitation in one short clause.

**Chapter:** ch07

## Cliché‑Refresh

**When to use:** When a familiar idiom is unavoidable but feels stale.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the literal elements of the idiom.
2. Rewrite the sentence using those literal elements in the current context.
3. If possible, replace with a fresh metaphor that matches the concrete scene.

**Chapter:** ch08

---

## Phonesthetic Word Selection

**When to use:** Choosing an uncommon word for impact.

**Recipe:**

1. List candidate words that convey the precise meaning.
2. Read each aloud to feel its sound.
3. Select the word whose sound reinforces the intended image and is still understandable.

**Chapter:** ch09

---

## Minimal Signposting

**When to use:** When a paragraph transitions to a new idea.

**Recipe:**

1. Use a single transition word or question to cue the shift.
2. Avoid full‑sentence previews of the upcoming content.
3. Let the new sentence itself carry the forward momentum.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## Concrete‑Verb Rule

**When to use:** Any sentence describing an action.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the action being described.
2. Choose a strong, specific verb for that action.
3. Replace any noun‑based description with the verb and a concrete object.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## Avoid Unnecessary Metaconcepts

**When to use:** When academic jargon clouds meaning.

**Recipe:**

1. Spot abstract terms that package other ideas (e.g., “framework”, “level”).
2. Replace them with the concrete elements they refer to.
3. Rewrite the sentence using plain nouns and verbs.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## Checklist Integration

**When to use:** Final revision before publishing.

**Recipe:**

1. Run the 10‑step classic‑style checklist (chapter 10).
2. Mark each item as passed or revise accordingly.
3. Read the revised text aloud to confirm rhythm and clarity.

**Chapter:** ch10
