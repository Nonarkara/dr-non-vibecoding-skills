# ch06 — Active vs. Passive Voice

> Explains when to use active voice to highlight the agent and when passive voice can be a strategic choice.

Active voice puts the agent in the subject position, directing the reader’s attention to who is doing something: *Pooh ate the honey.* Passive voice can shift focus away from the agent when it is irrelevant or distracting: *The honey was eaten.* Use passive deliberately to hide the doer (e.g., “Mistakes were made”) or to keep the narrative flow on the more interesting character. Avoid passive as a default; it often signals laziness or a lack of concrete detail.
