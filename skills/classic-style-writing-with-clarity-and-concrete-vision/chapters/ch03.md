# ch03 — The Curse of Knowledge

> Explains why experts often write incomprehensibly: they forget what it’s like not to know what they know. Provides strategies to overcome this bias.

When you know a subject intimately, you lose the ability to imagine a reader’s ignorance. This “curse of knowledge” leads to jargon, skipped steps, and assumed familiarity. To combat it, explicitly ask: *What does the reader need to know?* Then supply that information in plain terms before moving on. Use analogies that map unfamiliar concepts onto everyday experiences—like comparing cosmic expansion to a ball tossed upward. Remember that the reader’s mental model starts empty; you must build it piece by piece, not assume it already exists.
