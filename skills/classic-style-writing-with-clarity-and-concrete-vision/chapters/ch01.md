# ch01 — The Classic‑Style Mindset

> Introduces the core metaphor of classic style—showing the world to a cooperative reader. Explains why writing is a simulated conversation and how this mindset differs from academic, bureaucratic, or post‑modern styles.

Classic style treats prose as a window onto the world. The writer first discovers a concrete fact—people moving, objects interacting, or a vivid scene—and then points the reader’s gaze toward it. Unlike academic prose, which often assumes a power imbalance, classic style assumes the reader is competent and will fill in logical steps. This conversational stance means the writer does not need to announce every move; the flow of ideas should be as natural as a spoken exchange. The style is aristocratic, not egalitarian: the truth is available, but the writer must work to reveal it. By visualizing the reader as a partner in a dialogue, the writer can focus on clarity, simplicity, and the pleasure of seeing something together.
