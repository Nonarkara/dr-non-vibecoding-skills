# ch08 — Clichés, Idioms, and Fresh Metaphors

> Discusses the danger of stale idioms and offers techniques to refresh or replace them with vivid imagery.

Clichés drain the reader’s visual brain because the mental image is already exhausted. Instead of “bite the bullet,” describe the literal action: *He swallowed his fear like a hard pill.* When a cliché is unavoidable, tweak it to match the concrete context (“the 800‑pound gorilla in the room” becomes a literal description of power). Fresh metaphors and similes that align with the subject’s sensory details keep the prose lively and memorable.
