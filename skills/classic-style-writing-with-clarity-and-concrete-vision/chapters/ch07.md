# ch07 — Hedging and Apologetic Language

> Shows how excessive hedging dilutes authority. Provides guidelines for when and how to qualify statements responsibly.

Words like *almost, apparently, arguably* are hedges that soften claims but also signal uncertainty. Hedge only when evidence truly warrants it, and do so with precise qualifiers (*on average*, *all things being equal*). Over‑hedging makes prose sound indecisive and can frustrate readers seeking clear guidance. When you must hedge, pair it with a brief explanation of the limitation. This keeps the writing honest without drowning the main point in filler.
