# ch05 — The Kula and the Circulation of Wealth

> The ceremonial exchange of valuables. How 'giving' creates power and stability.

The Kula is a vast, circular trade network involving the exchange of shell ornaments. It is the most famous example of Trobriand economics, but it is not 'trade' in the Western sense. The objects have no practical use; their value lies in their history and the act of giving them away. A Kula partner gives an 'opening gift' and expects a 'return gift' later. This system creates a permanent bond between individuals across different islands. It is a 'development corporation' that furthers human happiness and communication. The circulation of wealth is not about accumulation, but about movement. In any user group, look for the 'Kula'—the tokens of value that people exchange to build trust and status. These might be favors, information, or social credit. The 'clinching gift' that closes a transaction is as important as the 'solicitary gift' that starts it. Power in this system belongs to those who can move the most wealth, not those who keep it.
