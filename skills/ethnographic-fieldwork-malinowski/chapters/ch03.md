# ch03 — Economic Provinces and Specialization

> How geography and tradition create centers of industry. The 'caste' system of craftsmanship.

The Trobriand Islands are divided into distinct economic provinces, each with its own specialty. Kiriwina is the political and agricultural center, while Kuboma is the industrial heart, known for wood-carving and lime-pot production. Interestingly, high manual skill does not always equate to high social rank; the most skilled carvers are often found in the most 'despised' villages. This specialization is not like a modern market; it is a traditional monopoly. Certain villages have the 'legal' right to specific industries, such as pearl diving or basket weaving, and others will not poach these rights even if they have the technical skill. When analyzing a system, map out these 'provinces' of expertise. Understand that specialization is often rooted in mythology and social history rather than just efficiency. The flow of goods between these provinces—fish for yams, carvings for tobacco—creates a web of interdependence that maintains the social order. You cannot understand one village in isolation; you must see it as a node in a larger network of trade and obligation.
