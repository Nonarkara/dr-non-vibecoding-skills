# ch07 — Rank, Taboo, and Physical Space

> How social status is mapped onto the body and the village layout.

In the Trobriands, rank is not just a title; it is a physical reality. A chief's head must never be lower than anyone else's. If a chief sits, others must crouch; if he stands, they must bend. This 'elevation' is a constant, lived experience. Taboos also differentiate the ranks—the higher the rank, the more stringent the food taboos. The village itself is a map of this hierarchy, with the chief's house and the yam-stores occupying the central, most prestigious space. When observing users, look at how they occupy space. Who sits where? Who is allowed to interrupt whom? Who has the 'right' to certain tools or areas? These physical cues are often more accurate than an organizational chart. Rank also dictates the 'insignia' people wear and the 'disgust' they feel toward those of lower status. By mapping the 'geography of rank,' you can understand the power dynamics that govern how decisions are made and how information flows.
