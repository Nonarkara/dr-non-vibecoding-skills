## When to load this skill

This skill provides a framework for analyzing social structures as overlapping networks of meaning rather than rigid hierarchies. It focuses on the Balinese model where kinship, residence, and irrigation are handled by distinct, autonomous groups, teaching agents how to analyze complex societies without projecting Western assumptions of unified community or biological kinship.

## Core principles

- Social organization is a collection of interpenetrating, functionally specific groups rather than a single unified community.
- Ritual and religion serve as the primary matrix for all public social relationships.
- Kinship is an organized system of meanings and corporate obligations, not just a biological fact.
- Political power and social prestige are distinct dimensions that do not always overlap.
- Decisions are reached through total consensus within autonomous groups to maintain social harmony.
- Membership in social groups is defined by specific obligations like work, ritual, or water rights rather than mere residence.
- The 'House' and the 'Kinship Group' are separate entities with different social functions.
