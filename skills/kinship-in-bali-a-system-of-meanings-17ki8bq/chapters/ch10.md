# ch10 — Reading the Social Texture

> Understanding Balinese society requires seeing it as an intricate, embroidered texture of overlapping groups rather than a simple hierarchy.

Balinese social organization is best understood through the metaphor of an embroidered fabric rather than a repetitive wallpaper design. It consists of many interpenetrating shapes and overlapping memberships with few sharp edges. An individual's social identity is the sum of their roles in various autonomous groups: their hamlet, their irrigation society, their title group, their kinship dadia, and their spiritual clientage. Because these groups rarely have identical memberships, social life is characterized by a complex web of cross-cutting ties. This structure prevents any single organization from becoming all-powerful and ensures that social conflict in one area does not necessarily lead to a total breakdown of community relations. Analyzing this system requires looking at the specific meanings and functions of each group independently.
