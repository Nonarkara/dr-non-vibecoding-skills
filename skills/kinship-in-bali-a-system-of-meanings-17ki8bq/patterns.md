# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## Functional Specificity

**When to use:** When a new social task or goal is identified.

**Recipe:**

1. Define the specific purpose of the group.
2. Recruit members based on their interest or relevance to that purpose.
3. Establish the group as an independent 'seka' with its own rules.
4. Ensure the group does not take on unrelated social functions.

**Chapter:** ch08

---

## Consensus Decision Making

**When to use:** When a corporate group needs to make a decision.

**Recipe:**

1. Convene a formal meeting of all members.
2. Discuss the proposal thoroughly in both formal and informal settings.
3. Seek a solution that everyone can agree to.
4. If any member strongly opposes, delay or abandon the proposal to maintain harmony.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## Cross-Cutting Membership

**When to use:** When designing a stable social system.

**Recipe:**

1. Create multiple autonomous groups for different functions (water, ritual, residence).
2. Allow individuals to hold memberships in many different groups simultaneously.
3. Ensure the boundaries of these groups do not perfectly overlap.
4. Use the resulting web of ties to prevent the formation of rigid, opposing factions.

**Chapter:** ch10

---

## Ritual as Political Foundation

**When to use:** When organizing a large-scale political or social entity.

**Recipe:**

1. Center the organization on a shared temple or ritual cycle.
2. Use ceremonies to mobilize labor and resources from the population.
3. Validate social rank through participation in and funding of dramatic displays.
4. Focus on the symbolic expression of order rather than administrative control.

**Chapter:** ch06

---

## The Way-Station Principle

**When to use:** When a central authority needs to maintain ties with distant groups.

**Recipe:**

1. Establish a 'way-station' temple or altar in the distant location.
2. Symbolize the presence of the central deity or authority at this branch.
3. Allow local members to fulfill their ritual duties at the branch to save travel.
4. Use the ritual link to maintain a sense of inclusion in the larger group.

**Chapter:** ch01
