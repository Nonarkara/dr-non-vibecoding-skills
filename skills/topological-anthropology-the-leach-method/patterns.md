# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## Topological Stretching

**When to use:** When comparing two societies that seem different but share a common logic.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the key social variables (e.g., father, mother, group).
2. Map the lines of connectedness between them.
3. Abstract the pattern into a geometric or algebraic form.
4. Compare this form to other societies to find structural constants.

**Chapter:** ch02

## The Zero-Filiation Extreme

**When to use:** When a society denies a biological link to one parent.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify which parent is excluded from 'kinship.'
2. Observe how the relationship with that parent is redefined as 'affinity.'
3. Look for economic or ritual payments that treat that parent as an outsider.
4. Analyze how this exclusion strengthens the boundaries of the remaining group.

**Chapter:** ch05

---

## Substance-Symbol Mapping

**When to use:** When trying to understand group boundaries.

**Recipe:**

1. Ask what physical parts (bone, blood, flesh) are attributed to which parent.
2. Associate 'permanent' substances with group incorporation.
3. Associate 'temporary' or 'soft' substances with external alliance.
4. Use this map to predict inheritance and loyalty patterns.

**Chapter:** ch06

---

## The Affinity-Authority Link

**When to use:** When analyzing political hierarchies in kinship-based societies.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the source of 'fate' or involuntary mystical influence.
2. Identify the source of 'sorcery' or intentional ritual attack.
3. If these two sources are the same affinal group, that group holds political authority.
4. Treat the marriage alliance as a tributary relationship.

**Chapter:** ch09

---

## Ritual Substitution

**When to use:** When a social role is filled by an unexpected person.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the expected role (e.g., husband).
2. Identify the substitute (e.g., son).
3. Analyze the logic of the substitution (e.g., son acting as affine to mother).
4. Use the substitution to uncover the underlying structural category.

**Chapter:** ch04
