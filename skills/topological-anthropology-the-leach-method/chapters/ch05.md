# ch05 — The Algebra of Filiation

> Formalizes the relationship between maternal and paternal bonds using a variable-based ratio.

To move beyond value-loaded terms like 'filiation' and 'descent,' we can use algebraic notation. Let 'p' represent the bond with the mother and 'q' represent the bond with the father. The ratio of p to q (z = p/q) becomes a mathematical function that describes the kinship structure of a society. In the Trobriand case, q is zero, making z infinite. In the Lakher case, p is zero, making z zero. Most societies fall somewhere in between, where both parents have a value. This algebraic approach allows us to see that these 'extreme' societies are not anomalies but points on a single continuum. It removes the need for complex sub-categories like 'complementary filiation' by showing that all kinship systems are simply different values of the same variables. This abstraction helps the analyst avoid getting bogged down in the specific emotional or biological details of a case and instead focus on the structural logic of how individuals are assigned to groups.
