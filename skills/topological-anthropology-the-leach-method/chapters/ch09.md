# ch09 — Marriage as Political Dominance

> Explains how enduring marriage alliances can function as systems of political hierarchy.

In many societies, marriage is not a one-off event but a 'perpetual affinity' between lineages. These alliances often encode political dominance. For example, among the Kachin and Lakher, the group that 'gives' wives is often seen as superior or as a source of mystical power for the group that 'receives' them. This relationship is expressed through economic tribute and ritual respect. When the source of 'uncontrolled mystical influence' (fate) and 'controlled supernatural attack' (authority) coincide in the same person—such as a mother's brother who is also a political chief—the marriage alliance has become a system of political subordination. In these cases, the language of kinship (calling a chief 'uncle') is actually a language of power. The analyst must look past the family terms to see the underlying tributary and hierarchical relations that the marriage system sustains over generations.
