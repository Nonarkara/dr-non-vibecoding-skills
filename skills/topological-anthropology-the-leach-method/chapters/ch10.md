# ch10 — The Engineer's Conclusion

> A final call to abandon a priori definitions and focus on the specific facts of the case.

The ultimate goal of rethinking anthropology is to approach ethnographic facts without the baggage of pre-existing definitions. We must stop trying to fit the world into 'perfect circles' of English thought. Terms like 'marriage,' 'descent,' and 'sibling' are not absolute technical terms; they are ad hoc expedients. The analyst's job is to discover what the significant social categories are in a specific context, not what they 'ought' to be based on a textbook. This requires a high degree of imagination and a willingness to treat social systems as topological spaces where the variables are in constant flux. By sticking to the facts and using formal models to clarify our thinking, we can avoid the 'Ptolemaic' error of adding more and more complex sub-definitions to a failing theory. The value of anthropology lies in its ability to generalize from specific facts to universal structural principles, provided we don't let our own categories get in the way.
