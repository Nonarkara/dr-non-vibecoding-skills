# ch04 — The Lakher Extreme

> Examines the Lakher system where motherhood is treated as a social relationship of affinity.

The Lakher of Southeast Asia represent the logical opposite of the Trobriand case. In their patrilineal system, the mother is treated as having no kinship tie to her children; she is merely the 'father's wife.' This leads to social outcomes that seem bizarre to Western observers, such as the fact that children of the same mother by different fathers are not considered related and can marry. This extreme case proves that 'maternity' is just as much a sociological construct as 'paternity.' For the Lakher, the mother/child bond is one of affinity traced through the father. This is reinforced through death dues and economic obligations where the son acts as a deputy for the father in relation to the mother's lineage. By stripping away the assumption that the mother-child bond is a universal biological constant, we can see how the Lakher use the idea of motherhood to define the boundaries of their patrilineal groups and the alliances between them.
