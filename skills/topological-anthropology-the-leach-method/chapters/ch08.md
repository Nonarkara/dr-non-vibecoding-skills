# ch08 — The Two Faces of the Supernatural

> Distinguishes between uncontrolled mystical influence and controlled supernatural attack.

Supernatural beliefs are not just religious ideas; they are indicators of social structure. We can distinguish between two types of power: 'uncontrolled mystical influence' (like fate or involuntary witchcraft) and 'controlled supernatural attack' (like sorcery or a ritual curse). Uncontrolled influence is often associated with alliance; an individual is thought to be subject to it simply because of their structural position (e.g., being the child of a certain woman). Controlled attack is associated with authority; it is a power wielded intentionally by someone in a superior position. By mapping who is accused of witchcraft and who has the power to curse, we can see the hidden lines of political and social tension. For example, if witchcraft is thought to come from the mother's side in a patrilineal society, it marks the mother's lineage as a source of 'external' mystical influence that is distinct from the 'internal' authority of the father's lineage.
