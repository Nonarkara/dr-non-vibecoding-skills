# ch03 — The Trobriand Affinal Logic

> Re-evaluates Trobriand kinship to show how 'fatherhood' can be a category of affinity rather than filiation.

The Trobriand Islanders provide a classic case of how social dogma can override biological facts. They profess ignorance of the biological link between sex and pregnancy, asserting instead that a child is related only to its mother's lineage. In this system, the 'father' is not a kinsman but a 'mother's husband'—an affine. This is not a failure of logic but a specific structural pattern. The Trobrianders use the category of 'father' to establish a relationship of alliance rather than incorporation. This is evidenced by the fact that children are expected to resemble their fathers but not their mothers, and that fathers provide economic gifts (urigubu) to their children's households. By treating the father as an affine, the Trobriand system creates a bridge between distinct matrilineal groups. This demonstrates that 'paternity' is a sociological status that can be defined in ways that have nothing to do with genetics, serving instead to manage the flow of resources and social obligations between groups.
