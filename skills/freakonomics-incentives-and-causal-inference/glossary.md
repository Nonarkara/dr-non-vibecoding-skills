# Glossary

Terms and named ideas from the source, anchored to the chapter that explains them.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Incentive** | A means of urging people to do more of a good thing and less of a bad thing. | ch01 |
| **Economic Incentive** | A motivator based on financial gain or the avoidance of a loss. | ch01 |
| **Social Incentive** | A motivator based on the desire to be seen in a positive light by others. | ch01 |
| **Moral Incentive** | A motivator based on an individual's internal sense of right and wrong. | ch01 |
| **Information Asymmetry** | A situation where one party in a transaction has more or better information than the other. | ch07 |
| **Conventional Wisdom** | Ideas or explanations that are generally accepted as true but often lack factual evidence. | ch08 |
| **Correlation** | A statistical relationship between two variables that does not necessarily imply one causes the other. | ch09 |
| **Causality** | The relationship between an event (the cause) and a second event (the effect). | ch09 |
| **High-Stakes Testing** | Standardized testing where the results have significant consequences for students or staff. | ch04 |
| **Social Promotion** | The practice of promoting a student to the next grade regardless of their academic achievement. | ch04 |
| **Quid Pro Quo** | A favor or advantage granted in return for something else. | ch05 |
| **White-Collar Crime** | Non-violent crime committed by business and government professionals for financial gain. | ch07 |
| **The 9/11 Effect** | A temporary surge in empathy and honesty observed in data following the September 11 attacks. | ch06 |
| **Natural Experiment** | An empirical study in which individuals are exposed to conditions determined by nature or other factors outside the control of the investigators. | ch09 |
| **Algorithm** | A process or set of rules to be followed in calculations or other problem-solving operations. | ch04 |
| **Control Group** | A group in an experiment that does not receive the treatment and is used as a benchmark. | ch04 |
| **The Ring of Gyges** | A mythical ring that makes its wearer invisible, used to discuss whether people are naturally good. | ch06 |
| **Adam Smith** | The founder of classical economics who was also a moral philosopher. | ch10 |
| **Stetson Kennedy** | An activist who went undercover to expose the secrets of the Ku Klux Klan. | ch08 |
| **Paul Feldman** | The 'bagel man' who provided the data for a study on white-collar honesty. | ch06 |
