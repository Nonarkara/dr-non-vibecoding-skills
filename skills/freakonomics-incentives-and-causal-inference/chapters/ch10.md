# ch10 — The Impartial Observer's Toolkit

> How to adopt the mindset of a rogue economist to see the world as it actually is.

To see the hidden side of everything, you must adopt the mindset of an impartial observer. This means stripping away your moral biases and looking at the world as a series of measurements. Adam Smith, the father of economics, argued that we should try to see every case from the perspective of a third person who has no stake in the outcome. This allows you to form an objective view of the merits. The tools of this trade are not just math and statistics, but curiosity and a willingness to look at 'frivolous' subjects like sumo wrestling or bagel sales. By applying economic thinking to non-economic subjects, you can find patterns that others miss. The goal is not to judge whether a behavior is good or bad, but to understand why it is happening. When you replace moral posturing with an honest assessment of the data, the result is often a new and surprising insight that can solve problems more effectively than any moral crusade.
