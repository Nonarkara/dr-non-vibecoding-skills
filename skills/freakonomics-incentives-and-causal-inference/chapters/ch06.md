# ch06 — The Bagel Man's Honor System

> What an unmonitored bagel business reveals about the baseline honesty of office workers.

Paul Feldman delivered bagels to offices and left a cash box for payment, relying entirely on the honor system. This created a natural experiment in white-collar honesty. He found that, on average, people are about 87% honest. However, this rate fluctuates based on specific factors. Smaller offices are more honest than larger ones, likely because the social incentive of shame is stronger when everyone knows each other. Weather also plays a role; people pay more when the weather is pleasant and cheat more when it is unseasonably cold or rainy. High-stress holidays like Christmas and Thanksgiving lead to more theft, while low-stress holidays like the Fourth of July lead to more honesty. Feldman also observed that higher-ranking executives tended to cheat more than lower-level employees, perhaps due to a sense of entitlement. The bagel data suggests that most people are good even when no one is watching, but their honesty is fragile and affected by their environment and mood.
