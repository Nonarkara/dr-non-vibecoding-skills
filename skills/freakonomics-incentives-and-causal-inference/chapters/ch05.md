# ch05 — The Sumo Wrestler's Quid Pro Quo

> How data reveals collusion and match-rigging in a sport built on honor.

Sumo wrestling is a sport defined by honor, yet data shows it is prone to rigging. A wrestler's rank depends on winning at least eight out of fifteen matches in a tournament. On the final day, a wrestler with a 7-7 record (on the bubble) has everything to gain from a win, while an 8-6 opponent has already secured their rank and has little to lose. Data analysis of thousands of matches showed that 7-7 wrestlers win against 8-6 opponents nearly 80% of the time, even though their predicted win rate is less than 50%. The most telling evidence is the rematch: the next time these two wrestlers meet when neither is on the bubble, the previous winner loses much more often than expected. This suggests a quid pro quo: 'You let me win today when I need it, and I will let you win next time.' This pattern of collusion is invisible to the naked eye but obvious in the aggregate data, showing that even the most 'honorable' institutions can be corrupted by the right incentive structure.
