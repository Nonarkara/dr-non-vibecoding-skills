# ch03 — The Logic of Cheating

> Why people cheat and how the desire to win or survive drives dishonest behavior in every field.

Cheating is the act of getting more for less. It is a feature of almost every human endeavor where the stakes are high. People do not just cheat for money; they cheat for status, ease, or to avoid punishment. From the waitress who pockets tips to the CEO who manipulates stock prices, the underlying logic is the same: the perceived benefit of the cheat outweighs the perceived risk of being caught. Cheating is most common when there is a 'bright-line' incentive—a clear distinction between winning and losing, or passing and failing. When the line is sharp, the pressure to cross it increases. To catch a cheater, you must think like one. You must look for the patterns they leave behind, such as unusual clusters of success or results that are too perfect to be natural. Data is the best tool for this because while people lie, the numbers they generate often reveal the truth through their inconsistencies.
