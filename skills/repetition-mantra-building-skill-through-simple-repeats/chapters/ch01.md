> Introduces the idea of using a tiny, repeatable phrase as a learning tool. Explains why a simple sentence can serve as a mental anchor and how repetition turns it into a habit.

# The Mantra Concept

The core of this skill is a single short sentence that you repeat over and over. In the source text the sentence is “Hello world, this is a tiny book.” Though the words themselves carry little meaning, the act of saying them many times creates a pattern that the brain can latch onto. By treating the sentence as a repeatable unit, you turn an abstract goal—like “learn something new”—into a concrete action you can perform right now.

Repetition works because each utterance reinforces the neural pathways associated with the phrase. The more you say it, the easier it becomes to say it again, and the less mental effort is required to start the next round. This low‑effort entry point reduces the barrier to beginning a practice session, especially when you feel tired or distracted.

The mantra also serves as a metronome for your practice. You can decide to repeat it a set number of times per minute, or to continue until a timer rings. Either way, the mantra gives you a steady rhythm that you can hear, feel, or see, depending on whether you speak it aloud, whisper it, or write it down.

Finally, the mantra is portable. You do not need any special tools, books, or software. You only need the willingness to say the same short sentence again and again. This simplicity makes the technique applicable anywhere—on a commute, in a waiting room, or while waiting for a computer to boot.

In short, the mantra concept transforms a vague intention into a repeatable, observable action that you can start immediately and scale up as you wish.
