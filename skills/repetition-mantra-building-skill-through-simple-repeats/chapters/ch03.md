# ch03 — Measuring Progress and Adjusting Difficulty

> Describes how to use repetition counts and subjective feedback to gauge improvement and when to increase the challenge.

# Measuring Progress and Adjusting Difficulty

Progress in a repetition‑based practice is measured primarily by two signals: the number of repetitions you complete in a set time, and your subjective sense of ease or boredom while doing them.

First, track the raw count. If you aimed for 100 repetitions in five minutes and you consistently hit 110–120, your speed is increasing. If you stay around 80–90, you may need to adjust either your target or your technique (e.g., speak faster, reduce pauses).

Second, notice how the mantra feels. Early on, saying the same sentence may feel strange or even irritating. After several sessions, it often becomes neutral or even pleasant. When the mantra feels effortless and you can repeat it while thinking about other things, you have achieved fluency at that level.

When fluency appears, it is time to raise the difficulty. You can do this in three ways: increase the target count per session, shorten the time allowed for the same count, or add a slight variation—for example, say the mantra with a different intonation or add a small gesture each time. The variation keeps the brain engaged while preserving the core repetition principle.

If you notice increasing frustration or mental fatigue, scale back. Perhaps reduce the target, lengthen the rest between repetitions, or switch to a softer voice. The routine should stay challenging but not aversive.

Regular review—weekly or biweekly—helps you decide whether to keep the same parameters, increase them, or introduce a new variation. By treating the mantra as a measurable, adjustable unit, you create a feedback loop that drives steady improvement without needing complex assessments.
