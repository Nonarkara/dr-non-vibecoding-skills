# ch05 — Piecemeal Growth and Iterative Design

> Describes how to apply patterns gradually, letting each step reinforce larger patterns.

Designing with a pattern language is not a one‑off construction; it is a series of small, deliberate actions that accumulate into a coherent whole. Each act—planting a garden, adding a public square, or adjusting a building entrance—should be chosen to support existing larger patterns and to create the conditions for future smaller patterns. This iterative approach mirrors how real towns evolve: neighborhoods grow, public spaces emerge, and buildings adapt over decades. By working piece by piece, designers can respond to immediate needs while keeping the long‑term hierarchy in mind, ensuring that each addition strengthens the overall network.
