# ch10 — Evolving and Testing Patterns

> Shows how to treat patterns as hypotheses, test them, and improve them over time.

Patterns are presented as hypotheses about what arrangement solves a recurring problem. Designers should observe the outcomes of their implementations, gather feedback, and compare results to the expected invariant. When a pattern fails to produce the desired effect, it signals an opportunity to refine the solution or to discover a deeper invariant. This scientific attitude keeps the language dynamic, allowing it to adapt to new technologies, cultural shifts, and environmental challenges. Continuous improvement ensures that the pattern language remains a living tool rather than a static manual.
