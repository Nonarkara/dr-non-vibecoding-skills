# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## general

- If a city feels static, you aren't looking at the flows.
- Follow the water to find the power.
- Public space is a rehearsal for power relations.
- Luxury goods bring worlds together; basic goods keep them running.

## design

- Infrastructure is never neutral; it always favors someone.
- A 'dual city' requires a service class to maintain the elite's tech.
- Analyze a city by its speed, clarity, and access.

## process

- Don't assume 'community' means everyone inside is equal.
- Check the recycling bins to see where a city's waste flows next.

## product

- A city of flows still needs physical hardware to function.
- When a factory closes, the 'absence of flow' becomes the new urban reality.
- Cities are collection points for surplus goods.

## life

- Treat migration as a multidirectional loop, not a one-way trip.
- The 'bottom is rising' when labor flows lead to better local education.

## argument

- Look for 'paper sons'—the informal networks that bypass formal laws.

## code

- Digital networks don't kill cities; they make certain nodes more intense.
- If the internet is censored, the 'real' space usually is too.
- Virtual communities still need physical energy and hardware.

## writing

- Use 'community' as a tool for mobilization, but watch for its use as a tool for control.
- The 'golden age' of a neighborhood is usually a myth used to resist change.
