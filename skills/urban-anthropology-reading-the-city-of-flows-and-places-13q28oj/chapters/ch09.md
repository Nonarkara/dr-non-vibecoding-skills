# ch09 — The Politics of Public Space

> How plazas, streets, and markets serve as sites of social reality and contestation.

Public spaces are the stages where urban social life is performed. Plazas, markets, and streets are not just empty areas; they are sites where power relations are rehearsed and challenged. The design of these spaces often reflects a desire for control—such as wide boulevards that allow for military movement—but they are also sites for protest and informal trade. An agent should observe who has access to these spaces and how they are used. The 'flâneur' or urban observer can gain insights into class, gender, and age dynamics by watching how people move through and occupy public areas. When public space is privatized, it signals a shift in the city's social contract.
