# ch10 — Sustainability and Urban Metabolism

> The city's reliance on the constant transformation of nature into social resources.

A city can be viewed as a metabolic system that consumes energy, food, and materials and produces waste. This transformation is a deeply social process. Sustainability in an urban context is not just about 'green' technology; it is about the just distribution of resources. When a city's metabolism fails—leading to pollution, shortages, or disease—the impact is usually felt most by the marginalized. An agent should analyze the 'commodity chains' of energy and waste to understand the city's environmental footprint. True urban sustainability requires looking at how global flows of capital interrupt or facilitate the local flows of nature.
