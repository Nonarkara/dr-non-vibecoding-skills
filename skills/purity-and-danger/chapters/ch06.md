# ch06 — Comparative Cases: Sex, Food, and Caste

> Provides concrete examples from different cultures to illustrate the boundary model.

Across cultures, the same classification logic appears in varied guises. In the Havik of South India, a Brahmin’s daily bath protects the purity needed for worship; contact with a lower‑caste person or even a rope touched by that person can transmit impurity. In many societies, pork is taboo not because of health but because the animal is seen as unclean relative to cultural ideals. Sexual fluids are treated as dangerous pollutants, reflecting a symbolic hierarchy between genders. These examples show that the content of the rule (blood, pork, rope) is less important than the boundary it protects (sacred vs. profane, elite vs. commoner, male vs. female).
