# ch08 — Misreadings: Evolutionary and Moral Bias

> Critiques the tendency to label purity rules as primitive superstition or moral progress.

Early anthropologists like Frazer and Tylor saw purity rules as remnants of a magical past, while later scholars like Durkheim treated them as functional social glue. Both perspectives miss the nuance that purity rules can be both symbolic and functional at the same time. Labeling them as “primitive” obscures their role as sophisticated classification systems, and treating them as “advanced” discards the valuable symbolic content they carry. An AI must avoid these biases by focusing on the boundary logic rather than on a linear evolution narrative.
