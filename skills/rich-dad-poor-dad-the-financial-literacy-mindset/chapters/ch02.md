# ch02 — Defining Assets and Liabilities

> Provides a functional, cash-flow-based definition of assets versus liabilities.

Financial literacy begins with a simple rule: you must know the difference between an asset and a liability and buy assets. In the world of accounting, definitions can be complex, but for wealth building, the definition is based on cash flow. An asset is something that puts money in your pocket. A liability is something that takes money out of your pocket. Many people believe their home is their largest asset, but if it requires monthly payments for a mortgage, taxes, and maintenance without generating income, it is actually a liability. The rich focus on the asset column of their balance sheet, while the poor and middle class focus on their income statement, often mistaking liabilities for assets.
