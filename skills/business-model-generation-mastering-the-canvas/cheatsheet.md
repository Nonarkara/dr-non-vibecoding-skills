# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## process

- Start with the Value Proposition and Customer Segments before filling any other block.
- If a customer segment feels vague, split it into smaller, more homogeneous groups.
- Identify the single most expensive Key Resource and ask whether it can be leased, shared, or acquired from a partner.
- Map each Key Activity to the block it supports: production to Value Proposition, problem solving to Customer Relationships, platform to Channels.
- When forming a partnership, clarify which Key Resources or Activities you are gaining and which you are giving.
- Separate fixed and variable costs to see where scaling will save money and where it will not.
- Color‑code sticky notes by block (e.g., blue for Value Proposition, green for Customer Segments) to keep the canvas readable.
- After each workshop session, take a photo of the canvas and write down the three most important insights or open questions.
- Document the assumption behind each sticky note; later, validate or invalidate it with evidence.

## design

- Ask "What job is the customer trying to get done?" to test the strength of a Value Proposition.
- Choose Channels that match how your customers want to be reached, not just what is cheapest for you.
- For complex or high‑value products, invest in personal assistance; for low‑cost, high‑volume products, favor self‑service.
- Match pricing mechanism to purchase frequency: use subscriptions for recurring value, usage fees for metered consumption.
- If your model is cost‑driven, look for ways to eliminate frills, standardize offerings, and increase automation.
- If your model is value‑driven, be willing to spend more on Key Resources that directly enhance the Value Proposition.
- Use the canvas to sketch three alternatives: current state, a modest improvement, and a radical reinvention.
- Test a new Value Proposition by asking a handful of target customers: "Would you pay for this? How much?"
- When evaluating a Channel, estimate both the cost to reach a customer and the cost to serve them after the sale.
- If a Customer Segment shows low willingness to pay, consider whether you are solving a real pain or just a nice‑to‑have.
- For multi‑sided platforms, subsidize the side that is harder to attract until network effects take hold.
