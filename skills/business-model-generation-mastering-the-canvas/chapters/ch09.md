# ch09 — Key Partnerships

> Explains why businesses form partnerships, the four types (strategic alliances, coopetition, joint ventures, buyer‑supplier), and the three motivations (optimization, risk reduction, resource acquisition).

Key Partnerships are the network of suppliers and partners that enable a business model to work. Companies partner for four main reasons: strategic alliances between non‑competitors, coopetition (strategic partnerships between competitors), joint ventures to create new businesses, and buyer‑supplier relationships to secure reliable supplies. Partnerships are motivated by three goals: optimization and economy of scale (reducing cost by sharing infrastructure or outsourcing), reduction of risk and uncertainty (sharing risk in uncertain environments), and acquisition of particular resources and activities (gaining access to knowledge, licenses, customers, or capabilities that would be costly to develop alone). To define them, ask: Who are our Key Partners? Who are our key suppliers? Which Key Resources are we acquiring from partners? Which Key Activities do partners perform? Answering these helps decide whether to build or buy capabilities. For example, a mobile phone manufacturer may license an operating system instead of building one in‑house, reducing risk and time to market. A Blu‑ray consortium brought together competitors to develop the technology while each still competed on product sales.
