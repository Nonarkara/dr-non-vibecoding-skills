# ch08 — Key Activities

> Describes the most important actions a company must perform to operate its business model, categorizing them into production, problem solving, and platform/network management.

Key Activities are the critical actions a company must take to make its business model work. Like Key Resources, they are required to create and offer the Value Proposition, reach markets, maintain Customer Relationships, and earn revenues. Activities differ by model type but fall into three broad categories: production (designing, making, and delivering products in substantial quantities or superior quality, dominant in manufacturing), problem solving (coming up with new solutions to individual customer problems, typical of consultancies, hospitals, and other service organizations), and platform/network (managing and promoting a platform that enables interactions between other parties, seen in software, matchmaking, and brands that function as intermediaries). To identify them, ask: What Key Activities do our Value Propositions require? Our Distribution Channels? Customer Relationships? Revenue Streams? Answering this reveals where the business must focus its effort and investment. For example, a software maker’s key activities include coding, testing, and platform maintenance, while a retailer’s key activities involve procurement, inventory management, and store operations.
