# ch01 — The Business Model Canvas Overview

> Introduces the canvas as a visual language for business models, explains its nine blocks, and shows how to use it in group workshops with sticky notes or markers.

The Business Model Canvas is a single‑page template divided into nine building blocks: Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partnerships, and Cost Structure. It works best when printed large so teams can place ideas on it with Post‑it® notes or markers, enabling rapid sketching, discussion, and iteration. The canvas forces you to think about how each block influences the others—for example, a chosen Channel affects Customer Relationships and Cost Structure. By making the model explicit, gaps and mismatches become visible, allowing you to test assumptions before investing resources. The tool is neutral: it can describe existing businesses, explore new ventures, or compare alternative designs. Using the canvas in a workshop aligns stakeholders around a shared visual language, reduces misunderstandings, and accelerates decision‑making. The process typically starts with filling in the Value Proposition and Customer Segments, then works outward to Channels, Relationships, and Revenue Streams, and finally inward to Resources, Activities, Partnerships, and Costs. Iteration is encouraged: as you learn, you move notes around to reflect new insights.
