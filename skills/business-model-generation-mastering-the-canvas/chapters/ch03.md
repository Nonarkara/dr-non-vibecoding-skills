# ch03 — Value Propositions

> Explains how to craft the bundle of products and services that creates value for a chosen Customer Segment, detailing elements such as newness, performance, customization, design, brand, price, cost reduction, risk reduction, accessibility, and convenience.

The Value Proposition is the reason customers choose one company over another; it solves a problem or satisfies a need for a specific segment. It is not a feature list but a bundle of benefits that together deliver value. Elements that can compose a Value Proposition include newness (meeting a previously unperceived need), performance (improving speed, power, or effectiveness), customization (tailoring to individual needs), design (aesthetic appeal), brand/status (symbolic value), price (offering similar value for less), cost reduction (helping customers lower expenses), risk reduction (reducing perceived purchase risk), accessibility (making products available to those previously excluded), and convenience/usability (making things easier to get or use). To build a strong proposition, ask: What value do we deliver? Which customer problem are we solving? Which needs are we satisfying? What bundle of products and services are we offering to each segment? The answer should be expressed in terms of the customer’s jobs, pains, and gains. A clear, focused proposition guides decisions about Channels, Relationships, Revenue Streams, and the required Resources and Activities.
