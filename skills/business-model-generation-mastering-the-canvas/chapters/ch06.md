# ch06 — Revenue Streams

> Explains how a business earns cash from each Customer Segment, covering transaction versus recurring revenues, pricing mechanisms (fixed, dynamic, auction, yield management), and common stream types such as asset sale, usage fee, subscription, licensing, brokerage, and advertising.

Revenue Streams represent the cash a company generates from each Customer Segment, after subtracting costs to obtain earnings. A model can have two main types: transaction revenues (one‑time payments) and recurring revenues (ongoing payments for continued value delivery or post‑purchase support). Each stream may use different pricing mechanisms. Fixed pricing includes list price, product‑feature‑dependent, customer‑segment‑dependent, and volume‑dependent. Dynamic pricing includes negotiation (bargaining), yield management (perishable inventory like airline seats), real‑time‑market (supply and demand), and auctions (competitive bidding). Common stream types are: asset sale (selling ownership of a physical product), usage fee (charging based on use of a service), subscription fees (ongoing access to a service), licensing (permission to use intellectual property), brokerage fees (commission for matching two parties), and advertising (fees for exposing a brand to an audience). To design streams, ask: For what value are our customers really willing to pay? What do they currently pay? How do they prefer to pay? How much does each stream contribute to total revenues? Answering these helps match pricing to willingness to pay and ensures the model generates sufficient cash flow.
