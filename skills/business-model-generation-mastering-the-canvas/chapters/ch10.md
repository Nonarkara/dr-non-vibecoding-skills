# ch10 — Cost Structure

> Describes all costs incurred to operate a business model, distinguishing between cost‑driven and value‑driven models, and breaking down fixed versus variable costs and economies of scale and scope.

The Cost Structure block lists all expenses required to operate the business model. Costs arise from creating and delivering value, maintaining relationships, and generating revenue. Models can be broadly classified as cost‑driven (focused on minimizing expenses wherever possible) or value‑driven (willing to incur higher costs to create premium value, as seen in luxury hotels or personalized services). Many models lie between these extremes. Costs have two main behaviors: fixed (remain the same regardless of output, such as salaries, rent, and manufacturing facilities) and variable (change proportionally with volume, such as raw materials or direct labor). Understanding cost behavior reveals economies of scale (cost per unit falls as output rises due to bulk purchasing, spreading fixed costs, etc.) and economies of scope (cost advantages from a broader range of operations, such as shared marketing or distribution across multiple products). To analyze costs, ask: What are the most important costs inherent in our model? Which Key Resources are most expensive? Which Key Activities are most expensive? Answering this highlights where cost savings can be pursued without breaking the value proposition. For instance, a no‑frills airline minimizes costs by using a single aircraft type, point‑to‑point routes, and online sales, while a premium hotel invests heavily in luxurious facilities and personalized service.
