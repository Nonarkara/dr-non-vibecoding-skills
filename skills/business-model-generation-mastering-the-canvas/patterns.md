# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## Unbundling Business Models

**When to use:** When a single organization contains conflicting business types (customer relationship, product innovation, infrastructure) that hinder performance.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify the three fundamental business types present in the model.
2. Separate each type into its own distinct entity or business unit.
3. Allow each entity to focus on its own economic, competitive, and cultural logic.
4. Coordinate through clear interfaces or partnerships as needed.
5. Monitor for synergies and re‑integrate only when benefits outweigh conflicts.

## Long Tail

**When to use:** When distribution and inventory costs are low enough to profit from selling many niche items in small volumes.

**Recipe:**

1. Build a catalog that includes a large number of low‑demand products.
2. Use digital or on‑demand fulfillment to keep inventory costs minimal.
3. Leverage recommendation and search tools to help customers find niche items.
4. Earn revenue from the cumulative sales of many low‑volume items.
5. Continuously expand the catalog based on customer search and purchase data.

## Multi‑sided Platform

**When to use:** When two or more interdependent Customer Segments must be brought together to create value (e.g., readers & advertisers, buyers & sellers).

**Recipe:**

1. Identify each side of the platform and their respective needs.
2. Design a core service that attracts at least one side first (the "anchor" side).
3. Use the anchor side to draw in the other side(s) by offering access, data, or transactions.
4. Balance pricing and subsidies so each side pays according to the value they receive.
5. Facilitate interactions and monitor network effects to grow both sides.

**Chapter:** ch02

## FREE as a Business Model

**When to use:** When offering a core product or service at no charge accelerates adoption and enables monetization through complementary streams.

**Recipe:**

1. Determine which offering can be given free without destroying the ability to earn elsewhere.
2. Make the free version easily accessible and valuable enough to attract a large user base.
3. Identify monetization paths: premium upgrades, advertising, data, or cross‑sales.
4. Limit free features to create a clear upgrade path to paid tiers.
5. Track conversion rates and adjust the free/paid balance to maximize lifetime value.

## Open Business Model

**When to use:** When internal resources are insufficient to create all needed value and external ideas, technologies, or partnerships can expand the offering.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify which parts of the Value Proposition could be sourced externally.
2. Choose an openness mechanism: licensing, joint development, crowdsourcing, or open APIs.
3. Define clear terms for intellectual property, revenue sharing, and governance.
4. Integrate external contributions into the core offering or platform.
5. Measure the impact on innovation speed, cost, and market reach.

## Product Innovation Focus

**When to use:** When a business aims to differentiate through superior performance, newness, or customization rather than price or intimacy.

**Recipe:**

1. Target Customer Segments that value cutting‑edge features or novel solutions.
2. Allocate Key Resources to R&D, design, and intellectual property creation.
3. Structure Key Activities around prototyping, testing, and iterative improvement.
4. Price based on the value of innovation, often using premium or dynamic pricing.
5. Protect innovations through patents, trade secrets, or lead‑time advantages.

**Chapter:** ch03

## Infrastructure Management

**When to use:** When the business model relies on delivering high‑volume, repetitive transactions efficiently.

**Recipe:**

1. Build or acquire platforms that standardize and automate core processes.
2. Focus Key Activities on operating, maintaining, and scaling the platform.
3. Use Key Resources such as IT systems, networks, and distribution hubs.
4. Aim for low unit cost through economies of scale and tight process control.
5. Serve Customer Segments that prioritize reliability, low price, or convenience.

**Chapter:** ch08

## Customer Relationship Intimacy

**When to use:** When the business model seeks to earn revenue by deeply understanding and serving specific customer needs over time.

**Recipe:**

1. Select Customer Segments with high lifetime value and complex needs.
2. Invest in Key Resources such as skilled personnel, customer data, and relationship tools.
3. Design Key Activities around personal assistance, dedicated account management, and community building.
4. Shape Revenue Streams around subscription, usage fees, or long‑term contracts.
5. Measure success via retention, upsell rates, and customer satisfaction scores.

**Chapter:** ch05
