# ch08 — The Role of Memory and Attention in Language Processing

> Describes how limited working memory and selective attention shape the way people understand and produce sentences.

Humans can hold only a few chunks of information in working memory. Long sentences are parsed by chunking phrases, and attention is allocated to salient nouns and verbs. When a message overloads memory—by inserting many subordinate clauses or rare words—comprehension drops sharply. The brain also uses predictive coding: it anticipates upcoming words based on context, which speeds up processing but also makes it vulnerable to surprise. An AI should therefore keep sentences short, place the most important information early, and avoid unnecessary syntactic depth. Providing visual or auditory cues (bolding, pauses) can guide attention to the critical parts of the message.
