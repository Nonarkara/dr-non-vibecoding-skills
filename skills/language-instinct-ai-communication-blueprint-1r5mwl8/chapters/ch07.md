# ch07 — Dialect, Prestige, and Perceived Competence

> Shows how social factors influence judgments of grammaticality and how to navigate them in AI‑human interaction.

Listeners often equate standard dialects with intelligence and non‑standard varieties with lack of competence, even though grammatical complexity is present in all dialects. Studies of African American Vernacular English and other regional forms reveal systematic rules (e.g., negative concord, habitual aspect) that differ from Standard English but are no less rule‑governed. When an AI uses a dialect that matches the user’s background, perceived credibility rises; mismatched dialects can trigger bias and reduce trust. Therefore, an AI should detect the user’s linguistic style (through lexical choices, phonological markers, or syntactic patterns) and adapt its output to mirror that style while preserving clarity.
