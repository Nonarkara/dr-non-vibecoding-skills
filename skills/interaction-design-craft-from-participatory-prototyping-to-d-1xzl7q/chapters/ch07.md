# ch07 — Designing Within Constraints: Cost, Simplicity, and Clarity

> Both the Lisa and Mac teams used constraints—limited hardware, cost goals, and the desire for simplicity—to drive clearer interaction solutions rather than seeing them as obstacles.

The Lisa project began as a four‑year, twenty‑person effort to create a document‑based workstation with pull‑down menus, overlapping windows, and a white background—features that pushed the hardware of the day. When the Mac team had to shrink the Lisa feature set to fit on a cheaper machine, they did not simply cut features; they re‑thought them. Atkinson’s white‑background decision, for example, faced engineering objections about power consumption, but he insisted the team find a way to make it work because WYSIWYG mattered to users. The Mac’s small team of ten people, led by Atkinson’s design leadership, produced a machine that shipped only eight months after Lisa but at a fraction of the price. Constraints forced the designers to ask, ‘What is the simplest way to achieve this goal?’ leading to inventions like the single‑button mouse, the menu bar, and the desktop metaphor. The lesson is that constraints are not limits to avoid but lenses that focus creativity on what truly matters to the user.
