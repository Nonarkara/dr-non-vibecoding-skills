# ch08 — Designer‑Engineer Partnerships: The Tesler‑Atkinson Loop

> The most effective interaction designs emerged from tight collaboration where one partner built prototypes at night and the other tested them with real users by day, creating a rapid feedback loop.

At Apple, Bill Atkinson (designer/programmer) and Larry Tesler (usability advocate) instituted a rhythm: Atkinson would work through the night coding a prototype of a new interface idea—menus, windows, or graphics routines—while Tesler spent the day running tests with novice users, often fresh Apple hires who had never used a computer. At day’s end Tesler gave Atkinson a concise report of what worked, what confused users, and what needed change. Atkinson then used that feedback to refine the prototype before the next night’s build. This cycle allowed them to explore many ideas quickly, discard the ones that failed observation, and double‑down on the ones that felt intuitive. The same pattern had appeared earlier at PARC with Tesler and Tim Mott on Gypsy. The takeaway for interaction designers is to pair a builder who can turn ideas into testable artifacts quickly with an evaluator who can watch real people use those artifacts and report back in plain language.
