# ch03 — Prototyping as a Way of Thinking: Mini Mouse and Gypsy

> Tesler proved that sophisticated interaction ideas could be tested quickly by building minimal, usable prototypes. The Mini Mouse editor let newcomers learn basic text editing in five minutes, and the Gypsy project turned those insights into cut, copy, paste, and a modeless cursor.

Frustrated by the steep learning curve of the NLS system, Tesler created Mini Mouse—a one‑button mouse text editor written in Smalltalk. He ran a ‘blank screen experiment’ with a secretary who had never used a computer; she described exactly how she would delete and insert text using only the mouse and keyboard. From her natural actions he inferred a usable design. Mini Mouse was so easy that passers‑by could learn it in five minutes, compared with a week for NLS. This success gave Tesler the freedom to pursue Gypsy, a production‑ready editor for Ginn and Company. Gypsy incorporated Pentti Kanerva’s delete/insert technique, introduced the terms ‘cut’ and ‘paste’ for moving text, and placed the cursor between characters to make insertion obvious. The project showed that a prototype need not be a throw‑away sketch; it can be a functional piece of software that both tests ideas and becomes the basis for the final product.
