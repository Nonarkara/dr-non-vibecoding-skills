## Core principles

- Culture is a web of meanings that actors weave through their everyday actions.
- Thick description means describing an action together with its context, symbolism, and the actor’s own interpretation.
- The emic perspective captures the insider’s view of meaning, while the etic perspective provides the outsider’s analytical lens.
- Symbols acquire meaning only within the network of cultural practices that surround them.
- Anthropological interpretation must stay open‑ended; it should never reduce a culture to a single rule or pattern.
- Fieldwork is the only way to access the lived experience that gives symbols their significance.
- Interpretation is a dialogue between the observed action and the interpreter’s conceptual tools, not a one‑way extraction of facts.
- Rituals are staged performances that reveal the hierarchy of values in a society.
- Every cultural description is provisional and must be revisable when new contextual data appear.
