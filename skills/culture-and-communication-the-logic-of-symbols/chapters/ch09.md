# ch09 — The Grammar of Non-Verbal Acts

> How clothing, food, and layout function as a language.

Non-verbal dimensions of culture are organized in patterned sets, much like the words in a sentence. There are 'grammatical rules' for how we wear clothes, how we arrange furniture, and how we cook food. These rules allow us to generate meaning without speaking. A uniform tells you a person's rank and role immediately. The layout of a village tells you who is important and who is an outsider. These codes are simpler than spoken language because they rely on familiar conventions rather than spontaneous creation. If a person invents a private symbol, it fails to communicate until it is explained. Therefore, non-verbal language depends entirely on shared, public codes.
