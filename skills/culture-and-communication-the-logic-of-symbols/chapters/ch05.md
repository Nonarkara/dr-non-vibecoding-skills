# ch05 — The Logic of Magic

> Why magic is a communicative act rather than just bad science.

Magic is often dismissed as a primitive mistake in understanding cause and effect. However, magic follows a specific communicative logic. It occurs when a person treats a symbol as if it were a signal. A sorcerer might take a lock of hair from a victim. In the sorcerer's mind, the hair is a sign of the person. By destroying the hair while reciting a spell, the sorcerer expects to harm the person at a distance. The error is triple: they mistake a metaphor (the label 'this is X's hair') for a sign, treat that sign as a natural index, and then interpret that index as a signal that triggers a physical result. Magic is an expressive act—like a military coup that focuses on capturing the palace rather than killing the army—that people treat as a technical act.
