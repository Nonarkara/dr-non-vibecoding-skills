# ch01 — The Social Score

> Distinguishing between the underlying structure of a culture and the individual actions of its members.

Social life is comparable to a musical performance. There is a 'score'—the underlying structure of ideas and rules—and the 'performance'—what people actually do in their daily lives. A poor or incompetent performance does not mean the score itself is flawed. To understand a culture, an observer cannot rely on a single event or person. Instead, one must look at many different 'performances' to infer the abstract reality that guides them all. This approach shifts the focus from simple economic transactions to acts of communication. For example, giving a gift is not just a transfer of wealth; it is a message. If you give an identical gift back, you are signaling equality. If you give labor in exchange for money, you are signaling a hierarchy. The meaning is found in the relationship and the context, not just the object being exchanged.
