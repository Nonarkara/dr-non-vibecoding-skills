# ch04 — Closing Moves: Quiet Aphorisms and Open‑Ended Questions

> Explains how Non ends essays with a lingering thought, often an aphorism or a question that invites the reader to keep thinking.

Non’s conclusions rarely summarize; they instead leave a lingering note. A typical closing is a short aphorism—"the best of all possible worlds"—or a rhetorical question that circles back to the opening hook. The sentence often ends with a dash, ellipsis, or a soft period, creating a pause that feels like a breath after a long exhale. This technique turns the conclusion into a reflective space rather than a tidy wrap‑up, encouraging the reader to continue the internal dialogue sparked by the essay. By avoiding overt summarization, Non maintains the essay’s raw, ongoing feel.
