# Patterns

Reusable moves extracted from the source. Each is a recipe — context, then steps, then the chapter to read for the full argument.

## blunt_open

**When to use:** When you need immediate impact or a strong opinion statement.

**Recipe:**

1. Start with a direct claim about the topic.
2. Follow with a brief personal anecdote that illustrates the claim.
3. Insert a thinker reference to add weight.
4. End with a short pause or dash.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## hook_then_turn

**When to use:** For narrative essays that begin with a vivid scene.

**Recipe:**

1. Describe a concrete image in one sentence.
2. Pivot with "so I thought" or similar phrase.
3. Introduce the personal relevance.
4. Tie back to the main argument using a thinker name.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## stream_consciousness

**When to use:** When writing reflective journal entries or brainstorming drafts.

**Recipe:**

1. Set a timer (10‑15 minutes).
2. Write continuously without editing.
3. When the flow naturally pauses, note the emerging theme.
4. Insert the relevant theory or quote after the pause.

**Chapter:** ch03

---

## before_after

**When to use:** To illustrate a change in perception or habit.

**Recipe:**

1. State the situation before the event.
2. Describe the event briefly.
3. State the situation after the event.
4. Conclude with a reflective line that links both states.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## thinker_load

**When to use:** When an essay needs an intellectual anchor.

**Recipe:**

1. Introduce the main idea.
2. Insert the name of a philosopher or author as a clause.
3. Explain how that thinker’s concept aligns with your experience.
4. Proceed with personal narrative.

**Chapter:** ch02

---

## quiet_aphorism_close

**When to use:** To end an essay with lingering thought.

**Recipe:**

1. Summarize the core insight in a single, pithy sentence.
2. Add a dash or ellipsis for a pause.
3. Optionally, pose an open‑ended question.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## embedded_dialogue

**When to use:** When internal conflict needs dramatization.

**Recipe:**

1. Write a sentence describing a thought.
2. Insert a short quoted line of imagined speech.
3. Continue the narrative, showing the impact of that dialogue.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## contrast_reversal

**When to use:** To highlight tension between two opposing ideas.

**Recipe:**

1. State the first idea.
2. Introduce the opposite idea using "but" or "yet".
3. Explain how the two coexist.
4. Close with a reflective comment.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## non_possessive_love

**When to use:** When discussing relationships without ownership language.

**Recipe:**

1. State the common possessive view.
2. Reject it with a clear statement of non‑possessiveness.
3. Provide a philosopher’s quote supporting the view.
4. End with a question about true love.

**Chapter:** ch08
