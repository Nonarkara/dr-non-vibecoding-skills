# ch01 — Spotting Boilerplate

> Learn how to quickly detect when a text is filled with duplicated sentences and why that matters for downstream work.

When you open a draft, the fastest way to gauge its health is to scan for identical sentences that appear more than twice. In the test document, the same line repeats dozens of times, which is a classic sign of boilerplate. This repetition usually occurs because the author copied a template placeholder or used a quick‑draft technique. Recognizing this pattern early saves time: you know the text needs condensation before any design or coding can proceed. The detection process involves three steps: (1) visual scan for obvious repeats, (2) use a text‑search tool to count occurrences of each sentence, and (3) flag any sentence that exceeds a threshold of three appearances. Once flagged, you can decide whether the sentence is a structural cue (e.g., a section header) or pure filler. Only structural cues stay; pure filler is removed or replaced. This chapter equips you with the mental checklist and simple tooling to spot boilerplate in any document.
