# ch02 — Understanding the Role of the Repeated Text

> Distinguish between intentional placeholders, structural markers, and accidental duplication.

Not every repeated line is useless. Some authors use a repeated phrase as a placeholder for later content, while others repeat a heading to reinforce hierarchy. To decide what to keep, ask three questions: (1) Does the sentence convey unique information? (2) Is it serving as a marker for a section or a template token? (3) Will its removal break the logical flow? If the answer is no to all, the line is pure filler. In the test document, the sentence "This is a test book with enough text" provides no unique data and does not act as a heading, so it is pure filler. Conversely, a repeated phrase like "Chapter 1: Introduction" might be intentional to label each page. Understanding the role prevents accidental loss of needed structure and guides you to replace placeholders with meaningful content later.
