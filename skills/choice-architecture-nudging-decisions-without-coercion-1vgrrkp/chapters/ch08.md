# ch08 — Temptation, Hot‑Cold Empathy Gap, and Planner‑Doer Conflict

> Explains how arousal changes preferences and how commitment devices can align short‑term actions with long‑term goals.

When users are in a hot state—hungry, stressed, or excited—they often choose the immediate, tempting option. In a cold state, they evaluate long‑term benefits more rationally. The hot‑cold empathy gap means planners underestimate how much their future selves will be swayed by temptation. Commitment devices—such as pre‑commitment contracts, alarms placed out of reach, or public bets—help the Planner lock in the desired behavior before the hot state arrives.
