# ch03 — Basic Empirical Operations and Representational Theory

> Describes the observable operations—equality, order, equality of intervals, equality of ratios—that ground each scale type. Shows how representational theory links these operations to numerical transformations and why they are the source of invariance properties.

Representational theory holds that measurement is not about labeling objects but about representing relationships between them. Stevens called these relationships ‘basic empirical operations’. For all scales we can determine equality (are two objects the same with respect to the attribute?). For all scales except nominal we can determine order (which is greater?). For interval and ratio scales we can determine equality of intervals (is the difference between A and B the same as between C and D?). For ratio scales we can also determine equality of ratios (is A:B the same as C:D?). The type of scale is defined by which of these operations are empirically meaningful and how they transform under numerical changes. If the purpose of measurement is to preserve equality of ratios, then only multiplication by a constant leaves that purpose unchanged—hence a ratio scale. If the purpose is to preserve equality of intervals, then multiplying and adding a constant works—hence an interval scale. Thus, the invariance properties of a scale are not arbitrary; they follow directly from which empirical operations we intend to represent. When evaluating a claim, the AI should ask which basic empirical operations the author intends to capture and whether there is evidence that those operations hold in the data.
