# ch04 — Suppes’ Axiomatic Approach to Extensive Measurement

> Introduces the axiomatic method that treats measurement as proving a homomorphism from an empirical relational structure to the positive real numbers. Covers the six axioms for extensive structures (e.g., length) and the resulting representation and uniqueness theorems.

Suppes shifted measurement from a conventionalist stance to a realist one by demanding that the empirical relational system be investigated and confirmed before measurement is claimed. He formalized extensive quantities (those that can be concatenated, like rods) with six axioms: transitivity of order, associativity of concatenation, monotonicity, compensability, positivity, and the Archimedean condition (any rod, no matter how short, can be extended enough to exceed any longer rod). From these axioms he proved a representation theorem: any structure satisfying them is isomorphic to a subsystem of the positive real numbers, yielding a ratio scale. Moreover, a uniqueness theorem shows that any two such representations differ only by a similarity transformation (multiplication by a positive constant). This gives a firm mathematical basis to Stevens’ scale classification and shows that the scale type depends on the structure of the empirical relational system. For the AI, this chapter provides a template: to claim measurement of an extensive attribute, first specify the empirical relations (order, concatenation operation), then test whether the axioms hold; only then can numbers be assigned with confidence that they reflect real ratios.
