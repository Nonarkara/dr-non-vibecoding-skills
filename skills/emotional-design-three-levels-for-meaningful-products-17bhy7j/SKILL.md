## When to load this skill

This skill teaches how to shape products so they engage users at the visceral (appearance), behavioral (use), and reflective (meaning) levels. By aligning aesthetics, usability, and personal significance, designers can create objects that attract, are easy to use, and foster lasting emotional bonds.

## Core principles

- Visceral design influences immediate judgments of safety and desirability through appearance, touch, and sound.
- Behavioral design determines how well a product supports goal‑directed action and the pleasure of use.
- Reflective design shapes self‑image, memories, and the long‑term meaning users attach to a product.
- Positive affect broadens thinking, making users more creative and tolerant of minor problems.
- Negative affect narrows focus, which is useful for danger detection but hinders creative problem solving.
- Effective products balance all three levels; a strength at one level cannot compensate for weakness at another.
- Designers must match the product’s personality to the audience’s culture, experience, and context.
- Emotional responses are rooted in biology but are modified by learning, culture, and individual experience.
