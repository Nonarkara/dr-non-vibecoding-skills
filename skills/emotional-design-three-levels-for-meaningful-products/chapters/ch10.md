# ch10 — Design Process and Iteration

> Outlines how to integrate emotional design goals into a practical workflow, from research to testing and refinement.

Emotional design is not a one‑time checklist; it requires ongoing attention to how users feel at each level. Begin by researching the target audience’s visceral preferences, behavioral skills, and reflective values through interviews, observation, and cultural probes. Generate concepts that explicitly address each level: sketch appearance options, storyboard interaction flows, and draft narratives or personalization features. Build low‑fidelity prototypes to test visceral reactions (first‑glance appeal) and behavioral usability (ease of completing core tasks). Use reflective probes—asking users what the product says about them or what memories it might evoke—to gauge meaning. Iterate based on feedback: adjust form to improve immediate appeal, refine controls to reduce errors, and deepen personalization options to strengthen identity fit. Throughout, measure affect directly (e.g., self‑report scales, facial expression analysis) alongside traditional metrics like time on task and error rate. Because affect influences cognition, a design that passes usability tests but leaves users feeling anxious or indifferent will likely fail in the wild. Repeating this loop ensures the final product balances attraction, usability, and meaning.
