# ch02 — Behavioral Design

> Covers the pleasure and effectiveness that arise when a product supports skilled, goal‑directed action. Describes how usability, feedback, and the feeling of accomplishment shape moment‑to‑moment experience.

At the behavioral level, users evaluate how well a product lets them achieve their goals. This includes functional correctness, performance speed, and the ease of learning and remembering how to operate it. When a product matches the user’s mental model, provides clear and immediate feedback, and allows smooth, error‑free interaction, the experience feels fluid and satisfying. Skilled use—such as typing on a well‑designed keyboard or chopping with a balanced knife—generates pleasure from mastery. Designers improve behavioral quality by reducing unnecessary steps, providing affordances that suggest correct actions, and ensuring that errors are obvious and easy to correct. For instance, a software dialog that highlights the required field in red and offers inline validation helps users correct mistakes without frustration quickly. The behavioral level also underpins the sense of accomplishment: when users feel they have successfully completed a task, positive affect reinforces future use. Therefore, behavioral design is not just about efficiency; it is about creating a loop of action, feedback, and reward that keeps users engaged.
