# ch07 — Product Personality and Self‑Image

> Explains how a product’s consistent traits—tone, appearance, behavior—convey a personality that users can relate to or aspire to.

Just as people have personalities, products can be perceived as friendly, authoritative, playful, or reliable. This personality emerges from the sum of design decisions across the three levels: the choice of colors and materials (visceral), the way controls respond and feedback is given (behavioral), and the narratives or values communicated in marketing and customization options (reflective). A consistent personality helps users predict how the product will behave and what it says about them when they use it. For example, a budgeting app that uses calm blues, simple language, and encouraging messages conveys a trustworthy, supportive personality, inviting users to feel in control of their finances. Inconsistent signals—such as a toy that looks cute but emits harsh, aggressive sounds—create confusion and erode trust. Designers should define the desired personality early, then verify that every touchpoint reinforces it. They must also consider the audience’s self‑image: a product that makes users feel competent, innovative, or caring will be adopted more readily than one that clashes with how they see themselves. Personality is not static; it can evolve with the product line, but changes should be deliberate and communicated to avoid alienating existing users.
