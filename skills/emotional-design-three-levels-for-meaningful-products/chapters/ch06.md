# ch06 — Design for Memory and Sentiment

> Shows how objects become vessels for personal memory and why sentimental value often outweighs pure aesthetics or usability.

People attach emotional weight to objects that remind them of people, places, or events, regardless of the object’s visual appeal. A chipped mug, a faded ticket stub, or a mass‑produced souvenir can become cherished because it triggers a vivid recollection. This sentimental power arises from the reflective level, where the object serves as a cue for a stored story. Designers can enhance this effect by making the object a clear, unique reminder of a specific experience—through shape, material, or embedded data (e.g., a USB drive shaped like a key from a hotel room). Conversely, they can avoid unintended negative memories by steering clear of forms that resemble traumatic stimuli for the target audience. In product families, offering a line of keepsakes (engraved jewelry, custom‑printed photo books) lets users attach personal meaning. Because sentiment is highly individual, the most reliable approach is to give users ways to imprint their own stories onto the product, rather than trying to prescribe a universal emotional trigger. When an object successfully evokes a pleasant memory, users are more likely to keep it, repair it, and pass it on, extending the product’s life and emotional resonance.
