# Cheatsheet

Imperative decision rules from the source, grouped by category. Each is the kind of sentence you would write on an index card.

## process

- If the task feels overwhelming, shrink it until it feels doable.
- Measure users’ self‑report mood after using a prototype.
- When designing for high‑risk environments, run a stress test with simulated alarms.
- Ask users what the product says about them after a week of use.
- Schedule a brief, fun warm‑up before any brainstorming session.
- Document the personality statement and review it at each design milestone.

## design

- Use rounded, smooth edges for objects that will be held or touched often.
- Provide a single, obvious way to recover from an error.
- When users are stressed, make alarm signals impossible to miss.
- Add a brief, pleasant sound or vibration each time a user completes a small step.
- Show the user’s progress toward a goal with a visible bar or counter.
- Let users engrave, choose colors, or add accessories to make the product theirs.
- Research local color meanings before choosing a palette for a new market.
- Test first‑glance appeal with a five‑second exposure test.
- If a control is rarely used, hide it until needed to reduce visual clutter.
- Use consistent icons and labels across all screens of an application.
- Replace long manuals with short, contextual tips that appear at the point of need.
- Use symmetrical layouts for objects meant to feel stable and trustworthy.
- If the product is a keepsake, make its shape unique to the memory it should evoke.

## writing

- Speak in the tone of voice that matches the product’s intended personality.
