## Use Pleasant Aesthetics to Boost Creativity

**When to use:** When users need to generate ideas or explore alternatives.

**Recipe:**

1. Select warm lighting, rounded forms, and harmonious colors for the product or environment.
2. Add subtle, pleasing details such as soft textures or gentle sounds.
3. Test that the aesthetic induces a relaxed, positive mood before idea‑generation activities.

## Add Small Rewards to Induce Positive Affect

**When to use:** During repetitive or frustrating tasks to maintain motivation.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify moments where users complete a micro‑goal.
2. Provide a brief, pleasant signal (sound, animation, haptic pulse) at each completion.
3. Ensure the reward is noticeable but not distracting from the main task.

**Chapter:** ch04

---

## Design Clear, Unmistakable Alerts for High‑Stress Situations

**When to use:** When users must react quickly to danger or system failure.

**Recipe:**

1. Use high‑contrast visual signals (flashing red, bold icons) combined with loud, distinct auditory tones.
2. Make the alert impossible to ignore by placing it in the central field of view or using vibration.
3. Pair the alert with a single, obvious correct action (e.g., press big red button).

**Chapter:** ch09

## Provide Continuous Visibility of Critical Information

**When to use:** In tasks where loss of situational awareness leads to error.

**Recipe:**

1. Determine which data must always be known (e.g., altitude, pressure, speed).
2. Display those items in a fixed, uncluttered region of the interface.
3. Use size, color, or movement to draw attention only when values approach unsafe limits.

**Chapter:** ch09

## Create a Narrative Hook for Reflective Attachment

**When to use:** When aiming for long‑term emotional bond through story or memory.

**Recipe:**

1. Identify a meaningful experience, value, or aspiration of the target audience.
2. Embed a symbolic element (shape, material, custom engraving) that recalls that experience.
3. Allow users to personalize the element to make the story their own.

**Chapter:** ch06

## Match Controls to Users’ Mental Models

**When to use:** When designing for efficient, error‑free operation.

**Recipe:**

1. Research how the target audience expects similar devices to work.
2. Arrange controls and feedback to match those expectations.
3. Validate with usability testing that actions produce the expected outcome without hesitation.

**Chapter:** ch02

## Use Consistent Personality Signals Across Touchpoints

**When to use:** When launching a product line or updating an existing product.

**Recipe:**

1. Define a short personality statement (e.g., trustworthy, playful, authoritative).
2. Check that visual language, tone of voice, and interaction style all reflect that statement.
3. Revise any element that sends a conflicting signal before release.

**Chapter:** ch07

---

## Tailor Appearance to Cultural Symbols

**When to use:** When entering a new geographic or subcultural market.

**Recipe:**

1. Research colors, patterns, and forms that carry positive or negative meanings locally.
2. Adjust the product’s exterior or packaging to use the positively coded symbols.
3. Avoid shapes or images that are taboo or associated with negative concepts in that culture.

**Chapter:** ch08
