# ch01 — Method Phase Map

> Shows how the 51 methods split across the five design phases and why phase alignment matters.

### Overview
The IDEO Method Cards are organized into four primary categories that map onto the classic design process: **Learn** (research), **Look** (observation), **Ask** (interview & inquiry), and **Try** (prototyping & testing). A fifth implicit phase, **Implement**, gathers the outputs of the previous phases into production.

### Phase Definitions
- **Learn**: Gather background knowledge, secondary research, and contextual data.
- **Look**: Observe people in their natural environments; methods include Fly on the Wall, Shadowing, and Behavioral Mapping.
- **Ask**: Directly query participants through interviews, surveys, or role‑playing; includes Extreme User Interviews, Five Whys, and Structured Interviews.
- **Try**: Build low‑fidelity prototypes, simulations, or experiences; methods include Experience Prototype, Paper Prototyping, and Quick‑and‑Dirty Prototyping.
- **Implement**: Not a card category but the stage where the chosen solution is refined and shipped.

### Why Phase Alignment Matters
Using a synthesis method like Affinity Diagrams before any data exists yields no insight. Conversely, applying a prototyping method before you understand user needs leads to wasted effort. The AI should first locate the current project phase, then filter the method list to those tagged for that phase.
