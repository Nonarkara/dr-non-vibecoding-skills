# ch09 — Corporate Kinship and the Dadia

> Kinship groups function as autonomous corporate organizations that manage their own temples and internal affairs independently of other ties.

Kinship in Bali is organized into corporate groups known as dadias. These groups are defined by descent from a common ancestor and are centered on a private kinship temple. A dadia functions much like a voluntary organization or a seka; it is an independent entity that manages its own property, rituals, and internal disputes. While gentry dadias tend to be more hierarchical and geographically widespread, commoner dadias are usually localized within a single hamlet. Kinship is just one of many overlapping structures an individual belongs to, and it must constantly negotiate with the demands of the hamlet, the irrigation society, and the state. The dadia provides a sense of historical continuity and shared identity through the worship of deified ancestors.
