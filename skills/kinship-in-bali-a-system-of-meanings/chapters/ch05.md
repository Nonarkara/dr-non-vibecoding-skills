# ch05 — The Language of Titles

> Balinese society uses a system of inherited titles to define prestige and etiquette, separating social rank from actual political power.

Nearly every Balinese person holds an inherited title that determines their social rank. These titles are broadly divided into the gentry, known as 'insiders,' and the commoners, known as 'outsiders.' This system of prestige is distinct from political power or wealth; a high-titled person may be poor, while a commoner may be influential. Titles primarily regulate etiquette, such as seating arrangements and the level of language used in conversation. Marriage is also strictly regulated by title, with strong social sanctions against women marrying men of lower rank. While titles provide a basis for social identity, they do not always correspond to organized groups, and their relative importance varies across different regions of the island.
