# ch03 — The Hamlet: Implementing Community

> The hamlet, or banjar, is the primary secular community for most people, managing daily life, work, and local regulation through a council of citizens.

The hamlet is the most significant social group for the average person outside of their immediate family. It is the organization responsible for local regulation, public welfare, and the preservation of moral behavior. Membership in the hamlet council is mandatory for all adult men, who must have a female partner to assist with ritual duties. Decisions within the council are made through absolute unanimity; if there is vocal opposition, a proposal is typically abandoned. The hamlet owns significant property, including the land where houses are built, meeting halls, and musical instruments. It has the power to grant or revoke residency rights, making it a powerful tool for social control. It also manages the labor-intensive processes of burials and cremations for its members.
