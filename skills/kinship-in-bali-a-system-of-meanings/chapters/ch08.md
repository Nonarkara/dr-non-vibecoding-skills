# ch08 — The Principle of Seka

> Balinese social life is organized through the 'seka' principle: creating a specific, egalitarian group for every distinct purpose.

The term 'seka' refers to any organized group and embodies the principle of functional specificity. In Bali, the rule is 'one group for one purpose.' If a new need arises—such as forming a dance troupe, a harvesting team, or a construction group—a new seka is established rather than adding the task to an existing organization. Within a seka, all members are considered equal in their rights and duties, regardless of their status or titles in the outside world. This egalitarian structure and the focus on a single goal allow these groups to operate autonomously and avoid the complications of broader social hierarchies. This results in a highly complex social landscape where individuals belong to many different, overlapping organizations.
