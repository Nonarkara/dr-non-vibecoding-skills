# ch04 — Water and the Subak

> Irrigation societies are independent of village government, organized entirely around the shared use of water and agricultural ritual.

The subak is an autonomous association of landowners and sharecroppers who share a common water source for their rice fields. This system is entirely independent of village or state political structures. Membership is determined solely by land ownership or farming rights within a specific irrigation section. Like other Balinese groups, the subak is centered on a set of temples located at dams and in the middle of fields. The group manages the complex engineering of canals and tunnels, synchronizes planting schedules, and performs necessary agricultural rituals. Because water is a shared and often scarce resource, the subak exercises strong authority over its members, using the threat of spiritual retribution or fines to ensure cooperation and fair distribution.
