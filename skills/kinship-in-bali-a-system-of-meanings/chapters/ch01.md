# ch01 — The Temple as Social Matrix

> Temples in Bali are not just places of worship but the centers of corporate social groups. Every adult belongs to multiple temple congregations that manage property and labor.

In Bali, religion is a public fact that permeates every social relationship. The temple system serves as the primary framework for society, with over twenty thousand public temples and hundreds of thousands of household shrines. Each temple is the focus of a specific, organized association called a congregation. These groups are responsible for the physical upkeep of the temple, the funding of ceremonies, and the provision of daily offerings. Membership is exclusive and based on specific grounds, such as residence, kinship, or shared work. A key feature of these groups is collective responsibility; the entire congregation is held accountable by the gods for the ritual accuracy of its members. This creates a high degree of social pressure for conformity within the group.
