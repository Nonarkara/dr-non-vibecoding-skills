# ch06 — The Theater State

> Traditional Balinese kingdoms were built on personal ties and ritual display rather than territorial control or administrative bureaucracy.

The traditional Balinese state was a complex network of personal allegiances between lords and subjects. These ties were not territorial; subjects of different lords often lived side-by-side in the same village. The primary function of the political system was not administration but the organization of massive ritual festivals and dramatic displays. A lord's power was measured by the number of subjects he could mobilize for these ceremonies. While lords had the right to call on subjects for war or labor, their influence over internal village affairs was strictly limited by customary law and the autonomy of the hamlet councils. This 'theater state' used ritual to symbolize and validate the social order rather than to enforce centralized political control.
