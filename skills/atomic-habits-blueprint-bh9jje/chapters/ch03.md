# ch03 — Identity‑Based Habits

> Shows how linking habits to self‑image creates lasting motivation and how to shift from outcome‑focused goals to identity‑focused actions.

## Identity‑Based Habits

Instead of saying, “I want to run a marathon,” adopt the identity, “I am a runner.” This subtle shift changes the habit’s purpose: actions become proof of who you are, not merely steps toward a distant goal. The brain seeks consistency; once a behavior aligns with self‑concept, it is reinforced automatically. To build identity‑based habits, start with a *tiny* action that a desired self would do daily—e.g., a 5‑minute stretch for a “flexible person.” Track the action as a badge of identity, not a metric of performance. Over time, the habit solidifies the self‑image, which in turn fuels more ambitious behaviors.

For an AI assistant, prompt users to articulate the person they wish to become, then suggest micro‑behaviors that embody that role. Reinforce each completed action with affirmations (“You acted like a writer today”) to tighten the identity loop. When the user’s self‑image evolves, the assistant updates the habit suggestions accordingly, ensuring continuous alignment between who they are and what they do.
