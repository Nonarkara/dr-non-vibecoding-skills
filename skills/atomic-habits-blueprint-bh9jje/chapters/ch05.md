# ch05 — Environment Design & Habit Stacking

> Explains how to shape physical and digital spaces to cue desired habits and how to attach new habits to existing routines.

## Environment Design & Habit Stacking

**Environment Design** – The surroundings dictate which cues are visible. To promote reading, place a book on the nightstand; to curb phone scrolling, keep the device in another room. Digital workspaces can be organized with folders that surface the next step of a project. The assistant can audit a user’s workspace, suggest relocations, and automate environment changes (e.g., switch to “focus mode” at 9 am).

**Habit Stacking** – Link a new habit to an established one using the formula: *After [existing habit], I will [new habit].* Example: “After I brew coffee, I will write one journal entry.” This leverages the existing cue‑response pattern, reducing the need for a new trigger. The assistant can scan a user’s calendar for recurring events and propose stackable actions that fit the time slot.

Together, environment design and stacking create a cascade of cues that keep the habit loop running with minimal effort. By continuously refining the environment and stacking opportunities, the AI maintains a self‑reinforcing system that scales as the user’s routine evolves.
