# ch02 — Customer Segments

> Defines how to identify and describe the groups of people or organizations a business aims to serve, including mass market, niche, segmented, diversified, and multi‑sided platforms.

Customer Segments are the distinct groups of people or organizations a business decides to target. A model can serve one or several segments, and each segment may have different needs, behaviors, and willingness to pay. Common segment types include mass market (one large group with similar needs), niche market (a specialized subgroup with specific requirements), segmented (different groups with slightly different needs), diversified (unrelated segments served by the same business), and multi‑sided platforms (two or more interdependent segments that need each other, such as readers and advertisers for a free newspaper). To define segments, ask: Who are our customers? What jobs are they trying to get done? What pains and gains do they experience? Segmentation helps tailor the Value Proposition, Channels, and Relationships to the specific characteristics of each group, improving fit and efficiency. Ignoring segment differences leads to a generic offering that fails to resonate. The canvas encourages you to list each segment separately and consider how the rest of the model serves them.
