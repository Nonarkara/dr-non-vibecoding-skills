# ch05 — Customer Relationships

> Details the types of relationships a business can establish with each segment—from personal assistance to automated services—and how they support acquisition, retention, and upselling.

Customer Relationships describe the kind of interaction a company establishes with each Customer Segment to acquire, retain, and increase sales. Relationships range from personal to automated and can coexist for the same segment. Categories include personal assistance (human interaction during sales or after), dedicated personal assistance (a specific representative assigned to an individual client), self‑service (providing tools for customers to help themselves), automated services (mixing self‑service with automated processes that recognize individual traits), communities (online forums where users help each other and the company learns), and co‑creation (inviting customers to shape products or services). Relationships are driven by motivations such as acquisition, retention, and upselling (boosting sales through upgrades or add‑ons). To define them, ask: What type of relationship does each segment expect us to establish? Which have we established? How costly are they? How are they integrated with the rest of the model? Choosing the right relationship affects customer satisfaction, lifetime value, and cost. For example, a self‑service model reduces cost but may hinder acquisition for complex products, while dedicated personal assistance increases cost but can drive high‑value sales.
