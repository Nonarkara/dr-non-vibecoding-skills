# ch04 — Channels

> Describes how a company communicates with, reaches, and delivers value to its Customer Segments, covering the five phases (awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery, after sales) and the choice between owned, partner, and mixed channels.

Channels are the touchpoints a company uses to interact with customers throughout the relationship lifecycle. They serve five functions: raising awareness, helping evaluation, enabling purchase, delivering the Value Proposition, and providing after‑sales support. Each channel can cover some or all of these phases. Channels are classified as owned (direct, such as an in‑house sales force or a website; indirect, such as retail stores you own) or partner (indirect, such as wholesale distribution, retail partners, or partner‑owned websites). Owned channels typically yield higher margins but require greater investment; partner channels lower margins but extend reach and leverage partner strengths. The challenge is to find the right mix that matches how customers want to be reached, is cost‑efficient, and integrates smoothly with their routines. Questions to ask include: Through which channels do our segments want to be reached? How are we reaching them now? Which channels work best? Which are most cost‑effective? How do we integrate them with customer habits? Answering these helps design a channel strategy that supports the Value Proposition while controlling costs and delivering a good customer experience.
