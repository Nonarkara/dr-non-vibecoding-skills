# ch07 — Key Resources

> Identifies the most important assets—physical, financial, intellectual, or human—required to make the business model work, and shows how they support the other blocks.

Key Resources are the essential assets needed to create and offer a Value Proposition, reach markets, maintain Customer Relationships, and earn revenues. They fall into four categories: physical (manufacturing facilities, buildings, vehicles, machines, point‑of‑sale systems, distribution networks), financial (cash, lines of credit, stock option pools), intellectual (brands, proprietary knowledge, patents, copyrights, customer databases, partnerships), and human (people, especially in knowledge‑intensive or creative industries). Resources can be owned, leased, or acquired from key partners. The required resources depend on the model type: a microchip manufacturer needs capital‑intensive factories, while a microchip designer relies more on human expertise and intellectual property. To identify them, ask: What Key Resources do our Value Propositions require? Our Distribution Channels? Customer Relationships? Revenue Streams? Answering this highlights where the business must invest or partner. For example, a low‑cost airline’s key resources are efficient aircraft and airport slots, whereas a premium consultancy’s key resources are experienced consultants and proprietary methodologies.
