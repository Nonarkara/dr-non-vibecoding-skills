## The work this came from — the core components

This stack did not start as a stack. It started as a 100-day writing practice. The 127 posts published between 2015 and 2025 on [nonharvard.wordpress.com](https://nonharvard.wordpress.com) (1.6 MB / 258,389 words) are the *source code* of the work. The skills in this repo are the *distillation* — the load-bearing moves extracted from a decade of daily reflection.

The core components, before they became the system:

### 1. Writing as a thinking tool (Days 1-10)

> "I don't always think of myself as being right even when I have adequate evidence that I may be right."
> — Day 58, *On the Experience of Writing about Morality*

The practice began with the observation that writing is not documentation; writing *is* the thinking. The gap between the idea in the head and the fingers on the keyboard is where the test happens. The 5-minute reflection at the end of every session, the single vivid user story as the kickoff, the writing-as-low-fi-prototype — all load-bearing skills in this stack come from this.

### 2. The tradition audit (Day 5)

> "Treat tradition as a hypothesis, not a rule."

Before adopting any "best practice" — in design, in code, in business — the operator asks: *which current need does this satisfy?* If the answer is weak, the practice is operating as a rule, not a hypothesis. The `qualitative-reasoning` and `ninja-innovation` skills carry this discipline.

### 3. Non-violent communication as the floor (Day 94, *On Making an Argument*)

> "Have you ever found yourself in the middle of an argument listening to your opposition, and you have a strong feeling that something is very wrong with the point that they're making, but you can't pinpoint exactly what it is?"

The 8 rules of a good argument (Nonviolent Communication floor, steelman first, "I" + "because" + source, Keynes' "change my mind when the facts change") and the 6 named fallacies (ad hominem, slippery slope, appeal to consequences, appeal to ignorance, status quo, straw man / false equivalence / bandwagon) are the `argument-construction` skill in this stack. The skill was extracted from this chapter.

### 4. The falliblist stance (Days 12, 42, 58)

The discipline of holding a position as a *position*, not as a *self*. The source of `karpathy-guidelines` §1a *Reflexivity* and `moral-reasoning` Move 2. The author's exact words: *"I am a falliblist, so I don't always think of myself as being right even when I have adequate evidence that I may be right."*

### 5. Economy of words (Day 57)

> "I want to speak less, write less, and mean more."

The aphorism craft — *precise, concise, poetic*. The discipline that became `aesthetics-and-economy`, `caveman` (output compression), and the `axiom-design-core` design law. The Thai-derived discipline of *Kodawari* — perfection within constraint — is the deeper substrate.

### 6. The "weirder than we think" rule (Day 14)

> "We're weirder than we think."

The assumption that a behavior, norm, or value is *universal* is a claim that should be tested by the counter-example. The discipline became `design-anthropology` (where the counter-examples come from), `qualitative-reasoning` Move 6 (the counter-example as a reasoning move), and the biopower check (is the norm state-driven or product-driven?).

### 7. The Thinker → Doer system (the spine)

The two layers of the operator:
- **The Thinker** — the lived domain truth, the human empathy, the systemic taste. The thing the AI cannot generate.
- **The Doer** — the agent, the skills, the harness. The thing the AI can amplify.

The 100-day writing practice is *The Thinker* on the page. This stack is *The Doer* off the page. They are the same system, viewed from two sides.

## The research — three arcs

The 127 posts are organized into three intellectual epochs, each contributing a layer to the practice.

### Arc I: The Solitary Ethnographer (Days 1-75, 2015-2018)

Written from a Shanghai alleyway room, biking the Huangpu riverbanks, finishing the Harvard/Oxford PhD. The epistemic grounding: writing as thinking, the floor of the tunnel, Rousseauian authenticity, Stoic equanimity, Sartrean refusal of bad faith. The critique of social mimicry and insecurity. The Tradition Audit. The I-should-vs-I-have-to test.

The skills that came from this arc: `design-by-writing-100-day-reflection-engine`, `karpathy-guidelines` §1a, `moral-reasoning`, `argument-construction`, `qualitative-reasoning`.

### Arc II: The Design Thinker & Anti-Fragile Synthesis (Days 76-117, 2018-2021)

Written as an IDEO/top-firm design consultant and civic strategist. The 11-part Design Thinking canon (integrating Kahneman, Taleb, the Four Noble Truths, Gawande's *Checklist Manifesto*). The Minimalism & Economy of Words chapters — the 16-things-I-never-have, the light-and-cheap discipline, the Kodawari of perfection within constraint.

The skills that came from this arc: `aesthetics-and-economy`, `systems-thinking`, `axiom-design-core`, the playbook line (the *engineering-for-distribution*, *multi-agent-and-worktrees*, *ship-to-production-daily* playbooks).

### Arc III: The Technologist & Speculative Urbanist (Days 118-126, 2022-2025)

Written from depa and Bangkok, deploying smart-city sensor grids, reflecting on AI and simulation theory. Flow & Mastery (the 5,000-hour threshold). The Digital Twin and the simulation hypothesis. The civic stack as a living system where data feeds, human dignity, and policy intersect.

The skills that came from this arc: `power-of-hindsight` (the data-driven retrospective), `restore-drill` (a dated restore, not a green backup), `synthetic-data-floor`, `local-ai-fabric` and the local-first inference path, the philosophical spine (playbook 16).

## How this stack came to exist

The transformation from blog → skills → stack happened in 5 stages over 2024-2026.

**Stage 1 — The blog (2015-2025).** 127 posts. 1.6 MB. 258K words. The corpus. The author wrote daily for 100 days, then kept going. The corpus was the *private* practice.

**Stage 2 — The first skill (2025-07).** A single skill — `design-by-writing-100-day-reflection-engine` — was distilled from the blog. The 5 core moves of writing-as-design. The first packaging of a daily practice as a *reusable instruction* for an agent.

**Stage 3 — The nonism bundle (2026-09).** Four skills distilled from the corpus in one sitting, via an LLM call against a JSON-shape prompt. The bundle was the first attempt at *systematic* distillation. Of the four, only one (`design-by-writing-100-day-reflection-engine`) was strong enough to ship; the other three were personal-philosophy cheatsheet cards that were stripped or rejected. The lesson: *the discipline travels, the implementation is one of many*.

**Stage 4 — The stack (2026-09).** A new agent (Mavis) joined the work. The skill-curation discipline was formalized: sample 2-4 chapters per theme, extract paths not content, strip personal philosophy, cap at 3 skills per round. The 100-skill milestone was crossed in three rounds of corpus extraction. The 16 playbooks were written to capture the *why* and the *war stories* behind the skills. The 11 references and 13 templates were assembled as the *operational surface* for the stack.

**Stage 5 — The philosophical spine (2026-09).** The 100-skill stack was given a *spine* — the 5 layers (Moral & Judgment Floor, Human-Centered Subject, Aesthetic & Economic Discipline, Systemic & Long-Term Invariants, Epistemic Humility & Truth Gates), the 5 moves for the agent, the 8 rules of cyber-hygiene, the 6 rules of off-grid operation. The spine is the contract: the *why* of every skill, in one document.

The work is not finished. The journal keeps going.

## The journal — the flight recorder

Every meaningful change to this stack is recorded in [`JOURNAL.md`](JOURNAL.md) — the flight recorder. The journal is maintained by the agent (Mavis) on the user's behalf. It is the *narrative* of the project, not the *log*: a new builder can read the journal to understand what was built, what was added, what was discussed, and *why*. The journal is a black box that is always on.

The journal is the *what* of the work. The about is the *who* and the *why*. Together, the two are the iPhone-unboxing experience: open the box, see the device, see the history, turn it on.

## How to engage

- **Use the stack.** `./setup.sh --become-builder` is the one-liner. The agent does the rest. If the first user is confused, the journal and the war-stories playbook (`playbooks/06-war-stories.md`) are the recovery path.
- **Read the corpus.** The 127 posts at [nonharvard.wordpress.com](https://nonharvard.wordpress.com) are the source. The skills in this stack are the distillation. The two are not equivalent; the corpus has the *texture*, the stack has the *shape*.
- **Suggest changes.** Open an issue. The journal records the discussion. The PR adds the change. The 16 playbooks document the reasoning.
- **Contribute skills.** New skills are added through the same distillation discipline — sample 2-4 sources, extract paths, strip personal content, cap at 3 per round. The `skill-writing` skill is the procedure.
- **Follow along.** The journal is the timeline. The author writes at the Axiom X studio in Bangkok. The civic work continues.
